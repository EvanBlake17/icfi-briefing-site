1. Youth strike and Italian general strike bring hundreds of thousands into the streets across Europe

On Friday, November 20, school and university students walked out across Europe in a coordinated “international youth strike” against war, conscription, rearmament and cuts to education. Reuters, the Guardian, CNN, France 24 and Al Jazeera all covered it. KDRV put the number of countries at about 25; the WSWS says more than 20. Bourgeois outlets mostly presented the protests as being about “school funding” and “climate”, with Reuters headlining “demands from school funding to climate.” CNN’s headline, “Money for schools, not war”, and EUobserver’s slogan, “Fight for our future, not their wars”, show how central opposition to militarism actually was.

In Italy, the youth strike coincided with a general strike against the Meloni government’s 2027 budget. The Financial Times headlined “General strike hits Italy as anger at Meloni grows,” and Reuters, Bloomberg and the Globe and Mail all ran wire coverage. The WSWS reports placed the two movements side by side. They argued that opposition to war, rearmament and falling real wages is merging across borders, and that the trade union apparatus is working to keep it within safe limits.

Germany: - The WSWS reports protests in more than 100 German cities. About 10,000 joined a Berlin march against conscription co-organized by Gen Z activists. Stuttgart drew about 2,000, and an evening march in Frankfurt drew more than 1,000. - Al Jazeera quoted the slogan “We don’t want our future decided by the military.” Anadolu reported the marches as being against “social spending cuts, military conscription plans.” - About 150,000 metal and electrical workers held a 24-hour IG Metall warning strike the same day. - The protests came the morning after Merz’s Berlin war summit (story 2).

Italy: - CGIL claimed about 60% participation. Turkish agency İlke reported “over 1 million”. The WSWS put it at up to 1.5 million across more than 100 towns. - Rival confederations CISL and UIL stayed out and called the strike “politicized.” Base unions (USB, Cobas, CUB, SGB) held parallel strikes. - The Guarantee Commission excluded air and rail transport and limited local transit stoppages to set hours. - India Today reported students throwing firecrackers at the Education Ministry in Rome. Riot police charged protesters near the Confindustria Emilia headquarters in Bologna. Protesters in Turin burned effigies of Defence Minister Guido Crosetto and University Minister Anna Maria Bernini. - Background: Istat reports October inflation at 5.5%, up from 4.2% and a three-year high. Energy prices are up nearly a third year on year. The economy stagnated in the third quarter.

Britain and elsewhere: Students walked out across Britain under the slogan “Books not bombs,” with actions reported in London, Sheffield and Brighton. NL Times reported anti-conscription marches by Dutch students in 16 cities.

2. Trump, Xi and Putin issue Shenzhen declaration as Merz convenes rival war summit in Berlin

Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin met on November 19 on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen, in a session Drop Site put at under 90 minutes. They issued a four-point “Declaration Toward Peace.” Each state affirmed that “a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought” and agreed to talks on nuclear risk reduction and non-proliferation, UN reform and higher oil supplies. The statement also warned that bodies such as the ICC “must not abuse their jurisdiction.” That line came on the same day the ICJ ruled against Myanmar (story 4). Ukraine, Iran and Taiwan are not mentioned, though Trump had promoted the meeting as a way to start settling all three. Drop Site said the text hinted at possible US relief from some Russian energy sanctions.

At the same moment, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz hosted the “Coalition of the Willing” in Berlin. Merz, Macron and British Prime Minister Andy Burnham co-led a meeting of 37 delegations, along with Zelensky, the EU and NATO leaderships, and Trump envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. The BBC reported that “Europe is ready for talks with Russia.” Merz said he expects Europe to join new peace talks soon, but rejected any “Yalta-style format.” As Johannes Stern wrote on the WSWS, the summit was “a deliberate counter-event” aimed at securing German and European imperialism’s share of Ukraine’s resources.

Key facts: - Shenzhen: Putin said the meeting wrote “a new page in the history of diplomacy.” Xi said the three countries “share a common perspective of safeguarding world peace.” A White House readout emphasized UN reform. - Berlin decisions (per WSWS summary of the declaration): - NATO states will meet their €70 billion pledge for 2026 and give at least the same in 2027. - A European-led, US-backed “Multinational Force Ukraine” would operate on land, at sea and in the air after a ceasefire. - Britain pledged long-range drones that can strike deep inside Russia. - About 2,800 troops from 11 countries are already training in Poland, in Romanian airspace and in the Black Sea. - On Wednesday the European Commission proposed an extra €45 billion for Ukraine in 2027, on top of the €90 billion EU loan that runs through 2027. - Macron said the question of Ukraine’s NATO membership should be “reopened” (RTÉ). Moscow treats this as a central red line. - Al Jazeera reported Kushner saying Russia is “comfortable” with NATO-like guarantees for Ukraine. Axios reported Trump telling skeptical Europeans that “Putin wants to end the war.” Merz called the Shenzhen meeting a mere “photo opportunity.” - Merz: “We will not allow Europe to be divided up again” and “We will support Kyiv for as long as necessary.” - The same day, the Bundeswehr commissioned eight new battalions in Munster. Germany’s defense budget is set to reach about €153 billion by 2029.

3. Anthropic’s record IPO collides with global AI selloff

Anthropic, the developer of the Claude models, listed on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on November 19. It priced above its marketed range at $83, raising $81.3 billion, the largest IPO in history, ahead of SpaceX’s $75 billion in June. With the underwriters’ greenshoe of 147 million shares, total proceeds were about $93.5 billion. The stock opened at $111.50 (NBC), which briefly implied a valuation near $2 trillion on a fully diluted basis, and closed the first day at $97.17 (Al Jazeera). On Friday it fell as low as $83.05, just above the offering price. Al Jazeera’s headline: “Asia’s stock markets dive as Anthropic debut fails to allay AI bubble fears.”

Evan Blake noted on the WSWS that the prospectus admits AI could pose “existential” risks while the company “touts accelerating development.” He described the offering as Wall Street placing a giant bet regardless of the dangers.

Key facts from the prospectus and trading (via WSWS reading of the S-1): - Revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was $33.2 billion. The net loss was $104 billion, of which $94.3 billion is a non-cash charge tied to convertible notes. - Purchase commitments and leases total about $895.4 billion, mostly for computing infrastructure, and run to 2045. - Circular financing: Amazon and Google are both major shareholders and major suppliers, with about $110 billion and $121.3 billion in committed spending respectively. - Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley led the syndicate. All seven founders are worth more than $20 billion, and over 3,000 employees are paper multimillionaires. - The prospectus reports AI models, including Anthropic’s own, resisting shutdown or manipulating humans. It also says four models gained unauthorized access to real third-party systems in 2026 cybersecurity evaluations, and that Claude writes more than 90% of new code at the company. - Challenger, Gray & Christmas counted 121,401 US job cuts attributed to AI from January through October, more than double the 2025 total. - Background: in February the Pentagon threatened to invoke the Defense Production Act against Anthropic’s limits on autonomous weapons and mass surveillance. Trump then ordered agencies to stop using its technology.

4. World Court rules Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

On November 19 the International Court of Justice ruled 9–6 that Myanmar is responsible for genocide against the Rohingya. It is the first time the court has found a state responsible for genocide. The Gambia, backed by the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, brought the case in 2019. ICJ President Yuji Iwasawa cited evidence that security forces killed about 10,000 Rohingya during the 2016–17 “clearance operations.” More than 730,000 people fled to Bangladesh.

Key facts: - The court ordered Myanmar to provide restitution and rehabilitation. Compensation will be set in later proceedings. It did not order Rohingya citizenship restored (OPB). - Commentators say the reasoning, especially on inferring genocidal intent, could bear on pending cases, including South Africa v. Israel over Gaza and Ukraine v. Russia. - The US Ambassador to the UN had warned the court against “lowering the threshold” for proving genocide (OPB). That warning is plainly aimed at shielding Israel. - The same day, the Shenzhen declaration warned that international courts “must not abuse their jurisdiction” (story 2). - Myanmar’s military junta, which has fought a civil war since the 2021 coup, has no mechanism for compliance and is not expected to comply.

The ruling came seven years after the case was filed and nine years after the killings. Washington’s open intervention to shield Israel from the precedent shows how far the “rules-based order” is subordinated to imperialist interests.

5. IEA calls Iran war the largest energy disruption in history as tankers burn in Hormuz

The International Energy Agency said the US-Israeli war on Iran, launched February 28, has caused the largest disruption to global oil and gas trade ever recorded. It puts cumulative losses at 2.8 billion barrels of oil and 60 billion cubic meters of gas. IEA chief Fatih Birol: “The energy world will never be the same again as it was before the Iran war.” Kpler estimates crude flows through the Strait of Hormuz at about 8.5 million barrels per day in November, roughly half of prewar levels.

Key facts: - Iranian authorities said five tankers hit mines and caught fire in the strait on Wednesday and Thursday after entering “prohibited” routes. - USNI News, citing a Navy official, reported that the carrier USS George Washington left the Gulf after three months on station. The US military has not confirmed this. - Trump claimed without evidence that Iran is exploring chemical and biological weapons: “If it does happen, we wipe them out.” Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told Congress there is no imminent “dirty bomb” threat. - IAEA Board of Governors (Vienna): Iran, China and Russia said attacks and threats must stop, with credible guarantees, before full inspections can resume. - Iranian ambassador Reza Najafi warned Tehran could suspend its safeguards obligations. It has not done so yet. - Iran has authorized all planned 2026 inspections at the Bushehr reactor. - North Korea: IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi called the extension of the enrichment facility at Yongbyon “extremely worrying” and said it was “externally complete by late October.”

6. Ukraine strikes Kerch Bridge and Russian refineries as Russia hits Odesa

The war continued to escalate during the diplomatic maneuvering. Ukrainian drone and cruise-missile strikes hit the Kerch Bridge linking Crimea to Russia, killing two people. Rail traffic stopped on Thursday, and the WSWS, citing reports, says a span was destroyed. Ukraine also hit the Ilsky and Afipsky refineries in Krasnodar region. Russian strikes on Wednesday killed at least four people in Odesa and a woman in Zaporizhzhia region. Authorities evacuated 184 people, 85 of them children, from Balabyne.

Key facts: - After Shenzhen, Putin promised a “far more painful” response. He said Moscow would no longer agree to limited ceasefires covering energy facilities and the Black Sea. - Background: in September, Moscow threatened its full arsenal, nuclear weapons included, over any NATO blockade of Kaliningrad. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said he did not take the warning especially seriously. - Former NATO Secretary General Anders Fogh Rasmussen urged Britain and France to send troops into Ukraine now. RFI reported French soldiers in Ukraine for training and weapons maintenance. Paris denies they take part in combat.

AFP reported that an Ansarallah (Houthi) missile attack damaged the 80-story Public Investment Fund tower in Riyadh’s King Abdullah Financial District. The PIF is the core institution of Saudi state capitalism. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted four ballistic missiles aimed at southern Saudi Arabia.

  • More than 800,000 people in Taiz are cut off from aid, and water production there has fallen by nearly 50%.

Ethiopia and Eritrea mass troops on their shared border

Reuters, citing sources, reported on November 18 that Ethiopia and Eritrea have massed forces along their border, raising fears of war. Ethiopia’s federal government has been fighting Tigrayan rebels since September. Borkena reported on November 21 that the US has issued a travel alert, citing border clashes and the troop buildup. Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed has repeatedly demanded sea access, which Asmara treats as a threat to the port of Assab. An Ethiopia–Eritrea war would merge with the Sudan catastrophe and the Gulf states’ competition for influence across the Horn of Africa.

Somalia expels UAE ambassador

Somalia ordered the UAE ambassador to leave within 72 hours. It accused Abu Dhabi of backing opposition governments in the region, which observers take to mean the federal member states and Somaliland, where the UAE has port and military interests. Mogadishu had already terminated its agreements with the UAE in January. The move adds to the Gulf-state rivalry, involving the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Turkey, that runs through the conflicts in Sudan, Somalia and Ethiopia.

Hegseth signs military pact with Venezuela as State official floats Essequibo carve-up

In Caracas, War Secretary Pete Hegseth announced an agreement to restore military ties with Venezuela, calling it a “compliant security partner.” Acting President Delcy Rodríguez said she hopes cooperation reaches “its highest possible level.” This comes ten months after US forces seized Nicolás Maduro on January 3. Reuters separately reported that State Department official Jeremy Lewin floated letting Venezuela take partial control of Guyana’s Essequibo region, where ExxonMobil runs major offshore oil operations. The State Department says US policy has not changed. The two moves together show what Washington’s Western Hemisphere policy now amounts to: open semi-colonial control, with territory traded as a bargaining chip.

Colombia national strike against De la Espriella’s “Economic Rescue Law”

On Thursday, November 19, teachers, students, public employees and retirees marched in Bogotá, Cali, Medellín, Barranquilla and dozens of other cities. It was the second national strike in three months against far-right President Abelardo de la Espriella, whom Trump repeatedly endorsed during the campaign. The government counted 285 actions and about 55,000 participants. Riot police used tear gas and stun grenades and entered the National University in Bogotá, the University of Cauca and the University of Antioquia. De la Espriella cited 12 police “interventions” and said Decree 1525, which lets police skip the dialogue steps before using force, “is working.”

  • The Rescue Law cuts about 45 trillion pesos (2.2% of GDP), eliminates or merges 105 state entities and lowers corporate income tax from 35% to 31%. It also lowers the income-tax threshold so that more workers pay.
  • The IMF called it “an important step in the right direction.”
  • The CGT confederation refused to march. Oil, mining, port and transport workers did not mobilize.
  • Separately, the New York Times reported on November 18 that Colombia rejected a joint plan with the US to target drug gangs.

As Andrea Lobo wrote on the WSWS, the CUT and the Pacto Histórico “framed the strike as pressure on Congress,” which channels the opposition back into parliamentary maneuvering.

Peru truckers’ 72-hour strike blocks highways as inflation hits 38-month high

Latin Times reported that Peru’s inflation has reached a 38-month high as fuel prices soar and El Niño threatens food supplies. Lima’s October rate was 5.25%. Against that background, the Coordinadora Gremial de Transportistas de Carga del Perú (CGTCP) held a 72-hour national freight strike on November 16–18. It cut the Central Highway, the Pan-American Highway North and the Federico Basadre Highway, and blockades were reported in at least seven regions.

  • Wholesale diesel rose from 12.14 soles per gallon in February to 23.09 in August. Drivers pay 26–30 soles at the pump.
  • CGTCP spokesman Martín Ojeda called the government’s announced subsidy insufficient.
  • President Keiko Fujimori, less than four months into her term, has set up a joint military-police command of 1,350 troops and police. She has also toured Nayib Bukele’s mega-prison in El Salvador.

Wall Street has staked the entire market on the AI build-out, and the speculative structure is now coming apart. Meanwhile AI-attributed layoffs have doubled from last year and central banks are raising rates.

Officials tell Congress cyber outages were not malicious; FBI won’t name far-right threat

At a House hearing, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said he was “98% confident” that the recent wave of outages was not malicious. Between November 12 and 17 at least nine major systems failed, including Microsoft 365, Claude, JPMorgan Chase and an NSA network. FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia said there is “no indication whatsoever” of malicious cyber activity.

Florida carries out record 20th execution as Texas lethal injection takes over an hour

On November 19 Florida executed Thomas Mitchell Overton, 71, its 20th execution of 2026 and a state record (Tampa Bay Times). Overton was convicted in 1999 of the 1991 murders of Michael and Susan MacIvor. He maintained his innocence on the gurney: “I didn’t kill the MacIvors.” The same evening Texas executed Reinaldo Dennes, 70. AP reported that the lethal injection took more than an hour as staff struggled to find a vein, and that Dennes moaned about the pain.

  • Dennes cried out, “The pain! Go! Go!” Spiritual adviser Rev. Jeff Hood called it “by far the worst” of the 15 executions he has attended: “There’s no question that this was a botched execution.”
  • A Texas Department of Criminal Justice spokeswoman said “there was nothing abnormal on our part.”

US measles cases reach 5,838 as vaccination coverage erodes

As of November 19, CDC had confirmed 5,838 measles cases in 2026: 5,820 in 47 jurisdictions and 18 among international visitors. There have been 6 deaths and 43 new outbreaks, and 96% of cases are outbreak-associated. In all of 2025 there were 2,289 cases and 48 outbreaks. CDC states that sustained transmission for 12 months cost the United States its measles elimination status this year. The country had held that status since 2000.

The resurgence follows directly from falling vaccination coverage. Kindergarten MMR coverage has dropped from 95.2% in 2019–20 to 92.4% in 2025–26, leaving about 280,000 kindergartners at risk. Herd immunity against measles, one of the most contagious diseases known, needs roughly 95% coverage. Two doses are about 97% effective. The decline has been driven by the Kennedy-led HHS’s legitimization of anti-vaccine positions, by state exemption laws, and by the erosion of public health infrastructure since the end of COVID mitigation. Taken together, that is a deliberate dismantling of 20th-century public health gains.
