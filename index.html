1. Democratic Senate majority climbs toward 56 as Trump says he will “veto” the new Congress

The scale of Tuesday’s Republican defeat grew as counting continued. NBC News projects Democrats at about 255 House seats; the WSWS, using AP calls, puts them at 259 to 176 with several dozen races still open. That would be the largest Democratic majority since 1990. NBC also projects a Democratic Senate. Patrick Martin reports a 54–44 Democratic majority with two Republican-held seats still undecided, and Democrats lead narrowly in both: - Mississippi: Scott Colom’s lead over Cindy Hyde-Smith has grown from 65 votes to more than 1,000, with thousands of provisional ballots still to count. - Alaska: Mary Peltola leads Dan Sullivan by about 600 votes in a four-way race. Ranked-choice tabulation will not start until the last mail ballots are in, probably late this month.

A 56-seat majority would be the biggest either party has held since 2010. The new Congress convenes January 3.

Trump told Fox News he was “a little bit surprised” by the result. He told the New York Times he was “absolutely willing to accept those results.” He said he would work with Democrats but would veto what they pass. He blamed weak Republican candidates and high gas prices, which come from his own war on Iran, and insisted he was not on the ballot, even though he had told rallies in 20 states to “pretend I’m on the ballot.” In Moscow, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov called the shift “an internal matter for America” and said: “We sincerely hope that he will also continue his peacemaking efforts.” In today’s WSWS Perspective, Joseph Kishore and David North call Trump “a symptom, not the cause,” and reject the New York Times editorial board’s verdict that “the system has held.”

Key facts: - Popular vote: Democrats won the national House vote by about 13 points, the widest margin for either party in half a century (WSWS). Pre-election polls had put the generic-ballot lead at nine points. - Exit polls, reported by the WSWS: - About 60 percent said attacking Iran was the wrong decision, and 74 percent called the war a crucial or major factor in their vote. - Between 80 and 90 percent rated the economy poor, and two-thirds blamed Trump’s policies. - Trump’s approval stood at 38 percent. Of voters, 43 percent said they voted to oppose him and 16 percent to support him. - Notable House flips (NBC): - Pennsylvania: Janelle Stelson ousted Scott Perry (PA-10), Paige Cognetti beat Rob Bresnahan (PA-8), Bob Brooks beat Ryan Mackenzie (PA-7), and Bob Harvie beat Brian Fitzpatrick. - North Carolina: Richard Ojeda II upset Richard Hudson, who chairs the House GOP’s campaign arm. - Virginia: Elaine Luria retook her old seat from Jen Kiggans. - Where the swing came from: Small cities and rural areas swung more than 15 points against Trump. Some Rio Grande Valley counties swung more than 50 points among Hispanic voters. Trump lost every age group (WSWS). - DeSantis: He called the result “close to an extinction-level event” for Republicans in many areas.

2. Hormuz shipping war kills an Indian seafarer as Iran says more tankers are burning

Merchant shipping is bearing the cost of the stalled war around the Strait of Hormuz. The Indian-crewed dhow MSV Al Jusufi was struck near the strait on Tuesday. One Indian crew member died, and Oman’s coast guard rescued the other 13. The same day, two projectiles hit another tanker off Oman and damaged its engine room, with no injuries reported. On Thursday Iran’s Revolutionary Guard (IRGC) said two oil tankers and a tugboat had caught fire in the strait after using an “unauthorised route.” The IRGC also said its electronic-warfare units had captured two US drones intact. Earlier this week Tehran said 11 tankers had caught fire in nine days. Washington disputes that figure.

The war is also squeezing supply. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre (UKMTO) recorded 30 attacks on commercial vessels in October, the most of any month. Gulf crude exports averaged 17.1 million barrels a day in October, down from 18.9 million in September. Iranian officials are warning their own population of winter gas shortages and asking households to conserve. Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei said the US midterm result would not end Washington’s hostility toward Iran.

Key facts: - Earlier incidents: A Qatari LNG carrier was attacked on October 31. On November 1 a vessel was hit by an unknown projectile about nine nautical miles off Lima, Oman. - Trump on November 1: He said the US still had to “take care of” one or two targets in Iran, including a suspected nuclear site at Pickaxe Mountain. He also said the war would end “very soon, one way or the other.” - UAE: Presidential adviser Anwar Gargash said the Emirates are braced for “the worst.” - Russia: Mohsen Rezaei, of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, thanked Putin for Russian support in a phone call.

3. Trump says US aid to Israel will end as Qatar awaits Iranian and US replies on an interim deal

Trump told Fox News that US military aid to Israel, about $4 billion a year, will end by the close of 2026, and that Netanyahu had agreed. CBS’s headline and body text disagree on the date, giving 2027 in one place and the end of this year in the other. No legislative mechanism has been announced, and the annual figure is set by a 10-year memorandum of understanding. The remark came the same week the AP and Axios reported that Netanyahu was pressing Trump for a renewed and wider air campaign against Iran once the midterms were over.

Qatar, which is mediating together with Pakistan, said it expects “feedback” from both Washington and Tehran within 36 hours and is “hopeful” about an interim deal. Iran’s seven conditions include an end to fighting on all fronts, Lebanon and Gaza among them, the lifting of the US naval blockade of Iranian ports and the release of frozen assets. Mohsen Rezaei repeated that all seven must be met before the strait reopens. Earlier in the week Tehran offered to shorten the implementation period of any deal from seven days to two.

Key facts: - Tehran: Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei said Iran approaches diplomacy with “complete distrust.” Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said Iran “retains the right to legitimate self-defense.” The IRGC said Iran’s leadership will decide “the time and conditions for ending the enemy’s attacks.” - Washington: Trump wrote that Iran “wants a settlement, as soon as possible.” Vice President Vance reportedly wrote directly to Rezaei. - Egypt: Its foreign minister urged Iran to show “flexibility.” - WSWS: Ulaş Sevinç argues that the war on Iran, Israel’s genocide in Gaza, the Saudi war in Yemen and NATO’s war against Russia are “interconnected fronts” in a struggle to redivide the world. On that reading, a cut in aid would be a shift in method, not a turn toward peace.

4. Herzog opens coalition talks as Arab parties’ record 19 seats put Eisenkot within reach of 61

President Isaac Herzog accepted the certified results of Israel’s October 27 election on Wednesday. He began consultations with the factions, which continue today, on whom to task with forming a government. “The elections are over. Now is the time to face reality,” he said. Netanyahu’s bloc won 53 of the Knesset’s 120 seats and Gadi Eisenkot’s bloc 48, against the 61 needed to govern. The Arab-led parties won a record 19 seats between them: 11 for the Joint List and 8 for Ra’am.

Ra’am will recommend Eisenkot, which Ynet calculates lifts his support to about 56. Channel 12 reports that the Joint List may either back Eisenkot or abstain, and a Joint List endorsement could take him past 61. Three Netanyahu-aligned factions recommended Netanyahu. A fourth, led by a former Netanyahu ally, backed Eisenkot. Degel HaTorah, a faction of United Torah Judaism, will recommend neither, after breaking with Netanyahu over legislation on Haredi exemptions from military service.

Key facts: - “Stolen election” claims: Netanyahu and his allies keep claiming, without evidence, that the vote was “stolen,” possibly with foreign help. Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara refused a request to question elected officials without supporting evidence. - Herzog: “I condemn the coarseness and racist expressions that we have heard recently.” The remark refers to incitement against Arab parties as possible kingmakers. - Timetable: The nominee has 28 days to form a government, plus a possible 14-day extension. If no one succeeds, Israel holds a new election early next year. - Context: Eisenkot is a former chief of staff of the Israeli military and a member of the war cabinet during the Gaza genocide. A change of government would not change the class character of the state or its war aims.

5. France stages national strike under a 70,000-strong police deployment

France’s inter-union coalition led today’s national strike and demonstrations over living costs, public services and education. The CGT called the action, student groups joined, and public-sector workers were called out. The unions expected 500,000 to 700,000 people outside Paris and 40,000 to 80,000 in the capital. Domestic intelligence gave the same figure for the provinces. About 70,000 police were deployed nationwide. A Paris court late Wednesday approved the police-proposed route from Place de la République to Place de la Nation, over the organisers’ objections. Macron convened a defence and security council on Wednesday. Government spokeswoman Maud Bregeon said: “We are extremely vigilant because it is out of the question to relive October 17.”

The strike comes as the state hardens its legal arsenal against protest. A bill already passed by the Senate would make anyone who “knowingly” joined a group that committed damage, or who stayed after two police orders to disperse, liable for up to €10,000 in damages, whether or not they personally caused any. It would allow the state to recover the money by seizing social benefits, including the minimum income (RSA), housing aid and low-income household assistance. Communist senator Fabien Gay calculated that a €57 monthly deduction would take 14 years to reach €10,000. Amnesty International called the bill a “deeply regressive step.” The Magistrates’ Union, the lawyers’ union SAF and the Human Rights League have taken a complaint to France’s highest court over an “authoritarian shift.” The bill now goes to the National Assembly.

Key facts: - October 17 riots: The Chamber of Commerce counts nearly 2,500 businesses damaged and more than 1,000 looted or burned. The free-market IFRAP foundation puts the cost near €1 billion. - CGT line: CGT leader Sophie Binet called on workers to “generalize” the strike but not to call a general strike (WSWS). - Coming dates: One-day national strikes are scheduled in Spain (November 11), Italy (November 20), Belgium (November 23) and Greece (November 25) (WSWS). - Media: A petition to ban a French right-wing television channel after a racist killing has passed 600,000 signatures (France 24).

6. Ebola deaths pass 5,300 as US bars entry to anyone, citizens included, recently in Congo

The Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak that began in the Democratic Republic of Congo in May has killed more than 5,300 people. The Africa CDC has confirmed 10,444 cases, which makes it the largest and fastest-growing outbreak in the country’s history. The Bundibugyo strain kills about half of those it infects, and there is no licensed vaccine against it. WHO officials report 50 to 80 new cases a day. About 80 percent of new cases cannot be linked to a known chain of transmission, a sign that contact tracing has broken down across eight provinces. Kenya has confirmed 9 cases and 2 deaths, and Nigeria has one confirmed case. Sequencing shows both countries’ index patients passed through Uganda’s Entebbe airport on October 3.

WHO expert groups endorsed two new vaccine trials on Monday, led by Congo’s National Institute for Biomedical Research. One candidate was developed by Oxford University, the Serum Institute of India and Moderna. Results are not expected before early 2027. A trial that began last month uses Ervebo, which is licensed only against the Zaire strain. Since November 3 the CDC has required every passenger boarding a US-bound international flight to attest that they have not been in the DRC in the previous 21 days. The rule bars US citizens and nationals as well and runs to December 18.

Key facts: - Tedros: He said travel bans “punish countries for doing the right thing” and warned that vaccines “are not substitutes for the proven measures that can save lives now.” - Africa CDC: Director-General Jean Kaseya warned the virus could reach the UK and Europe. “It is the first time three African regions have been affected,” he said. - UK: An infected British health worker is being evacuated from the DRC and is due to arrive today. - CDC: The agency rates the risk to Americans as low and has about 500 staff on the response, more than 120 of them deployed abroad.

Middle East

Turkey’s parliament weighs the Saudi-Pakistani “Mecca” mutual-defence pact as Syria signs up with Riyadh

Turkey’s parliament is due to vote on the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan. The pact is modelled on NATO’s Article 5: an armed attack on any one member counts as an attack on all three, and no member can withdraw for the first 10 years. Trump called it a “big, bold and important first step.” Turkish Deputy Foreign Minister Levent Gümrükçü described it as “complementary” to European security platforms, NATO above all (WSWS).

On Wednesday Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa signed a defence cooperation agreement in Riyadh, his second visit in less than a month. Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman oversaw the signing, and the terms were not disclosed. The Saudi Fund for Development committed more than $403 million in concessional loans: $100 million for health facilities, $105 million for civil defence, $90 million for the Aleppo thermal power plant, $60 million for housing repairs and $30 million for drinking water in Damascus and Idlib. Syrian Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani told the Manama Dialogue: “Discussions are currently under way to evaluate Syria’s entry into the Mecca alliance.” The two governments demanded an “immediate cessation” of threats to shipping in the Red Sea, Bab al-Mandeb and the Strait of Hormuz.

Sevinç writes that the agreement is a war alliance under US sponsorship, not a defensive one. Its effect is to bind Turkish and Pakistani workers to the defence of a Saudi monarchy that, he notes, opened its bases to the US war on Iran.

Saudi forces intercepted two drones over the Eastern Province on Tuesday. The Houthis denied a Saudi claim that a drone had been intercepted near Mecca. Africa

Ethiopia closes Sudan’s border consulate as Horn of Africa tensions mount

Ethiopia gave six staff of Sudan’s consulate in the border town of Asosa 48 hours to leave, and they departed Tuesday evening. On Sunday Sudan complained to the UN Security Council and the African Union that Ethiopia was letting the RSF launch drone attacks on Sudanese infrastructure from its territory. The same day, drone strikes on substations in Al-Dabbah and Abu Hamad cut power in northern Sudan. Ethiopia in turn accuses Khartoum of backing the Tigrayan rebels who launched an offensive in September. Sovereign Council adviser Amjad Farid said Sudan “remains committed to its right to defend its territory” but is trying to avoid open war. Ethiopia expelled 10 Eritrean diplomats on October 1, after which Eritrea broke off relations, and expelled an Egyptian diplomat last month amid the Nile dam dispute. Sudan’s war has displaced more than 14 million people.

Americas

Bukele sends 2,000 Salvadoran troops into Haiti

Salvadoran President Nayib Bukele posted videos of heavily armed soldiers patrolling Port-au-Prince and said more than 2,000 troops and military equipment had been sent. He addressed the gangs directly: “Gather your gangs so we can go fight with them.” The deployment followed an October 18 gang ambush after which four Salvadoran aviation technicians are still missing. UN spokesman Stéphane Dujarric said only about 200 Salvadoran soldiers fall under the UN-backed Gang Suppression Force. Haitian Prime Minister Alix Didier Fils-Aimé said the foreign troops operate “under Haitian authority.”

  • Elections: Haiti has had no elected government since 2021. The first round has been delayed from December 13 to July 18, 2027. Fewer than one million of about 6.5 million eligible voters have registered.
  • Humanitarian: About 5.94 million Haitians face acute hunger. Doctors Without Borders treated 30 injured people in the eight days after the disappearance and warns that “this armed conflict will claim even more victims.”

The deployment applies the methods of Bukele’s mass-incarceration regime, praised by Washington, to a country that has endured repeated US-backed occupations.

Pennsylvania records a second measles death as the state’s count passes 1,460

Pennsylvania has reported its second measles death of the outbreak, in Jefferson County, and now has 1,467 cases, the most of any state. In the latest week Pennsylvania added 88 cases and Wisconsin 82, according to CIDRAP’s tally. Nationally the CDC counts 4,852 confirmed cases in 2026, compared with 2,289 in all of 2025 and 285 in 2024.
