1. Xi and Putin meet ahead of first three-way summit with Trump in Shenzhen

Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin held talks in Shenzhen on Wednesday, the opening day of the APEC summit (November 18–19), one day before what the White House calls the first-ever trilateral meeting of the US, Chinese and Russian leaders. Both used the occasion to denounce Western “protectionism” and sanctions. Press reports quote the greeting “My dear friend, I am truly happy to meet you.” The Times of India reports that Xi made a veiled dig at Washington’s war on Iran (“Hands off Hormuz”) and called for dialogue to resolve the crisis. Trump flew to Shenzhen from Manila on Wednesday morning.

The trilateral is scheduled for Thursday. Trump reportedly plans to raise nuclear arms reductions and the Iran war. He is not expected to discuss Ukraine, and he has no separate one-on-one scheduled with Putin. A separate Trump–Xi bilateral would be their third meeting this year, after Beijing in May and Washington in September. Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum, who met Xi, Canadian PM Mark Carney and IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva at the forum, was due for a brief encounter with Trump at the leaders’ dinner.

Key facts: - Zelensky has warned of a Yalta-style “redistribution of spheres of influence” among the three powers. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov replied that agreements reached in Shenzhen cannot be enforced on others. - The summit follows the recent US-brokered Abu Dhabi talks between Ukraine, Russia and the US, from which the European powers were excluded. - The Economist’s morning brief paired the summit with Nvidia’s earnings as the day’s two dominant stories. That reflects how far the US–China technology war now shapes great-power diplomacy (see Nvidia). - Trump arrives from a Quad summit in Manila, where he promoted a critical-minerals and semiconductor bloc aimed at China (see International).

The meeting is a negotiation among nuclear-armed powers over spheres of influence, held while the US wages war on Iran and Ukraine is under heavy bombardment.

2. Record Ukrainian drone barrage strikes Kerch bridge as Europe backs escalation

Overnight on Sunday into Monday, Ukraine launched what NBC called its biggest drone attack on Russia since the war began. Moscow said its air defenses intercepted 1,336 drones. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said about 600 flew toward the capital region and 191 were shot down on approach. Ukrainian drones and missiles hit the Kerch Strait bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea, and PBS reported on Tuesday that a span had been destroyed. Zelensky called the strike “unprecedented.” The WSWS Perspective reports that two civilians were killed in Russia and six in occupied territories.

Scripps argues in today’s WSWS Perspective that Brussels, London, Berlin and Paris welcomed the strike as an answer to the Abu Dhabi talks, which sidelined Europe. In his reading, “Europe redoubles its commitment to the NATO-Russia war.” He writes that the European powers want the war to continue until Moscow suffers a decisive defeat, since their financing of Kyiv is premised on Russian reparations.

Key facts: - Russian attacks on Ukraine: Zelensky said Russia launched more than 2,300 attack drones, 1,590 glide bombs and 15 missiles in the past week. He called the strikes “brutal… difficult to counter” and cited a shortage of Patriot interceptors. - UN civilian toll, October 2026: 372 civilians killed and nearly 2,000 injured in Ukraine. That is the highest October toll since the full-scale invasion and a 95 percent increase year-on-year. In Russia and occupied territory, 149 civilians were killed and about 700 injured. - Infrastructure war: Ukraine is hitting Russian refineries, ports and cargo ships. Russia is striking Ukrainian power, transport and export infrastructure, causing widespread outages in near-freezing temperatures. - European positions: EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas reiterated that Russia is the aggressor. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said the European security architecture “already exists.”

The US–Iran war, now in its ninth month, escalated again across the Gulf on Tuesday. Al Jazeera and the Inquirer report that US forces struck Iran’s Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz after the Revolutionary Guard targeted Bahrain. The social cost of the US war and blockade is falling on Iranian workers going into winter. The bourgeois press frames it almost entirely as a question of “regime stability.”

4. United States loses measles elimination status after 25 years

On Tuesday, November 17, the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) announced that the United States has lost the measles elimination status it had held since 2000. Mexico and Bolivia lost theirs the same day. PAHO Director Jarbas Barbosa said “endemic measles transmission has now been reestablished in Bolivia, Mexico and the United States.” A source told Reuters that the CDC’s own report to PAHO found 12 months of continuous transmission. HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. said the administration will “work to regain” the status (ABC News). Kennedy has spent his tenure undermining vaccination.

PAHO had originally scheduled the US review for April and moved it on March 2 to its November meeting, after the midterms. Former CDC director Tom Frieden said, “One could hypothesize that they didn’t want to annoy the administration before the election.” PAHO denies this. Noel Brewer, who chairs the independent US committee that presented the findings, told the Wall Street Journal: “We’ve gotten comfortable letting children be stopped by a deadly disease and die from it.”

Key facts: - Cases: 5,412 confirmed in 2026 as of November 12 across 47 jurisdictions, with 43 new outbreaks. The totals were 2,289 in 2025 and 285 in 2024. Cases have exceeded 100 a week for 14 straight weeks. - Deaths: state health departments report 10 in 2026, five of them in November. The CDC counts 6 and PAHO 3. The last year with 10 or more US measles deaths was 1991, and only one death was recorded between 2004 and 2024. - Epicentres: Pennsylvania has 1,542 cases, 305 hospitalizations and 7 deaths. Wisconsin has 649 cases, its most in over 35 years, and its second pediatric death in eight days. Ohio has 372 cases, Indiana 171 and Minnesota 160. - Vaccination: kindergarten MMR coverage has fallen to 92.4 percent from 95.2 percent in 2019–20, leaving about 280,000 kindergartners unprotected. In LaGrange County, Indiana, coverage was reported at 35.9 percent. - Congenital measles: a CDC alert in NEJM Evidence counted 112 cases during pregnancy and 13 congenital cases since early 2025. CIDRAP’s Michael Osterholm called it “a brand new phenomenon.” - Regaining status requires interrupting transmission for at least 12 months. Canada lost its status in November 2025, so the Americas region has already lost elimination status.

As Benjamin Mateus writes on the WSWS, the collapse follows the decline of vaccination under policies pursued by both major parties, which HHS under Kennedy has now accelerated.

5. Nvidia beats forecasts in first post-Huang quarter as SEC probe and spy scandal widen

On Tuesday after the close, Nvidia reported third-quarter results that beat expectations. Revenue was reported at about $115.8 billion and the company issued a raised outlook (Bloomberg, DW). Yahoo Finance reported a forecast of $130 billion for the coming quarter and a 2.3 percent rise in the stock after hours. It was the company’s first report since co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on November 12. The resignation followed reports tying him to an affair with a woman described as a suspected Chinese intelligence operative.

The financial results were overshadowed by the political crisis around the world’s most valuable chipmaker. The Wall Street Journal reported Tuesday that the SEC is investigating Nvidia over Huang’s departure. Huang is expected to appear before three House committees investigating the company’s chip dealings with China. On Tuesday he signalled that he wanted to return as CEO, and the board said he would not be reinstated. Trump told CNBC on Monday he was “OK” with Huang returning, and by Wednesday was casting doubt on the spy allegation itself (South China Morning Post). Reports also say Trump’s personal Nvidia stake had risen to about $62 million before the resignation.

Key facts: - Bloomberg’s headline: “Nvidia Looks Past Jensen Huang With Raised Outlook, Strong Earnings.” - The Australian Financial Review reports that “tales of subterfuge cloud Nvidia as it tries to move on from Huang.” - The Commerce Department is moving to close a loophole allowing Chinese firms to use US data centers (CNBC, Nov 16). - Nasdaq futures rose after the report, following a session in which Salesforce fell about 9 percent and the Dow lost 849 points (see World economy).

The affair has drawn the AI monopoly at the center of the stock market boom directly into the US–China conflict, with the SEC, Congress and the White House all involved, on the eve of the Trump–Xi meeting.

6. Second nationwide strike in France as European protest wave builds toward November 20

Hundreds of thousands of French workers struck and marched on Tuesday, November 17, at the call of all the public-sector union federations, demanding wage increases against the freeze on civil servants’ pay. The Interior Ministry counted 146,000 marchers nationwide, including 28,000 in Paris. The CGT claimed more than 500,000, including 175,000 in Paris. Reuters reported that turnout fell at “largely peaceful” marches. On the first cross-industry strike day, November 5, the ministry had counted 575,000 and the CGT about 1.6 million. Le Monde wrote that the movement was sending a message to “the next occupant of the Elysée.”

The strike spread beyond the public sector. The CGT Ports and Docks Federation called stoppages at Le Havre, Rouen, Saint-Nazaire and Marseille-Fos. The CGT-Cheminots, SUD-Rail and CFDT-Cheminots rail unions also struck, and the CGT says nearly one in three rail workers stopped work. Regional train service was disrupted in several regions. High school students have blockaded schools each morning for about two months.

Key facts: - The state civil-service strike rate fell from nearly 10 percent on November 5 to 2.6 percent. The WSWS attributes the drop to the union bureaucracies and La France insoumise holding back the mobilization, not to any waning of anger. - On the day of the strike, LFI leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon announced at the Paris march that he would dine that evening with billionaire Bernard Arnault and TotalEnergies CEO Patrick Pouyanné. “We are condemned to live together, so we’ll do it,” he said. - Earlier, on November 15, organizers said 200,000 marched across France. Police reported 219 arrests, and demonstrations were banned in several cities. - Across Europe: Spain held a 4-million-strong strike on November 11. National strikes are planned in Italy, Belgium and Greece. Youth in Germany are protesting against military service. A coordinated European youth day of action is set for Friday, November 20, in more than 20 countries.

Turkey seizes Sozcu TV and detains opponents amid investment fund scandal

Europe/Middle East. At about 4:30 a.m. on Tuesday, counterterrorism police raided the Sözcü Media Group building in Istanbul’s Maslak district. The government appointed a trustee to Sözcü TV and sozcu.com.tr. The Financial Times and France 24 described Sözcü TV as Turkey’s last pro-opposition broadcaster. Detention warrants were issued for four people, including board chair Funda Tuncer Sıdalı. The pretext is a reopened “FETÖ” investigation into publisher Burak Akbay. Critics, as reported by the Stockholm Center for Freedom, link the takeover to the station’s coverage of a widening investment-fund scandal.

Key facts: - The scandal: on September 17 the Capital Markets Board liquidated 131 investment funds, freezing the savings of more than 450,000 people. The government is withholding the names of 141 individuals and 73 companies that made an “informed exit.” - Halk TV was taken off air on October 16. Since March 2025, 11 outlets have been placed under the state Savings Deposit Insurance Fund. - Dawn raids detained at least 16 people in İzmir, including TSS-İş union chair Ülkü Şeyda Başaraner, and nine in Diyarbakır, including Kurdistan Socialist Party chair Bayram Bozyel. - The opposition New Party (led by Özgür Özel) claims as many as 60 MPs may be named on the withheld list. - A MAK Consultancy poll (Nov 1–8) shows AKP support down 4.5 points in two months, to 27.5 percent.

Hundreds of thousands rally in Prague against Babis government media law

Europe. On Sunday, November 15, between 350,000 and 450,000 people rallied on Prague’s Letná Plain, according to organizers. Expats.cz called it the city’s biggest protest since 1989, and Reuters reported “hundreds of thousands.” The rally targeted the coalition of Prime Minister Andrej Babiš’s ANO, the far-right SPD and the Motorists. The trigger was a bill to abolish licence fees for Czech Television and Czech Radio, fund them from the state budget and cut their funding by about 15 percent. The broadcasters say this would force hundreds of layoffs. Babiš, SPD leader and Chamber speaker Tomio Okamura, and Foreign Minister Petr Macinka said immediately afterward that they would not change course (Radio Prague).

Key facts: - The bill passed its first reading at 4:36 a.m. on October 24 under fast-track procedure, hence the slogan “Letná against 4:36.” - The demands are withdrawal of the bill and Okamura’s removal as speaker. - Broadcast staff struck on November 3, and students are protesting cuts to higher education and research. - The organizer, Milion chvilek pro demokracii, is aligned with opposition parties close to President Petr Pavel, a former NATO general. The WSWS notes that speakers attacked the government for cutting defense spending.

Forensic scientist says Epstein autopsy evidence points to homicide

The New York Times reported Tuesday on a new analysis by forensic scientist Dr. Michael Freeman, working with pathologist Anders Eriksson (Umeå University) and cognitive neuroscientist Itiel Dror. They argue that the autopsy photographs of Jeffrey Epstein are inconsistent with suicidal hanging. Freeman presented the work at London’s Middle Temple on November 16. He told CBS the injuries are “most consistent with a homicidal strangulation from behind using a narrow knotted fabric ligature.” Epstein’s brother Mark supplied the autopsy file.

Key facts: - The cited evidence includes a second neck mark lower than and angled differently from the first (“There is no mechanism to produce a second mark that would be lower”), facial trauma, possible fingernail marks and deep left-shoulder hemorrhaging. - Knotted fabric photographed in the cell was never given to the medical examiner. - The original autopsy pathologist, Dr. Kristin Roman: “This doesn’t look anything like a strangulation.” New York’s medical examiner’s office stands by its suicide finding. - The analysis has not passed peer review. One journal rejected it, and Freeman plans to resubmit.

Ebola outbreak in Congo outpaces response as vaccine trials near

The Bundibugyo-strain Ebola epidemic in the Democratic Republic of Congo, declared on May 15, is spreading and killing “at a record pace” (Financial Express). WHO Director-General Tedros opened the third meeting of the International Health Regulations Emergency Committee on the outbreak on Tuesday. WHO announced that two Bundibugyo-specific vaccine candidates will enter phase 2 trials in the DRC next week as part of the Tumaini trial (TRT Afrika, Modern Ghana). WHO also raised concern over rising deaths and insecurity in the outbreak zone.

Case counts are inconsistent. DW reported on Tuesday that the caseload had topped 6,000. Earlier government figures gave 5,514 confirmed cases and 2,642 deaths, a fatality rate of about 48 percent. One specialist outlet (CASRAI) cites more than 11,000 cases, a figure that cannot be reconciled with the others and probably includes suspected cases. Ituri province accounts for about 85 percent of cases, and WHO says roughly two-thirds of deaths occur in communities rather than treatment centres. A genomic reconstruction reported by CIDRAP finds that human travel drove the spread. The epidemic is centred on a mineral-rich conflict zone, and the strain has no approved vaccine or therapy.

H5N1 human case count holds at 74

The CDC’s H5 situation page, updated November 5, lists 74 human cases since February 2024. Seven were detected through national flu surveillance and 67 through monitoring of exposed workers. The CDC no longer reports animal detections, which USDA now publishes. Transmission continues among wild birds, poultry and dairy cattle, and the CDC’s public risk assessment remains “low.” No new November human cases were found.

Artists should defend Panahi without reservation. Sri Lanka’s 2027 budget intensifies IMF austerity against working people — Saman Gunadasa

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake’s 2027 budget, presented November 12, projects revenue of 6.23 trillion rupees against debt repayments of 4.85 trillion ($15.5 billion), nearly four-fifths of revenue. Interest alone takes about 43 percent. The budget follows IMF targets of a 2.3 percent of GDP primary surplus and a 5 percent deficit. It requires every adult to register for a Taxpayer Identification Number, cuts the Aswesuma welfare program for nearly half a million people, plans to close 34 state enterprises and introduces a Public-Private Partnership Bill. There is no general wage rise, only allowances for the military, police, judiciary and doctors. Meanwhile, exporters investing over $3 million get double capital deductions, and the Ceylon Chamber of Commerce praised the budget. Real median household income fell 11.1 percent from 2019 to 2025, while the richest quintile’s wealth rose 6.8 percent. John Keells Holdings reported a 213 percent jump in quarterly profit. The current IMF program ends in March 2027, and a new one is under study. The JVP/NPP, elected in 2024 on anti-austerity promises, is carrying out the IMF’s program, and the SEP calls for independent action committees.

Workers and students in these struggles should be told about each other’s fights, and the case for linking them through rank-and-file committees can be made concretely.
