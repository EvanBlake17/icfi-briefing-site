1. Iran begins Khamenei funeral as fragile US-Israel ceasefire holds

Iran entered the second day of a six-day state funeral for Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, 86, who was killed along with several family members — including a 3-year-old granddaughter — in a joint US-Israeli strike on his compound on February 28, the opening day of the US-Israel war on Iran. The funeral program moves from Tehran to Qom, then to Iraq, and finally to Mashhad for burial. Representatives from more than 100 countries are attending, including Russia’s Dmitry Medvedev.

President Trump stated that “neither side will shoot the other during the funeral proceedings” and said negotiations with Iran will resume afterward, with both sides taking “a week off.” Trump and Netanyahu agreed to meet in Washington soon, though an Israeli official said the NATO summit in Turkey (July 7-8) could push the visit back a week. Sources describe growing distrust of Netanyahu inside Trump’s circle. Crowds in Tehran chanted for revenge against the US and Israel.

Key facts: - The strike that killed Khamenei was the opening act of a war of aggression launched by the two nuclear-armed powers against Iran. - Mojtaba Khamenei, the late leader’s second son, was designated supreme leader on March 8 but has not appeared publicly, fueling questions over who is actually governing. - The “week off” framing underscores that the ceasefire is a tactical pause, not a settlement.

Sources Al Jazeera Huge crowds mourn Khamenei; Trump vows calm during funeral · CBS News US-Iran war: Trump negotiations pause for ayatollah funeral · NPR Dayslong funeral for slain Supreme Leader Khamenei begins in Tehran

2. Gaza war reaches 1,000 days with 90 percent of the Strip destroyed

The Israeli assault on Gaza passed its 1,000th day with at least 73,066 Palestinians killed, including more than 21,500 children (1,022 of them babies). Roughly 9,500 people remain missing, believed buried under rubble, and 173,514 have been wounded. Israeli forces now control roughly 80% of Gaza after Netanyahu ordered the IDF on May 28 to seize 70% of the territory. Israel has killed at least 922 people during the nominal “ceasefire” period.

More than 90% of the Strip is destroyed, generating an estimated 68 million tonnes of rubble; at current clearance rates (under 0.5% removed) it would take more than 140 years to clear. Nearly 400,000 people are surviving on one meal a day, life expectancy has fallen to 40, and the UN says human development has been set back 77 years.

Key facts: - Israeli Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich: “We need to complete the conquest of the remaining area … establish a belt of Jewish settlements.” - The scale of destruction and forced starvation is a continuing, US-armed genocide, not a concluded war. - The “ceasefire” has functioned as cover for continued killing and territorial seizure.

Sources Al Jazeera Gaza war’s 1,000 days: 90% of Strip destroyed, 80% seized by Israel · CNN Netanyahu orders military to seize 70 percent of Gaza

3. Russian barrage on Kyiv kills at least 22

At least 22 people were killed and dozens injured after Russia launched 74 missiles and 496 drones at Kyiv. Ukrainian air defenses intercepted 48 missiles and 476 drones, but 25 ballistic missiles and 12 drones struck 33 locations. A nine-story residential building had 64 apartments destroyed; roughly 800,000 books were destroyed at a publishing warehouse; energy infrastructure was hit. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko declared a day of mourning.

Key facts: - Zelensky, cutting short a trip to Ireland, warned: “we have information about another massive Russian strike.” - Ukraine’s Air Force vowed, “We will take revenge!”; Russia’s defense ministry claimed it struck “military and energy facilities” in retaliation for Ukrainian strikes on Crimea and Belgorod. - The escalating tit-for-tat strikes underscore that the NATO-Russia proxy war continues without any diplomatic off-ramp.

Sources Al Jazeera Kyiv attacked after Zelensky warns of massive Russian strike · Kyiv Independent Russia launches mass missile and drone attack on Kyiv

4. Venezuela earthquake death toll nears 3,000

The official death toll from the twin earthquakes (magnitude 7.5 and 7.2) that struck June 24-25 has climbed to 2,954 as of July 4, with 12,400 injured, according to acting President Delcy Rodríguez. Nearly 50,000 people remain unaccounted for. Rodríguez declared seven days of national mourning as rescue and recovery operations continue nearly two weeks after the initial quakes.

Key facts: - The enormous number of missing suggests the confirmed toll will rise substantially. - US sanctions on Venezuela have gutted the country’s capacity to fund disaster response, medical supplies, and reconstruction — a decisive factor in the mounting casualty count. - The disaster exposes how imperialist economic warfare compounds natural catastrophe for the working class and poor.

Sources ABC News Venezuela earthquakes: 50,000 unaccounted as death toll climbs · Wikipedia 2026 Venezuela earthquakes

5. European heatwave death toll passes 1,300

The WHO recorded more than 1,300 excess deaths across Europe since June 21, disproportionately among people over 65. France recorded 2,025 excess deaths tied to the June heatwave, per the health minister, including at least 74 drowning deaths since June 18. Spain recorded more than 1,000 excess heat deaths in its second-hottest June on record. Météo-France logged the country’s hottest day since 1947 on June 23, hitting 44.3°C (111.7°F) in Pissos.

Key facts: - The elderly and the poor — those least able to afford cooling — bear the overwhelming share of the death toll. - The disaster is a direct product of capitalist-driven climate change combined with the absence of public infrastructure to protect vulnerable populations.

Sources Al Jazeera More than 1,300 deaths in Europe amid heatwave · France 24 France saw 2,025 excess deaths in June heatwave

6. Trump delivers politically charged July 4 address

Trump spoke after 11 p.m. following a weather delay, in record 102°F heat that broke Washington’s 1919 July 4 record; 12 people on the National Mall were hospitalized for heat. Breaking from the traditionally apolitical character of Independence Day addresses, Trump repeatedly attacked “communism” and promoted the SAVE America Act, which would impose proof-of-citizenship and photo-ID requirements on voter registration. “Tonight we pledge allegiance to the flag they gave us, and God bless the immortal patriots of 1776,” he said. A record 850,000 shells were fired in the fireworks display.

Key facts: - Cited polling puts Trump at 38% approval and 55% disapproval. - The address explicitly wove nationalist iconography together with an authoritarian program to restrict the franchise. - The rhetoric came amid a record ICE detention buildup and mass deportation drive (see United States).

Sources ABC News Trump holds rally to celebrate July Fourth in Washington · CNBC Trump veers into darkly political speech for US 250th anniversary

7. Sudan’s civil war escalates as RSF strikes El Obeid

The UN documented 15 drone strikes on El Obeid and surrounding areas in three weeks, killing at least 45 civilians and hitting markets, schools, and water infrastructure. Roughly 300 people were reportedly killed in RSF-led attacks in July on Barra locality in North Kordofan; SAF airstrikes in West Kordofan killed at least 23 civilians. This month the RSF and allied groups announced a parallel government based in Darfur, raising the prospect of Sudan’s de facto partition.

Key facts: - The war has internally displaced more than 13 million people, with millions more at risk of famine. - A senior UN official warned the window to prevent a “major escalation” in El Obeid is “rapidly narrowing.” - The conflict is fueled by rival regional and imperialist powers competing over Sudan’s gold, agricultural land, and Red Sea access.

Sources UN News Sudan: Civilians under siege as drone strikes hit El Obeid · Human Rights Watch Sudan: Risk of imminent atrocities in and around El Obeid

Slovak referendum on Fico payments fails on low turnout

A referendum organized by Slovakia’s opposition Democrats party against populist PM Robert Fico failed after turnout reached just 16.1% with 99.5% of polling stations counted — far short of the 50% threshold needed for validity. Triggered by a 350,000-signature petition, the vote asked whether to cancel lifelong payments to former PMs and speakers and whether to reopen the anti-corruption special prosecutor’s office and National Criminal Agency. The collapse in turnout reflects widespread popular alienation from all wings of the official political establishment.

Sources Washington Post Slovakia referendum on Fico fails amid low turnout · Wikipedia 2026 Slovak referendum

Pope Leo XIV visits Lampedusa, defends migrants

The first US-born pope visited Lampedusa, a key Mediterranean migrant arrival point, on July 4, calling migration a “momentous challenge” Europe can meet “with compassion and planning.” He prayed at a cemetery for migrants who died at sea and blessed a pier renamed for Pope Francis, who visited the island in 2013. In a message to Americans on Independence Day, Leo said defending human life means “welcoming, protecting and assisting immigrants” — an implicit rebuke of US deportation policy. The intervention highlights the intensifying contradiction between official state persecution of migrants and mass humanitarian sentiment.

US-brokered Congo-Rwanda deal ties minerals to Western investment

Congo and Rwanda signed a US-brokered agreement on June 27 linking a ceasefire and territorial-integrity pledges to Western economic investment, even as M23/AFC fighting continues and roughly 7 million Congolese remain internally displaced. Critics note the deal centers resource-access arrangements for foreign capital — including access to cobalt, coltan, and other strategic minerals — rather than accountability for the conflict’s violence. The framework is a naked expression of the scramble for Central Africa’s mineral wealth dressed as peacemaking.

ICE detention population hits record 73,000

ICE detention capacity has grown more than 75% under the current administration, reaching a record roughly 73,000 people in custody, following passage of the Secure America Act, which included $350 million to expand arrests in “sanctuary” jurisdictions. Immigration advocates describe an “increasingly unaccountable” detention system with minimal independent oversight. The buildup — the material infrastructure behind the mass deportation drive — accompanies Trump’s July 4 nationalist rhetoric and the criminalization of protest against ICE.

Sources American Immigration Council ICE’s expanding and increasingly unaccountable detention system

PECO utility workers strike for the first time in over a century

IBEW Local 614 workers at PECO, Philadelphia’s electric and gas utility — including gas-emergency responders — walked off the job at midnight on July 4 after five months of failed negotiations. Central issues include pension benefits for newer hires and medical savings account eligibility for employees hired after 2014. It is the first strike at the utility in more than a century, a major utility-sector labor action with direct public-safety implications that has received minimal national coverage.

Sources WHYY PECO strike 2026: union negotiations

State-level proof-of-citizenship voting laws take effect

Even as the federal SAVE America Act stalls in Congress, proof-of-citizenship voting laws modeled on it are already being enforced in six states — including New Hampshire, Wyoming, South Dakota, Ohio, and Utah — for the 2026 midterms, effectively achieving the voter-restriction agenda without federal passage. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer has called it “Jim Crow 2.0,” even as the Democratic Party mounts no serious mobilization against the disenfranchisement drive.

Sources Brennan Center for Justice The SAVE Act and the election power grab

Deadly heat wave grips 185 million as federal safety rule stalls

A severe heat wave affected more than 185 million people across the US, with Washington hitting a record 102°F on July 4. There remains no enforced federal heat-safety standard — OSHA’s rule has been pending for over 50 years — and municipal cooling-assistance funds are exhausted. Workers in construction, agriculture, warehousing, and delivery remained on the job with no mandated protections, exposing what the WSWS calls government and corporate indifference to preventable heat deaths.

US measles cases near full-year 2025 total

As of July 2, 2026, the US has recorded 2,170 confirmed measles cases across 41 jurisdictions, with 31 new outbreaks in 2026 and 93% of cases (2,019 of 2,170) outbreak-associated; 138 people (6%) have been hospitalized, with no deaths reported so far this year. Case counts are already approaching the full-year 2025 total with six months still to go, driven by kindergarten MMR vaccination coverage falling to 92.5% nationally — below the 95% herd-immunity threshold — leaving an estimated 286,000 kindergartners unprotected. The resurgence is the direct product of the anti-vaccine agenda now entrenched in federal public-health leadership.

Sources Global Biodefense US measles cases in 2026 nearly match full-year 2025 toll · CDC Measles data and research

H5N1 surveillance updated; udder-tropism mystery solved

Between August 4, 2025 and June 10, 2026, 12 human H5N1 infections were reported outside the US (Bangladesh: 3, Cambodia: 8, India: 1), including 3 deaths, all linked to sick or dead poultry exposure with no evidence of human-to-human transmission. The CDC and WHO maintain that public-health risk remains low, but continued mammalian spillover keeps surveillance a priority. Separately, researchers explained why H5N1 behaves differently in dairy cattle: the receptors the virus needs are abundant throughout udder tissue but nearly absent in airway tissue, explaining why it concentrates in milk rather than causing respiratory disease. One hundred dairy herds across 18 states remain in USDA’s voluntary monitoring program as field trials of a cattle vaccine continue.

Sources CDC H5N1 global summary · ScienceDaily Why H5N1 behaves differently in dairy cattle

Additional developments:

  • Neuroscience: A study in Nature (Vol. 655, July 2) combined single-neuron recordings from the human frontotemporal cortex with language models to identify distinct neuron populations encoding grammatical relationships, parts of speech, and higher-order syntactic structure during speech production — one of the most granular maps yet of how the brain physically implements grammar, with implications for speech-restoration brain-computer interfaces. (Nature)
  • AI policy: The FTC voted 2-0 on July 1 to publish a proposed policy statement asserting that AI companies steering model outputs toward undisclosed ideological objectives may violate Section 5 of the FTC Act’s ban on deceptive practices — the first federal move to frame AI “viewpoint steering” as a consumer-protection violation. Public comment runs through July 31. (FTC)
  • AI and labor: The RAISE US coalition, backed by major AI labs including Anthropic, estimated that AI-displaced roles accounted for roughly 88,000 fewer jobs than trend expectations in June — among the first monthly reports attributing a specific, quantified jobs shortfall directly to AI adoption. (Tech Startups)

Arts and culture

The most politically revealing cultural event of the week was the reported $25-100 million wedding of pop superstar Taylor Swift and NFL player Travis Kelce, staged at Madison Square Garden. As Kevin Reed argued on the WSWS, the spectacle functions as modern “bread and circuses” — a lavish celebrity distraction unfolding in the same city where hundreds of thousands live in poverty, and timed suspiciously close to the Independence Day festivities. The event, and the wall-to-wall entertainment-press coverage it commanded, epitomizes a culture industry that recycles extreme private wealth as mass entertainment while the social crisis deepens. That such extravagance is presented as aspirational, rather than obscene, is itself a measure of the ideological role assigned to celebrity culture.

The week also underscored how sections of the media establishment are actively cultivating the far right. In Australia, TV host Karl Stefanovic launched a podcast featuring far-right figures including Pauline Hanson and British agitator Tommy Robinson before it was pulled following public backlash. The WSWS analysis situates this not as an isolated misjudgment but as part of a broader strategy by media elites to channel genuine social alienation toward reactionary, nationalist politics rather than allow it to find left-wing expression — a warning about the mainstreaming of fascistic voices under the guise of “anti-establishment” edginess.

Roundup:

  • Independence Day cultural programming: The federal “Freedom 250” commemoration drove a wave of official 250th-anniversary cultural events, museum exhibitions, and media retrospectives — overwhelmingly framed in nationalist and celebratory terms that suppress the revolutionary, egalitarian content of 1776 and its unfinished character.
  • Music and visual art: The RCP/IMT’s own arts coverage this week (see pseudo-left review) surveyed jazz titan Sonny Rollins, painter Hurvin Anderson, and the exhibition “Stitching the Intifada” — a reminder that Palestinian solidarity continues to surface in gallery and textile art even amid institutional pressure and the political climate around Gaza.
  • Publishing loss in Kyiv: The Russian strike that destroyed roughly 800,000 books at a Kyiv publishing warehouse (see Top stories) is also a cultural atrocity — the physical destruction of literature and the material basis of Ukrainian intellectual life, a dimension of the war that deserves recognition alongside the human toll.

Across these developments a common thread runs: culture under conditions of war, austerity, and oligarchy is increasingly pressed into service either as distraction from the social crisis or as a vehicle for reactionary politics — even as genuine artistic engagement with war and oppression persists at the margins.

Jacobin / DSA

Jacobin marked Independence Day with a trio of pieces reclaiming American nationalism for the reformist left: “In Defense of the Fourth of July,” which seeks to wrest the Declaration from the right by presenting a progressive-patriotic genealogy; “Burn the Constitution Once Again,” which frames the crisis as one of a defective founding document to be democratically rewritten; and “Zohran Mamdani on the Promise of America,” a platform for the DSA-backed New York mayoral figure’s “socialist” vision of national renewal. The line is constitutional reformism wrapped in socialist rhetoric: the crisis is presented as a matter of amending or rewriting American institutions rather than of the class character of the capitalist state. The promotion of Mamdani — who operates through the Democratic Party — reflects Jacobin’s core function of channeling radicalized workers and youth back into the two-party system. Overall orientation: left-nationalist rehabilitation of American democracy, directly counterposed to the WSWS’s insistence that the Declaration’s promise can only be realized through socialist revolution against both capitalist parties.

Left Voice

Left Voice continues to advance its call “For a Movement for a Revolutionary Socialist International — The Fourth International,” counterposing a diffuse “regroupment” of ostensibly revolutionary forces to the actual continuity of Trotskyism embodied in the ICFI. The line is classically Morenoite: an abstract appeal to internationalism combined, in practice, with the united-front maneuvers the WSWS has documented — a “united front” that in operation props up the union bureaucracy and the Democratic Party rather than breaking workers from them. Overall orientation: centrist, oscillating between revolutionary phraseology and adaptation to the existing apparatuses, with no independent perspective for state power.

PSL / Liberation News

Liberation News’s coverage of the US-Israeli war on Iran and the Khamenei funeral centers a defense of Iran against imperialist aggression that slides into political support for the bourgeois-clerical regime itself. The correct opposition to US-Israeli war is fused with campism — treating the Tehran government as an anti-imperialist camp to be defended rather than a capitalist regime that suppresses the Iranian working class. This popular-frontist framing obscures the class struggle within Iran and subordinates workers to bourgeois nationalist leaderships. Overall orientation: anti-imperialism defined geopolitically rather than through the independent mobilization of the working class.

Socialist Alternative

Socialist Alternative (ISA) continues to foreground spontaneous protest movements, as in “Albania’s ‘Flamingo Revolution’ Takes On Trump Family Corruption,” which celebrates youth-led mobilizations abroad, alongside general propaganda such as “Only Rotten Choices In Their System — We Need A Planned Economy.” The pattern is enthusiasm for the movement as such, with abstract calls for a planned economy standing in for a concrete revolutionary strategy and a struggle against the existing labor and “progressive” leaderships. Overall orientation: movementist and propagandistic, adapting to whatever protest wave is ascendant without a fight to build an independent revolutionary party.
