1. Russia launches devastating Kyiv strike as ground offensive collapses

An 11-hour Russian barrage of roughly 500 drones and dozens of missiles killed at least 30 people in Kyiv on July 2, the third-deadliest strike on the Ukrainian capital since the war began. The mass attack came even as Russia’s summer ground offensive has ground to a near-halt, exposing the deadlocked and attritional character of the conflict.

The imperialist proxy war remains a central, near-daily WSWS subject, framed around the mounting casualties on both sides and the drive of NATO powers to use Ukrainian lives to weaken Russia. The collapse of Russian territorial gains has not brought the war closer to an end but has instead intensified the aerial destruction of Ukrainian cities.

Key facts:

  • Russia’s net territorial advance fell to just 97 sq km in the first half of 2026, down from 2,190 sq km in the same period of 2025 — the daily advance rate collapsing from 16.6 sq km/day to 1.03 sq km/day.
  • Ukraine’s military estimates Russia suffered 39,490 casualties in June alone, against an estimated monthly recruitment capacity of only 24,000–30,000.
  • Ukraine destroyed 12 electricity substations in Crimea (July 1–2) and struck the Saky air base and a major Lukoil refinery as part of a 40-day long-range strike campaign.
  • Zelensky, on Russian losses: “If Putin wants to send another million of his soldiers… these million Russians… should think about what awaits them next.”

2. Gaza war reaches 1,000 days with 90% of the territory destroyed

The genocidal war on Gaza has passed its 1,000th day with the Strip effectively obliterated. Gaza’s Government Media Office puts the death toll at 73,066, including over 21,500 children — among them 1,022 babies — with 9,500 people still missing under the rubble. The October truce has proven a fiction, with mass killing continuing under the cover of a “ceasefire.”

Gaza is consistently the top international story on the WSWS, which frames it around US and Israeli war crimes and the direct complicity of the imperialist powers. The scale of physical destruction now documented underscores that the destruction of Palestinian society, not the defeat of Hamas, has been the aim.

Key facts:

  • 90% of Gaza has been destroyed and Israel controls 80% of the territory; 68 million tonnes of rubble have been generated, of which only 0.5% has been cleared — an estimated 140-plus years to remove at the current pace.
  • Since the October truce, at least 1,053 Palestinians have been killed and 3,406 wounded; only a third of the pledged daily aid trucks are entering.
  • Gaza City Mayor Yahya al-Sarraj: “We lost about 85 to 90 percent of our resources, our buildings and our infrastructure.”
  • Hamas has reportedly rejected disarmament proposals, arguing Israel is already violating its own ceasefire terms.

4. ICE arrests surge to 10,000 in five days under new White House quota

Federal agents detained more than 10,000 people in five days in late June — roughly 2,000 per day — under a new arrest quota imposed by the White House. The surge follows Trump’s signing of a reconciliation package containing $70 billion for immigration enforcement, with ICE directed to assign 80% of its officers to “arrest operations.”

Immigration enforcement and state repression are core recurring WSWS coverage areas. As Jacob Crosse wrote on the WSWS, the raids are “a fascistic assault on the whole working class,” mostly targeting people without criminal records and following the Supreme Court’s elimination of Temporary Protected Status for over a million people.

Key facts:

  • Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin pledged a “quieter” nationwide campaign spread across many cities rather than concentrated single-city surges, after the fallout from the Minnesota operation.
  • Many ICE agents are reportedly now working seven days a week to meet the new daily arrest standard.
  • The push targets worksites, with the White House ordering expanded worksite raids.

5. US hiring collapses to 57,000 jobs in June

Nonfarm payrolls rose just 57,000 in June, less than half of May’s downwardly revised 129,000 and well below the 115,000 consensus forecast, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. April and May payrolls were revised down a combined 74,000 jobs. The headline unemployment rate held at 4.2% only because workers left the labor force.

As Kevin Reed noted on the WSWS, the nominally steady jobless rate masks deepening weakness: 720,000 people left the labor force in June, driving participation down to its lowest level in over five years.

Key facts:

  • Labor force participation fell to 61.5%, the lowest since March 2021; the U-6 underemployment rate stood at 7.9%.
  • Leisure and hospitality employment fell by 61,000. Gains were concentrated in professional/business services (+36,000), social assistance (+25,000), and healthcare (+22,000).
  • The report sent Treasury yields lower and lifted expectations of Federal Reserve rate cuts.

Deadly heat wave drives a mounting public health emergency

The heat wave gripping the United States is fundamentally a public health emergency as well as a labor one. Heat indexes reaching 115°F across a region home to more than 185 million people put the elderly, the chronically ill, and outdoor workers at acute risk of heat stroke and cardiovascular collapse. Emergency departments across the affected regions reported spikes in heat-related admissions. Public health experts note that the absence of a federal occupational heat standard — 50 years after OSHA’s creation — leaves the workforce without enforceable protections, even as climate change makes such events more frequent and severe. The roughly 3,700 heat-related deaths recorded across Europe during comparable events underscore the scale of preventable mortality.

H5N1 bird flu continues low-level global spread

The CDC’s latest global summary reports 12 human infections with avian influenza A(H5N1) outside the United States between August 2025 and June 2026, reported by Bangladesh, Cambodia and India, with three deaths (one in Bangladesh, two in Cambodia). No person-to-person spread was identified, and the CDC continues to assess the risk to the US public as low. Within the US, H5N1 remains widespread in wild birds and continues to cause outbreaks in commercial poultry across several states and in dairy cattle, with sporadic human infections among dairy and poultry workers. The ongoing circulation keeps alive the risk of viral reassortment.

New studies link climate change to health and social breakdown

  • A study in Nature Human Behaviour found that “social health” — the ability to access and maintain meaningful human relationships — is a critical determinant of population health and climate resilience, and that climate change disrupts housing stability and community cohesion while social disconnection undermines collective capacity to respond.
  • Research in Nature Medicine warned that many climate-health projections conflate three distinct drivers of future risk: changes in climate hazards, population growth, and the underlying vulnerability of exposed populations — with implications for how mortality estimates are interpreted.

Other developments:

  • Measles: The US continues to log confirmed cases across multiple states in 2026, with Florida among the states reporting the most cases (21 confirmed) — sustaining the trend of resurgent vaccine-preventable disease amid declining immunization rates and public health funding cuts.
  • Technology/AI: The expanding role of Palantir in the British state — including an NHS data contract now reportedly under review — highlights the fusion of AI surveillance infrastructure with the state apparatus, a development with far-reaching implications for democratic rights.

WSWS coverage — last 24 hours

Perspective published: “250 years since the Declaration of Independence” by the WSWS Editorial Board (confirmed today, URL folder /2026/07/04/) — read in full on wsws.org.

The Perspective marks the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence, arguing the anniversary falls at a moment of “staggering crisis and decay” for American democracy. It traces the Declaration’s radical legacy — “all men are created equal,” government by “consent of the governed” — through its influence on the French Revolution, the Haitian Revolution, the revolutions of 1848 and 19th-century national unification struggles, citing Marx’s remark in Capital that the American War of Independence “sounded the tocsin” for European bourgeois revolutions. It stresses that 1776, as a bourgeois-democratic revolution, could not resolve the contradiction of slavery — a problem only settled by the Civil War, the “Second American Revolution.”

The editorial then maps the clauses of the Declaration’s indictment of King George III onto present conditions: the military rendered “superior to the Civil Power”; immigrants “transported beyond Seas” to the CECOT camp in El Salvador; agents shielded “from punishment for any Murders,” citing the killings of Renée Good and Alex Pretti. It invokes historian Adam Hochschild’s remark at a June 25 WSWS webinar that the indictment reads “as if it were written this morning.” On inequality, it notes society has “minted its first trillionaire, Elon Musk,” with nearly 1,000 billionaires holding $8.4 trillion. It identifies two complementary bourgeois responses — the far right’s nationalist appropriation of revolutionary imagery and the Democratic Party’s embrace of the 1619 Project’s view of the Revolution as a counter-revolution — and, quoting Trotsky’s Results and Prospects, argues the working class is now the legitimate heir of 1776. It concludes: “the third American revolution will be a socialist revolution, made by the working class as part of the world revolution against capitalism.”

USPS rural carrier kidnapped and murdered in North Carolina — Jane Wise

Documents the June 26 murder of USPS rural carrier Brandi Byrd Reynolds, 35, in Hays, NC, by a man charged with murder and kidnapping. The article contextualizes the killing against rising rural crime, USPS cost-cutting, and workers being forced to use personal vehicles on unsafe, isolated routes. It argues that union “law-and-order” responses are inadequate and calls for worker-controlled rank-and-file safety committees. Unique to this piece is the connection drawn between postal management’s degradation of working conditions — routes lengthened, protections stripped — and the vulnerability of carriers working alone, a dimension absent from the local crime coverage.

July 4th and America’s revolutionary influence on Britain — Thomas Scripps

A historical essay tracing the American Revolution’s influence on British abolitionism — over 400,000 anti-slavery petition signatures by 1792 — and on radical democratic movements from the Society for Constitutional Information through Chartism. It concludes that this internationalist revolutionary legacy belongs to socialism, not to nationalism. The piece provides a valuable transatlantic companion to the Perspective, documenting how 1776 catalyzed democratic radicalization in the imperial metropole itself.

1. PECO workers launch first strike in the company’s 100-plus year history

Roughly 1,500–1,600 IBEW Local 614 members walked off the job at the Philadelphia utility PECO just after midnight on July 4 after contract talks collapsed. The central dispute is over a two-tier retirement system — workers hired after 2014/2021 lack the pensions of senior staff — despite PECO’s 20% wage offer over five years. Other regional utility locals have directed members not to cross the picket line, raising the prospect of a broader confrontation with the union apparatus over the two-tier divide-and-rule strategy.

Recommended reading Philadelphia Inquirer With no deal in place, Peco workers are on strike

A Human Rights Watch report details how Operation Metro Surge in Minneapolis left two American citizens — nurse Alex Pretti and Renee Good — dead at the hands of federal agents in January 2026, with video evidence reportedly contradicting the official account. Months later, no federal investigation has concluded, and Minnesota has sued to obtain evidence. The report directly substantiates the Perspective’s charge that agents are shielded “from punishment for any Murders.”

Recommended reading Human Rights Watch “A Manufactured Crisis”: Minnesota Communities Terrorized by the Federal Government

A new Amnesty International report documents systematic RSF targeting of civilians, including children, during the capture of El Fasher — calling the atrocities “a stain on the conscience of humanity.” The findings illuminate the pattern now threatening El Obeid and raise urgent questions about the Gulf-state arms supplies sustaining the RSF, a dimension of imperialist responsibility that warrants dedicated WSWS treatment.

Recommended reading Amnesty International Sudan: RSF atrocities in El Fasher ‘a stain on the conscience of humanity’

4. Venezuela’s post-Maduro order: “authoritarian adaptation,” not democratic transition

Six months after the US military captured Nicolás Maduro and installed a client government, analysts note that repression, economic uncertainty and client-state dynamics persist unchanged for ordinary Venezuelans — a picture now compounded by the state’s strained earthquake response. This provides essential political context for the WSWS’s earthquake coverage and a case study in the reality behind US “democracy promotion.”

Recommended reading WOLA Two months without Maduro: Democratic transition or authoritarian adaptation?

New studies in Nature journals disentangle the drivers of climate-related mortality and document how warming erodes the “social health” underpinning population resilience. As heat kills workers across the US and Europe, the WSWS could connect this peer-reviewed science to its critique of a profit system incapable of a rational, planned response to climate change.

Recommended reading Nature Human Behaviour Climate change and social health

The case is a significant front in the defense of democratic rights against the administration’s assault, and the gap between legal victory and continued state defiance is politically instructive.
