1. US and Iran lurch toward full-scale war as Hormuz ceasefire collapses

The United States concluded its eighth consecutive night of strikes on Iran early July 19 as the collapse of the summer ceasefire pushed Washington and Tehran toward open war. Two US service members were killed and one remains missing after an Iranian attack on the Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan, raising total US fatalities to 16 since the conflict resumed. Iran’s Health Ministry reports 50 people killed and more than 500 injured — including two children — between June 27 and July 18.

The economic shockwave is already global. Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a fifth of the world’s oil passes, has collapsed from about 110 ships per day before the war to just 8–13 transits in the past 24 hours. Oil prices jumped more than 4 percent. Writing on the WSWS, Andre Damon documented that US strikes have widened from military to civilian targets — bridges, a railway station, an airport and the control tower of Iran’s only deep-water port — marking, in his words, a deliberate escalation of “US imperialist war aims against the civilian population.”

Key facts: - Iran retaliated by striking Kuwaiti desalination plants and oil facilities; the IRGC claims it hit a US support center at Camp Arifjan and destroyed a radar installation. - Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei called Trump’s signature on the earlier ceasefire “utterly worthless and devoid of” credibility, warning of “unforgettable lessons.” - The IMF calls the disruption the “largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market.”

The collapse of the ceasefire — signed under US pressure and abandoned within weeks — exposes the fraudulence of Washington’s claim to be seeking de-escalation. Each round of strikes and counter-strikes draws the region closer to a general conflagration that could pull in Israel, the Gulf monarchies and the major powers. The war is unfolding in tandem with the escalation of authoritarian rule at home, a connection the ICFI has stressed: imperialist war abroad and the assault on democratic rights domestically are two expressions of the same crisis of the profit system. Only the independent intervention of the international working class — including US and Iranian workers — can halt the drive to a Middle East war with global dimensions.

2. Israeli strikes keep killing Palestinians as Gaza “ceasefire” unravels

At least eight Palestinians were killed Saturday in Israeli strikes on the al-Nasr and al-Zeitoun neighborhoods of Gaza City. Five died when missiles struck an apartment; three more were killed by artillery fire. Since the US-brokered ceasefire began in October, at least 1,127 Palestinians have been killed, including 260 children, according to Gaza’s Health Ministry.

Israel has expanded military control to roughly 70 percent of Gaza, well beyond the approximately 50 percent it was permitted to hold under the “Yellow Line” terms of the agreement. Al Jazeera’s Hani Mahmoud reported that the expansion is producing an “erasure of urban life,” fragmenting the territory into isolated cantons. The WSWS has consistently characterized the “ceasefire” as a fiction masking the continuation of the genocide.

Key facts: - The strikes continue amid the ongoing enforcement of the ICC arrest warrant issued against Netanyahu in 2024 (see story 6). - Aid access remains sharply restricted despite the nominal truce, with international agencies reporting continued acute malnutrition.

The steady killing under cover of a nominal ceasefire — averaging more than a dozen deaths a day since October — confirms that the agreement served to legitimize, not end, the destruction of Gaza. The expansion of control to 70 percent of the territory points to a policy of permanent occupation and de facto annexation, carried out with the full backing of the Trump administration.

Sources Al Jazeera Israeli attacks on residential areas in Gaza kill eight · The Defense Post Gaza: Israeli airstrike kills eight

3. Ukraine hits Russian shadow fleet and refineries as casualties mount

Ukraine’s Unmanned Systems Forces struck 13 vessels of Russia’s “shadow fleet” in the Black Sea and Sea of Azov overnight July 18, including eight cargo ships, a tanker, a gas tanker, a tugboat and two floating cranes. The attack marks a significant expansion of Ukraine’s campaign against the sanctions-evading fleet Russia uses to sell oil.

On land, the grinding attritional character of the NATO-Russia proxy war was again on display. Russian forces carried out 851 strikes on 51 settlements in the Zaporizhzhia region in a single day, killing one person and wounding two, including a child. Some 276 combat clashes were recorded on July 17, concentrated in the Pokrovsk and Huliaipole directions.

Key facts: - Russia gained only 623 sq km in the first half of 2026; June alone is estimated to have cost roughly 39,500 Russian casualties for just 30 sq km of territory. - Ukrainian long-range strikes have now hit 25 refineries representing 82 percent of Russian refining capacity, cutting June refining output by an estimated 25 percent.

4. Mass protests sweep Israel against Netanyahu and Haredi draft law

More than 1,000 Israelis rallied at Tel Aviv’s Habima Square Saturday, with hundreds more demonstrating nationwide against Netanyahu’s coalition following the passage of judicial-overhaul and Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) draft-exemption legislation just before the Knesset’s summer recess. The protests unfold amid an active 2026 election cycle, with polling showing gains for the opposition.

The draft-exemption law — shielding ultra-Orthodox men from military service even as the government demands continued mobilization for its wars — has become a flashpoint for bereaved families. Gilad Golan, whose son was killed in combat on October 7, 2023, told demonstrators: “When a government doesn’t take responsibility, there’s no chance that anything will improve.”

Key facts: - The protests combine anger over the assault on judicial checks with resentment over the unequal burden of military service. - The WSWS did not cover the domestic protest movement directly — a coverage gap given the scale of internal Israeli political crisis.

Sources Haaretz Thousands protest Netanyahu govt, Haredi draft-evasion laws across Israel

5. Sudan’s El Obeid under RSF siege, half a million trapped

Roughly 500,000 people are trapped in El Obeid, the capital of North Kordofan, as the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) tighten a siege begun weeks ago, cutting nearly all supply routes except a single eastern corridor. The city has been without electricity for almost a month, and food prices have surged by as much as 300 percent. UN human rights officials warn of an impending “catastrophe,” expecting El Obeid to become the site of major ground clashes reminiscent of the El Fasher bloodbath.

Over three weeks, the UN documented 15 drone strikes on El Obeid and surrounding areas, killing at least 45 civilians. The drone campaign points directly to the RSF’s Gulf-state backers, principally the United Arab Emirates — the imperialist proxy dimension that receives far less Western scrutiny than the humanitarian toll alone.

Key facts: - The European Parliament adopted a resolution urging the EU to designate the RSF a terrorist organization. - The siege is the latest front in a war that has produced the world’s largest displacement crisis.

The El Obeid siege follows the pattern of El Fasher, where an RSF assault produced mass atrocities. The war between the RSF and the Sudanese Armed Forces is not a purely internal affair but a proxy conflict fueled by rival regional and imperialist powers competing for control of Sudan’s gold, agricultural land and Red Sea coastline — with the UAE’s arming of the RSF the most direct expression of this outside intervention.

Sources Al Jazeera El Obeid under siege by RSF: Could this be Sudan’s next El Fasher? · UN News Sudan: Warning of catastrophe in El Obeid

6. Mamdani says he is “actively” exploring arrest of Netanyahu under ICC warrant

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani said his administration is in “active conversation” with the city’s Law Department over whether he has the legal authority to order the arrest of Netanyahu should the Israeli prime minister visit New York for the UN General Assembly in September. “Whatever the law allows me to do in New York City, that’s what we will do,” Mamdani said, “but we won’t be writing our own laws to that end.”

The ICC issued an arrest warrant for Netanyahu in 2024 over alleged war crimes including “starvation as a method of warfare.” New York Governor Kathy Hochul has stated that the mayor lacks the legal authority to act, and the United States does not recognize ICC jurisdiction. The WSWS has been sharply critical of Mamdani as a Democratic Party figure whose gestures on Gaza are not matched by action.

China posts 4.3% Q2 GDP growth as Xi launches global AI alliance

China’s economy expanded 4.3 percent in the second quarter of 2026 as export strength offset domestic weakness. Full-year growth is projected to slow to 4.4 percent amid a continuing property-sector downturn. The data underscores Beijing’s growing reliance on external demand at a moment of intensifying trade conflict with Washington.

At the World Artificial Intelligence Conference in Shanghai, Xi Jinping formally launched the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization (WAICO) on July 16 with 29 founding members, including Russia, Brazil, Indonesia, South Africa and Pakistan. “AI development should not be a solo performance by a single country,” Xi said, “but a symphony of international cooperation” — a pointed challenge to US efforts to monopolize advanced computing. Chipmaker CXMT is seeking a $10 billion Shanghai IPO, which would be China’s largest since 2010.

Venezuela earthquake death toll surpasses 3,600

The official death toll from the June 24 twin earthquakes in Venezuela — magnitudes 7.2 and 7.5, the strongest to strike the country in over a century — has climbed above 3,600, with the real figure believed to be significantly higher as thousands remain unaccounted for. The quakes, centered in Yaracuy state, caused widespread destruction across multiple states.

The disaster compounds an already catastrophic social crisis. Civil-society group HumVenezuela estimates 18.2 million people are in humanitarian need as of January 2026, with 12.4 million facing critical needs even before the earthquakes. Years of US sanctions and economic collapse have gutted the infrastructure needed to respond, leaving relief efforts overwhelmed.

Sources Wikipedia Crisis in Venezuela · Global Centre for R2P Atrocity Alert No. 490: Sudan, Lebanon and Venezuela

European energy crisis deepens as Hormuz closure and Qatari LNG suspension hit workers

The Strait of Hormuz crisis and a suspension of Qatari LNG shipments have driven the Dutch TTF gas benchmark to nearly double, to over €60/MWh, delivering a second major energy shock to European workers in under five years. The European Central Bank has responded by delaying planned interest-rate cuts, and analysts warn the cost will be passed onto households through renewed inflation and austerity.

The shock lands on populations already squeezed by the post-2022 energy crisis and by the diversion of state budgets toward rearmament. The class logic is stark: military escalation in the Persian Gulf translates directly into higher heating and electricity bills for European working families this winter.

Asian currencies tumble as Korean won nears crisis-era lows

The day’s most consequential financial story remained the growing instability across Asian markets. The South Korean won slid toward its weakest level since the 2009 global financial crisis — a 17-year low — becoming Asia’s worst-performing currency in the first half of 2026. The rout exposes the fragility beneath the year’s speculative equity boom: the KOSPI index is up 105 percent year-to-date on AI-chip demand, with Samsung Electronics nearly tripling and SK Hynix up over 250 percent, but foreign investors are now taking profits, selling a net 1.46 trillion won ($938 million) in a single session — the eighth straight day of outflows.

The episode is a textbook illustration of financialized fragility: a rally built on AI-chip mania followed by a currency crash as foreign capital exits, leaving the working population exposed to imported inflation with no say in the process.

ICE arrests hit record pace of ~1,400 per day

Immigration and Customs Enforcement detained 39,500 people in June — a record — and is on pace to exceed 43,000 arrests in July at current rates, an increase of more than 40 percent year-on-year. The intensifying deportation dragnet is unfolding with minimal due process and represents a major escalation of state repression directed against the entire working class, immigrant and citizen alike.

The human cost is mounting. On the WSWS, Tom Hall reported on the wake for Lorenzo Salgado Araujo, killed by ICE in Houston, describing mourners lined up around the building and grief expressed in Spanish and English. “We’re not animals,” one mourner said. Hall situated the killing within a nationwide pattern of ICE violence.

Brigham and Women’s Hospital locks out over 4,000 nurses

Management at Boston’s Brigham and Women’s Hospital has locked out more than 4,000 nurses organized with the Massachusetts Nurses Association following a one-day strike on July 8 over wages and healthcare costs. The punitive lockout — using patient care as a bargaining lever against the very workers who provide it — is emblematic of the broader employer offensive against healthcare workers nationally.

The lockout underscores the readiness of hospital management to escalate against organized nurses even amid a national staffing crisis, and raises the question of coordinated action across the sector.

Bangladesh measles death toll climbs past 766

Seven more children died from measles in the 24 hours before July 19, pushing the confirmed and suspected death toll to 766. Since March 2026, over 101,000 suspected cases and 6,258 deaths have been reported, with 79 percent of cases among children under five. National vaccination coverage fell from 83.9 percent in 2019 to 81.6 percent in 2023 — a decline health officials link directly to the crisis — while malnutrition and low breastfeeding rates compound the toll. The outbreak is a preventable-disease catastrophe rooted in collapsing public-health infrastructure and social inequality, and it has received little sustained international coverage relative to its scale.

Public health and outbreaks

  • Cyclosporiasis (US): On the WSWS, Evan Blake reported that federal health officials identified Taylor Farms as the source behind the worst US cyclosporiasis outbreak on record but left disclosure and recall decisions to the company itself — a case study in regulatory capture prioritizing corporate profit over public health. Thousands have been poisoned.
  • Wildfire smoke: Shannon Jones (WSWS) documented that production continued at full capacity in US factories and worksites blanketed by hazardous wildfire smoke, with workers collapsing from heat and contaminated air. Fine particulate (PM2.5) exposure at these levels is associated with elevated cardiovascular and respiratory mortality; the trade-union bureaucracies offered no resistance to management’s insistence on maintaining output.

Technology and AI policy

The launch of China’s World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization (WAICO) with 29 founding members (covered above) marks a significant development in the geopolitics of AI, positioning Beijing as the sponsor of a multilateral bloc against US efforts to control advanced chips and models. The move sharpens the US-China technology conflict and raises the stakes of export controls on high-end semiconductors. The proposed $10 billion Shanghai IPO of chipmaker CXMT signals Beijing’s drive to build a domestic memory-chip champion insulated from US sanctions.

Arts and culture

The most significant cultural development of the past 24 hours was the resignation of PEN America’s president over the organization’s publication of an article blaming discrimination against Israeli and Jewish writers on anti-genocide cultural boycotts. As Sandy English documented on the WSWS, the resignation is part of a broader capitulation of literary institutions to pro-Israel pressure — the same dynamic that fractured PEN America’s 2024 World Voices Festival when dozens of writers withdrew over the organization’s stance on Gaza. The episode reveals how ostensibly independent cultural bodies are pressed into service policing dissent, recasting principled opposition to genocide as a form of discrimination. For a body founded to defend free expression, blaming boycott movements for a climate of intimidation inverts the actual relationship between state power and the writers being silenced.

A second front of cultural politics opened around the England–Argentina FIFA World Cup match. In a statement for the WSWS, Andrea Lobo condemned the exploitation of the fixture by political establishments and media in both countries to whip up nationalist chauvinism — reviving Malvinas/Falklands jingoism and imperial nostalgia in England — and called on workers internationally to reject this reactionary framing in favor of class unity. Sport, one of the most widely shared forms of popular culture, is here weaponized as an instrument of national mobilization, a reminder that the culture industry does not stand above the drive toward war but is enlisted within it.

Roundup: - Literary institutions: The PEN America resignation follows a pattern of arts organizations, universities and publishers narrowing the space for anti-war expression under political and donor pressure — a form of soft censorship operating through institutional self-policing rather than open state bans. - Culture and war: Both the PEN America affair and the World Cup nationalism controversy illustrate how the cultural sphere is being reorganized around the imperatives of militarism — pressure to conform on Gaza and the manufacture of national antagonism through mass spectacle. - A note on method: The WSWS framework insists that genuine artistic achievement be recognized regardless of an artist’s politics, while nationalist, militarist and identitarian tendencies in cultural production are named for what they are. The task is to defend the autonomy of art and free expression against both state repression and the identity-politics framing that recasts opposition to war as bigotry.

The through-line across the day’s cultural news is the mounting pressure on artists, writers and cultural institutions to fall into line behind war and censorship — and the resistance, exemplified by the PEN America resignation, of those who refuse.

What the WSWS should cover today

1. Mass protests across Israel against Netanyahu and the Haredi draft law

Thousands demonstrated at Habima Square and nationwide against Netanyahu’s coalition after the passage of judicial-overhaul and ultra-Orthodox draft-exemption legislation. The WSWS covers Israeli militarism but has not addressed the internal political crisis and mass opposition within Israel — a significant gap given the potential for the war regime to fracture from within.

Recommended reading Haaretz Thousands protest Netanyahu govt, Haredi draft-evasion laws across Israel

2. Bangladesh measles catastrophe: 766 dead as vaccination coverage collapses

A preventable-disease death toll approaching 800 — overwhelmingly children under five — reflects declining vaccination coverage and malnutrition, a global-health-inequality story that has received little sustained coverage relative to its scale. It connects directly to the WSWS’s ongoing exposure of the collapse of public health under capitalism.

Recommended reading NPR Measles kills more than 500 children in Bangladesh

3. Brigham and Women’s Hospital locks out 4,000 nurses

Boston hospital management locked out over 4,000 MNA nurses after a one-day strike over wages and healthcare costs — a punitive employer offensive that the WSWS’s coverage of the healthcare workforce and the union bureaucracy’s isolation of struggles should address.

Recommended reading OnLabor News & Commentary: July 10, 2026

4. South Korea’s AI stock bubble and won crash expose financialized fragility

The KOSPI’s 105 percent AI-driven rally followed by a currency rout to near-2009 lows illustrates how speculative financialization tied to the AI boom leaves working populations exposed to sudden capital flight — a concrete case study complementing the WSWS coverage of the Hyundai strike and global financial instability.

Recommended reading Axios How South Korea’s stock boom sparked a currency crisis

5. European energy shock: Hormuz and Qatari LNG suspension hit workers

Dutch TTF gas has nearly doubled to over €60/MWh, prompting the ECB to delay rate cuts — a second major energy shock for European workers in under five years, directly linking the Persian Gulf war to austerity and inflation at home.

Recommended reading Allianz Economic outlook 2026-27: The Fog of War

6. Venezuela earthquake toll surpasses 3,600 amid sanctions-crippled response

The death toll from the June 24 twin earthquakes has climbed above 3,600, with the real figure believed far higher, in a country where 18.2 million people already face humanitarian need. US sanctions have gutted the capacity to respond — a dimension the WSWS’s coverage of US intervention in Latin America should foreground.

Recommended reading Global Centre for R2P Atrocity Alert No. 490: Sudan, Lebanon and Venezuela
