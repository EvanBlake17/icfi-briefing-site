1. US launches seventh straight night of strikes on Iran

The United States carried out a seventh consecutive night of air and naval strikes on Iran on July 17, with the campaign now openly extended to civilian infrastructure. CENTCOM said it hit “surveillance sites, military logistics infrastructure, underground weapons storage, and maritime capabilities,” but Iranian officials and multiple wire services report that bridges, a railway station, an airport, water facilities, food silos, and residential neighborhoods in the country’s south have been struck.

Iran’s Health Ministry says at least 38 people have been killed and more than 400 wounded over the past week, with over 30 civilians among the recent dead in the south. Iran responded with renewed missile and drone attacks on US Gulf allies, and the UAE reported Iranian missiles hitting two tankers in Omani waters. A US naval blockade of Iranian ports remains in place and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has fallen sharply. Writing on the WSWS, Andre Damon characterized the expansion to civilian targets as “war crimes” and noted that ground operations are now under discussion.

Key facts: - Seventh consecutive night of strikes began at 19:00 GMT July 17 (Al Jazeera, NPR) - CENTCOM used “fighter aircraft, aerial drones and warships” (Al Jazeera) - 38 killed, 400+ wounded over the week; 8 killed and 20 wounded overnight, per Iranian state media - Iran renewed attacks on Gulf states; UAE reported two tankers hit in Omani waters (US News, Fox) - A poll cited by WSWS shows 57% of Americans oppose the war

2. Trump uses prime-time address to lay basis for overturning midterm elections

President Trump delivered a prime-time White House address the evening of July 16 in which he declassified documents purporting to show foreign interference and fraud in US elections, and ordered the Justice Department to identify and prosecute officials he accuses of concealing it. Trump claimed China accessed voter records in several states and stole files on 220 million Americans, alleged voting systems could be compromised by Russia and North Korea, and pushed for passage of his SAVE America Act — legislation requiring proof of citizenship to register and photo ID to vote, effectively ending mail voting. Senate Majority Leader John Thune has repeatedly said the bill lacks the votes to pass.

The WSWS Perspective by Jacob Crosse called the “manic and at times barely coherent” speech an effort to prepare the suppression or overturning of the 2026 midterms, delivered by “a ruling class in crisis.” Crosse noted Trump denounced China even as his administration prepares a September summit with Xi Jinping, and cited reports that Trump, his family and associates pocketed more than $1.4 billion from cryptocurrency ventures in a single year. Democratic leaders responded with insults rather than resistance: Schumer called Trump “weak and flailing,” Jeffries “feeble” and “pathetic,” and Newsom dismissed the speech as “make believe.”

Key facts: - Trump ordered DOJ, FBI and intelligence agencies to prosecute officials allegedly hiding fraud (ABC, NPR) - SAVE America Act would mandate proof-of-citizenship registration and photo ID, ending mail voting (CNN, The Hill) - Trump threatened the broadcast licenses of ABC and NBC for declining to interrupt programming - Cited a fabricated figure of 278,000 non-citizens allegedly on voter rolls (WSWS)

3. Rubio and Miller rally 67 nations against “far-left terrorism”

Secretary of State Marco Rubio convened a State Department “Ministerial on the Resurgence of Political Terrorism” on July 16, drawing representatives from 67 countries to build an international front against what the administration calls “far-left” and “radical leftism” violence. Rubio, White House Homeland Security Advisor Stephen Miller, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, FBI Director Kash Patel and Education Secretary Linda McMahon addressed the gathering. Miller labeled leftists a “cancer” the world must “root out,” language multiple critics described as fascistic.

The conference showcased Trump’s National Security Presidential Memorandum (NSPM-7), which directs law enforcement and intelligence agencies to “disrupt, identify, defund, debank, arrest, and prosecute” alleged left-wing terrorists. Former officials and terrorism researchers quoted by the Washington Post and NBC noted that documented incidents of far-left violence in the US are minimal compared to far-right violence, and that the initiative amounts to the politicization of counterterrorism power against domestic opposition. The WSWS Perspective tied the speeches directly to Trump’s election address as part of a coordinated lurch toward dictatorship.

4. Worst cyclosporiasis outbreak in US history linked to Taco Bell lettuce

The CDC and FDA confirmed that shredded iceberg lettuce served at Taco Bell restaurants is the source of a rapidly expanding cyclosporiasis outbreak, with Taylor Farms de Mexico identified as the single supplier. As of this week there were 1,645 confirmed cases and 141 hospitalizations across 34 states, with more than 5,100 additional suspected illnesses under investigation — putting 2026 on track to be the worst year for the parasite on record. Taco Bell said it removed Taylor Farms lettuce from its supply chain, and Taylor Farms announced a voluntary recall of all iceberg lettuce sourced from central Mexico.

In a WSWS investigative piece, Evan Blake put the combined confirmed-plus-suspected total at nearly 7,000 cases — with 5,002 in Michigan alone — and argued the scale of the outbreak reflects the Trump administration’s dismantling of CDC surveillance infrastructure. Blake called for the expropriation of agribusiness monopolies under democratic workers’ control.

Key facts: - 1,645 confirmed cases, 141 hospitalizations, plus 5,100+ suspected illnesses (CDC, Washington Post, STAT) - Taylor Farms de Mexico named as the sole lettuce supplier to affected Taco Bell locations (CNN, CBS) - Initial state cluster: Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, West Virginia (CDC) - Both Taylor Farms and Taco Bell have prior outbreak histories (Marler Blog)

5. Two new ICE killings spark nationwide protests

Nationwide protests and vigils continued this week over two fatal ICE shootings: Lorenzo Salgado Araujo, a 35-year resident and father of three US citizens killed in Houston on July 7, and Joan Sebastián (Johan Sebastian) Guerrero, killed in Biddeford, Maine on July 13. Both men were reportedly not the targets of the operations. ICE claimed Salgado “weaponized his vehicle” and DHS said Guerrero was shot over “public safety” concerns as he fled — accounts disputed by eyewitnesses. Hundreds attended a Houston viewing for Salgado; more than 100 gathered at Philadelphia City Hall for a vigil.

Nearly 200 members of Congress, led by Representatives Pramila Jayapal, Sylvia Garcia, Chellie Pingree, Jamie Raskin and Bennie Thompson, demanded independent investigations and a halt to removal proceedings against witnesses. ICE said it is pausing most traffic stops and expanding body-camera use. WSWS reporter Tom Hall noted that an FBI claim that Salgado carried drugs was debunked — the substance was salt for hydration — and cited a new HRW/ACLU report documenting abuse at Fort Bliss detention.

6. Canadian wildfire smoke blankets 120 million across Midwest and Northeast

Massive plumes of Canadian wildfire smoke pushed dangerous air quality over more than 120 million people across the US Midwest and Northeast this week, with more than 100 million in 18 states and DC under air quality alerts. “Very unhealthy” and “hazardous” readings stretched from northeast Minnesota to southeast Virginia, with the worst conditions in Chicago, Detroit, Milwaukee, Toledo and Toronto. More than 830 wildfires are burning across Canada, and stateside fires have consumed over 3.6 million acres this year.

WSWS reporter Shannon Jones reported that the smoke engulfed factories and workplaces across two dozen US states and six Canadian provinces, forcing workers to labor in hazardous conditions. She criticized UAW President Shawn Fain for a delayed and weak response, and highlighted contract “act of God” clauses that penalize workers who leave unsafe sites — noting rank-and-file candidate Will Lehman raised the issue directly.

Hamas dissolves Gaza governing body under Board of Peace

Hamas announced the dissolution of the emergency committee that has run Gaza since October 2023, transferring administrative authority to the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG) — 15 unelected Palestinian technocrats chaired by Ali Shaath who report to Nikolay Mladenov, director-general of the US-founded “Board of Peace.” The move is widely seen as largely symbolic: NCAG members have been unable to enter Gaza amid Israeli objections, and the announcement makes no mention of Hamas disarmament, a core Israeli demand. Left Voice framed the step as a capitulation to imperialist control brokered by regional bourgeois states.

Roughly 3,000 demonstrators protested at Rheinmetall’s Berlin-Wedding facility over German rearmament and the arms manufacturer’s contracts tied to Israel. Police physically detained 39 activists, including Greta Thunberg, after protesters glued themselves to the ground outside the company’s offices. “We have the duty to end our complicity in genocide and mass killings,” Thunberg said. WSWS correspondent Stefan Steinberg reported the aggressive police tactics and argued that militarization serves capitalist profit at the expense of social spending.

Hyundai autoworkers strike in South Korea

Members of the Korean Metal Workers’ Union (KMWU) at Hyundai Motor held partial strikes July 13-15, downing tools for two hours per shift after wage talks collapsed. The 39,668-member union demanded a 149,600-won monthly base-pay increase and performance pay equal to 30% of last year’s net profit; Hyundai offered an 89,000-won raise plus bonuses and shares. The KMWU announced further four-hour walkouts July 20-22. WSWS’s Ben McGrath argued the KCTU and KMWU function to contain rather than advance the struggle, amid 3.2% inflation and falling real wages.

New Zealand posts worst real-wage fall in the OECD

The OECD Employment Outlook found New Zealand’s real wages fell 6.4% between early 2021 and Q1 2026 — the steepest decline of all 38 OECD members, worse than Italy (-6.1%), Czechia (-5.8%) and Australia (-5.1%). The OECD said real wages sit near the trough of the cost-of-living crisis only in New Zealand and Australia. WSWS’s Tom Peters linked the decline to rising unemployment (5.3%; youth 17.3%) and a jump in the country’s billionaires from 18 to 26, blaming both Labour and National governments. Notably, several New Zealand economists have since argued the OECD used the wrong data series and that the true figure is closer to middle-of-the-pack.

Trump threatened the broadcast licenses of ABC and NBC after both networks declined to interrupt regular programming to carry his prime-time election address in full. Measles cases climb past 2,260 as outbreaks multiply

The CDC reported 2,260 confirmed measles cases in the US in 2026 as of July 16, with 34 new outbreaks and 93% of confirmed cases (2,108 of 2,260) tied to outbreaks — a marker of sustained community transmission fueled by falling vaccination rates and the gutting of public-health infrastructure. The surge, alongside the record cyclosporiasis outbreak covered above, illustrates the systemic erosion of disease surveillance under the current administration.

H5N1 bird flu remains occupational as CDC streamlines reporting

The CDC’s latest situation summary says A(H5) bird flu remains widespread in wild birds and continues to cause outbreaks in poultry and US dairy cattle, with sporadic human cases confined to dairy and poultry workers. The agency reports no indicators of unusual flu activity in people and rates public risk as low — but CIDRAP notes the CDC has moved to “streamline” (i.e., reduce the frequency of) its H5N1 reporting, raising concerns about surveillance blind spots as the virus continues circulating in mammals.

Research and technology roundup: - Glioblastoma immunotherapy: A study in Nature on “dual tumour–myeloid targeting of glioblastoma with GPNMB CAR-T cells” reports advances in engineering CAR-T therapy against one of the deadliest brain cancers. - Soil carbon: The world’s longest-running soil-warming experiment found that warming prompts microbes to break down stable soil carbon once thought inert — a potential feedback that could accelerate climate change. - Koala genomics: A major DNA study rewrote the koala’s evolutionary history, revealing a dramatic population collapse ~100,000 years ago, long before human arrival in Australia. - AI and markets: Chinese startup Moonshot AI released an advanced model rivaling leading US systems, triggering a global semiconductor sell-off (see World economy) and intensifying the US-China tech rivalry even as Washington prepares a September Trump-Xi summit. - Public health infrastructure: The cyclosporiasis and measles surges both point to the consequences of dismantled CDC surveillance capacity, a recurring theme in WSWS coverage of the administration’s assault on public health.

Arts and culture

The resignation of novelist Dinaw Mengestu as president of PEN America is the week’s most politically charged cultural development. Mengestu, an Ethiopian-American writer and Bard College professor who took the post only in December 2025, quit over PEN’s report “A Silent Moratorium,” which warned of the “rising isolation and exclusion” of Israeli and Jewish writers. He charged the organization with an “ongoing failure to defend free expression fairly and equitably” and with “production of work that supports suppression through bigotry and indifference,” arguing PEN had failed to defend the free-speech rights of writers participating in the cultural boycott of Israel. “Boycott is a form of dialogue,” he told The Atlantic. Board EVP Tracy Higgins will serve as interim president, and authors including Viet Thanh Nguyen and Sarah Schulman backed the resignation. Writing on the WSWS, Sandy English characterized PEN America as a “pro-imperialist appendage of the US State Department,” situating the episode within the broader use of “antisemitism” charges to suppress opposition to the Gaza genocide.

The Argentina-England World Cup semifinal (covered under International) has also become a cultural flashpoint, with the “Las Malvinas son argentinas” banner and the intervention of Milei and Starmer showing how nationalism is mobilized through mass sport — a phenomenon the WSWS analyzes as a diversion from class struggle rather than as harmless spectacle.

Notable deaths of cultural figures in recent days: - Sam Neill, the New Zealand actor best known for Jurassic Park, The Piano and The Hunt for Red October, died July 13 at age 78. A versatile performer across four decades, his work ranged from prestige art-house cinema to blockbuster franchises. - Bonnie Tyler, the Welsh singer behind “Total Eclipse of the Heart” and “Holding Out for a Hero,” died July 8 at 75 following surgery in Portugal. Her raspy, powerful voice defined a strand of 1970s–80s pop-rock. - Hal Williams, the American actor known for Sanford and Son and 227, died July 15 at 91. - Kjell Nilsson, the Swedish actor who played Lord Humungus in Mad Max 2, died July 2 at 76. - Barry Dickins, the Australian author, playwright and journalist, died July 13 at 76.

Pseudo-left press review

  • Jacobin / DSA — credits socialist electoral gains to “old-fashioned” campaign mechanics inside bourgeois channels; no independent working-class perspective
  • Left Voice — polemicizes leftward against Jacobin over the Malvinas, but tails Argentine nationalism in framing the claim as anti-colonial
  • Liberation News / PSL — names Democrats and Republicans as jointly responsible for ICE, avoiding electoral channeling
  • Socialist Alternative — uses the heatwave to demand socialist planning, but its “nationalize and plan” program remains within a left-reformist framework

Jacobin / DSA

Jacobin’s lead piece, “Socialists Are Winning Using Old-Fashioned Democratic Means,” credits recent socialist electoral wins to door-knocking and superior campaign organizing. The framing is straightforwardly electoralist: socialism is presented as achievable through better retail politics inside the existing bourgeois-electoral system, with no reference to the independent political mobilization of the working class against the capitalist state. A second article, “America’s Only Successful Coup Served the Ruling Class,” uses the 1898 Wilmington coup as the “real” precedent for measuring January 6 — a moral-historical analogy that implicitly relativizes the current fascist danger rather than analyzing the class role of the state. A third, “The Americanization of the World Cup Is Here to Stay,” offers pop-culture critique of Trump/FIFA commercialism that gestures at US imperial arrogance but never rises to systematic anti-imperialism. Overall orientation: Jacobin remains the clearest advocate of channeling social opposition into the Democratic Party’s orbit.

Left Voice

Left Voice published “Betrayed by its Allies, Hamas Accepts Imperialist Control of Gaza,” framing Hamas’s dissolution of its governing committee as a capitulation to imperialism brokered by regional bourgeois states; the group refuses political support to Hamas while opposing imperialist domination and calling for independent mass action — a comparatively principled formulation. Its second piece, “The Malvinas Debate and the World Cup: A Marxist Response to Bhaskar Sunkara,” is a direct polemic against Jacobin’s editor, arguing he reduces the Malvinas claim to the 1982 dictatorship rather than seeing it as rooted in British colonialism. Here Left Voice, while positioning itself left of Jacobin, adapts to Argentine nationalism by treating the territorial claim as a progressive anti-colonial cause — the same accommodation the WSWS’s Andrea Lobo criticizes in the Argentine pseudo-left. Overall orientation: intra-left polemic against reformism combined with a nationalist blind spot.

Liberation News / PSL

The PSL’s most recent output, “ICE terror can be stopped — and restarted — at any moment,” explicitly blames both Democrats and Republicans who funded ICE and calls for working-class direct action rather than electoral remedy — notably declining to route opposition through the Democratic Party. A companion piece indicts federal, state and county governments across party lines for abandoning Central Texas flood survivors. The PSL’s bipartisan-critique framing is a genuine strength relative to Jacobin, but it remains wedded to a campist, Stalinist-derived politics that substitutes protest mobilization and appeals to state agencies for the building of an independent revolutionary party. Overall orientation: militant in rhetoric, but without a program for workers’ political power.

Socialist Alternative

Socialist Alternative’s “Capitalism’s Face-Melting Heatwave Shows The Need For Socialist Planning” uses the European heatwave to argue that climate breakdown is unsolvable under capitalism, calling for nationalizing energy and banks under socialist planning and explicitly rejecting reform and Democratic Party framing. The article’s demands are correct in outline, but the ISA’s perspective consistently reduces “socialism” to a program of nationalizations and planning administered through pressure on existing state and union structures, rather than the conquest of power by the working class through its own organs. Overall orientation: left-reformist maximalism.

SWP (UK) and Socialist Appeal / RCP

Both British tendencies oriented to Andy Burnham’s ascension to the Labour leadership. The SWP’s “Burnham promises change, but can he deliver?” and “Starmer broke one pledge after another” offer skeptical, left-critical framing of Labour as a manager of capitalism, implying real change comes from extra-parliamentary struggle — yet still treat Labour’s internal contest as decisive terrain. Socialist Appeal / RCP (now publishing at communist.red after the IMT rebrand) sharpened this in “Burnham’s ‘Number 10 North’ and the myth behind ‘Manchesterism’” and “The many faces of Andy Burnham,” dismissing his devolution gimmick and attacking union and “left” officials for “sowing illusions” that Burnham represents hope. Both tendencies correctly reject Burnham, but neither breaks from the orientation to Labour as the axis of British working-class politics. No outlet in this scan took an openly campist line or attacked the ICFI/WSWS by name.

1. NSPM-7 and the international conference against “far-left terrorism”

Trump’s National Security Presidential Memorandum directing agencies to “debank, arrest and prosecute” alleged left-wing terrorists — showcased before 67 nations by Rubio and Miller — is a qualitative escalation in the construction of a police-state apparatus targeting the left. The WSWS Perspective referenced the conference but a dedicated analysis of NSPM-7’s legal mechanisms and its international dimension is warranted, given the direct threat to socialist and antiwar organizations.

2. Strait of Hormuz shipping collapse and the global economic shock
