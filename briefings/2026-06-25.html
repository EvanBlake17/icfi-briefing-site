1. Iran ceasefire frays as Tehran rejects new Hormuz shipping routes

The fragile ceasefire ending the 2026 Iran war (Operation Epic Fury, Feb. 28–June 17) came under severe strain on June 25 as Iran declared newly proposed Strait of Hormuz shipping routes “unacceptable and dangerous,” warning vessels against transiting without Iranian clearance. The declaration directly contradicts the US interpretation of the June 17 Islamabad Memorandum, signed by Trump at Versailles following the G7 and countersigned by Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian in Tehran. The 60-day window to negotiate a final settlement is now less than a month from expiry, and the dispute over who controls passage through the world’s most important oil chokepoint has become the central flashpoint that could reignite open war.

The standoff over Hormuz — through which roughly a fifth of global oil trade passes — has direct consequences for global energy markets and, through them, for working people facing surging fuel and food prices worldwide. Iran closed the strait on June 20, citing Israeli truce violations; some 150 freight vessels were stalled during earlier blockades. On June 24, Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz signed an order designating the Central Bank of Iran a terrorist organization, a provocative measure that further inflamed an already brittle truce. The interlocking of the US-Iran ceasefire with the unresolved Israel-Lebanon war means that continued Israeli operations in Lebanon are the specific violations Iran invokes — making a durable settlement in one theater impossible without the other.

Key facts: - The war killed an estimated 3,468–6,000 Iranian military personnel, 39 Israelis, 3,912 Lebanese, and 16 US soldiers; Arab states incurred roughly $120 billion in damages, and Iran faces an estimated $1 trillion in economic losses. - US military costs reached $40 billion by June 21; Trump has requested an additional $87 billion from Congress, pushing the projected total above $127 billion in roughly four months. - The IAEA inspections dispute (see International developments) is a parallel obstacle, as US hawks demand verified denuclearization before any sanctions relief.

The WSWS has covered the war throughout as a US-Israeli imperialist war over regional dominance and control of oil transit routes, in which the enormous human and financial costs are borne by workers in Iran, the Arab states, and the imperialist powers alike, while the oil and defense interests that drove the conflict reap the gains.

2. Ukraine strikes Crimea energy grid and air defenses in major escalation

Ukraine’s Special Operations Forces carried out simultaneous drone operations across occupied Crimea, striking four aircraft hangars at Saky airfield, destroying two S-400 components and two Pantsir-S1 systems near Kerch, and hitting Hvardiiske airfield. Separate strikes on the Simferopol Power Station and Sevastopol’s main electric substation caused outages affecting roughly half of Crimea. The SBU declared that “Russian occupiers are losing control of Crimea’s skies,” and characterized the peninsula as a “zone of constant losses” for Russian forces.

The operations represent the deepest sustained pressure on Russian rear-area infrastructure in months. Russia responded by repositioning air defense systems to protect Moscow and the Kerch Bridge — a redeployment that thins coverage across occupied territory and reflects the increasing strain on Russia’s air-defense network. Moscow’s largest oil refinery is unlikely to resume operations before year-end after earlier drone strikes, per Reuters, and a gas processing plant in Orenburg Oblast was also hit, signaling a Ukrainian strategy of degrading Russia’s energy and military-industrial capacity far behind the front.

At the same time, a significant domestic crisis is emerging inside the Ukrainian military. Ukraine’s State Bureau of Investigation opened a criminal inquiry into the Skelia assault regiment over at least 26 alleged noncombat recruit deaths, alongside reports of inadequate medical care and physical abuse between late 2025 and spring 2026 — a window into the conditions facing conscripted working-class Ukrainians fighting a war that Western governments frame in purely geopolitical terms. The WSWS covers the conflict as a NATO proxy war against Russia, while opposing both the imperialist war drive and the conduct of the Ukrainian state toward its own working class.

3. Mamdani-backed DSA candidates unseat Democratic incumbents in New York

Three candidates endorsed by NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani — the 34-year-old DSA member elected in 2025 — won Democratic congressional primaries in New York, the most consequential test yet of whether the DSA’s electoral movement can reshape the national party from within. Former NYC Comptroller Brad Lander defeated incumbent Rep. Dan Goldman (NY-10) with roughly two-thirds of the vote, the cycle’s highest-profile incumbent defeat. The results were widely interpreted by the bourgeois press as evidence of a rising “socialist” force inside the Democratic Party, even as moderate Democrats prevailed in more competitive seats the same night.

Key facts: - State legislator Claire Valdez won the open NY-7 seat (Rep. Nydia Velázquez retiring); Darializa Avila Chevalier defeated incumbent Rep. Adriano Espaillat in NY-13. - All three are safe Democratic districts, making November wins near-certain. - Establishment Democrats backed by House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries and Gov. Kathy Hochul prevailed in the more contested races.

As Barry Grey noted on the WSWS, the results reflect “genuine working-class radicalization fueled by hatred for Trump and the Democrats’ complicity in his program,” but the DSA functions as a capitalist stabilizer channeling that opposition “into the blind alley of electoral politics and the Democratic Party.” Grey points to Mamdani’s first-term record — supporting NYPD expansion, collaborating with Hochul during a nurses’ strike she broke with scab labor, and running a “DOGE-style” city austerity commission — as evidence of the DSA’s real class character.

4. Hegseth forces out Gen. Donahue as Pentagon purge tops 20 senior officers

Gen. Christopher Donahue — commander of US Army Europe and Africa and all NATO land forces in Europe — will relinquish command July 2 after Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth refused to support extending his tenure. Donahue is historically famous as the last US service member to leave Kabul during the 2021 Afghanistan withdrawal, a widely recognized symbol of that evacuation. Simultaneously, Hegseth is downgrading the Europe-Africa command from a four-star to a three-star position — a structural weakening of US military posture in Europe at the very moment the US is providing combat support to Ukraine.

Key facts: - Hegseth has now fired or pushed out more than 20 senior officers, including Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen. C.Q. Brown and Adm. Lisa Franchetti, the first woman to head naval operations. - The downgrade reduces the seniority and authority of the officer responsible for US land forces across the European theater during an active war. - The purge unfolds as the US simultaneously manages the Iran ceasefire, combat support for Ukraine, and Israel-Lebanon negotiations.

The replacement of experienced commanders with loyalists points to the politicization of the officer corps under Trump and the construction of a chain of command personally beholden to the president — a development with grave implications as Washington wages multiple simultaneous conflicts. The WSWS covers this as part of the broader assault on constitutional norms and the buildup of authoritarian forms of rule within the state apparatus.

5. US appeals court clears nationwide fast-track deportations without hearings

A 2–1 panel of the DC Circuit Court of Appeals reversed a lower-court injunction, allowing the Department of Homeland Security to implement expedited removal nationwide — deporting undocumented migrants without court hearings, no longer only near the border. The two majority judges, Walker and Rao, are both Trump appointees; the dissenter was an Obama appointee. The ruling clears the way for one of the most sweeping expansions of summary deportation authority in US history.

Key facts: - The lower court (Judge Cobb, a Biden appointee) had blocked the expansion in August, finding plaintiffs presented “substantial evidence” the process carried a high risk of error — including evidence of people who had lived in the US for over two years wrongly subjected to expedited removal. - The ACLU condemned the ruling, saying fast-track deportations subject people to “an unfair system” that “undermines the fundamental principle that people receive due process.” - Expedited removal strips migrants of immigration court hearings and the right to legal representation, leaving life-altering decisions to frontline DHS officers.

The decision represents a major erosion of due process rights, advancing the administration’s mass-deportation program by removing judicial review from the overwhelming majority of removal cases. The WSWS covers the attacks on immigrant rights and due process as part of a broader assault on democratic rights that ultimately threatens the entire working class.

6. Türkiye launches mass dragnet ahead of Ankara NATO summit

Ahead of the 36th NATO Summit scheduled for Ankara on July 7–8, the Erdoğan government has declared a de facto state of emergency in the capital. The Ankara Governorate banned all demonstrations, press statements, hunger strikes, and leafleting from June 28 to July 10, while 209 people were detained in nationwide raids targeting anti-war activists, journalists, academics, lawyers, and party representatives. The crackdown extended to elected CHP officials, with the Interior Ministry removing the mayors of Adalar and Silifke — Ali Ercan Akpolat and Mustafa Turgut — by decree, without any court ruling.

The removal of Akpolat carries particular historical weight. Büyükada (Prinkipo Island), in the Adalar municipality, is where Leon Trotsky lived in exile from 1929 to 1933, writing My Life and The History of the Russian Revolution and issuing the 1933 call founding the Fourth International. Since 2023 the WSWS has collaborated with Akpolat on annual international commemorations of Trotsky and on a project to transform his house into an international cultural center — a project the repression now directly threatens. The Socialist Equality Party (SGP) in Germany held a rally outside the Turkish Embassy in Berlin demanding Akpolat’s release; the embassy refused to accept the SGP’s open letter, a refusal that the WSWS Perspective calls confirmation of the “political nature of the operations.”

Key facts: - The dragnet follows İmamoğlu’s March 2025 arrest — which came as the Istanbul mayor pulled ahead of Erdoğan in polls — and last month’s removal of the CHP’s national elected leadership under Özgür Özel. - Erdoğan prepares to host Trump and other NATO heads of state while brokering the flow of Azerbaijani oil to Israel and participating in Trump’s “Board of Peace” for Gaza. - The WSWS Perspective by Ulaş Sevinç notes that “more than 90 percent of Türkiye’s population oppose the war against Iran and the US bases in the country.”

The crackdown is covered by AP, Reuters, The Guardian, and the New York Times — but without the class analysis, the Trotsky/Büyükada dimension, or the WSWS’s application of the theory of permanent revolution (summarized below in WSWS coverage).

IAEA chief insists Iran nuclear inspections “going to happen”

IAEA Director-General Rafael Mariano Grossi said Wednesday that his inspectors would visit Iranian enrichment sites under the interim US-Iran deal: “This is going to happen.” Iran’s Deputy Foreign Minister immediately rejected the framing, insisting that “these issues will be reviewed and decided only within the framework of a final agreement and as a result of practical action by the other side to end all sanctions.” The IAEA has been blocked from Iran’s enrichment sites since Israel’s strikes began, and the 60-day Islamabad Memorandum framework was intended to create space for negotiations. Iran has not confirmed what level of enrichment it retains post-war. The dispute is the central obstacle to converting the ceasefire into a durable agreement, with US hawks demanding verified denuclearization before any sanctions relief and Tehran refusing access absent guarantees the war is genuinely over.

Israel-Lebanon hold fifth round of peace talks amid Hezbollah dispute

The fifth round of Israel-Lebanon political and military talks ran June 23–25, with the parties discussing a pilot project for the Lebanese Army to control zones as Hezbollah withdraws. On June 19 a US official confirmed Israel and Hezbollah had agreed to a new US-Qatar-Iran-mediated truce, but Hezbollah has rejected prior frameworks and demands full Israeli withdrawal from Lebanon. The 2026 Lebanon war, ongoing since March 2, has killed 3,912 Lebanese and injured 11,873. Israeli troops stationed in Lebanon remain, per diplomatic sources, “caught between US and other parties’ interests” — and the continued operations are precisely the violations Iran cites in the broader ceasefire dispute. Coverage focuses overwhelmingly on the negotiations between states and armed factions, with near-zero attention to the conditions of displaced Lebanese civilians and workers.

United States

Trump cancels housing bill signing, holds it hostage to voter ID demand

Congress passed the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act by overwhelming margins — Senate 85–5, House 358–32 — billed as the largest housing affordability legislation in over 30 years. Hours later, Trump abruptly posted on social media canceling the signing ceremony, conditioning it on Congress also passing the “Save America Act,” a strict federal voter-ID bill. The maneuver creates a new standoff immediately after a rare moment of bipartisan consensus, leveraging a popular bill to extract a measure that would restrict ballot access.

Key facts: - The bill bars corporate investors owning more than 350 single-family rentals from purchasing additional ones, eliminates the manufactured-home chassis requirement (cutting $5,000–$10,000 per unit), creates a small-dollar mortgage program, and offers local permitting incentives. - It was led by Sens. Elizabeth Warren (D-MA) and Tim Scott (R-SC) and Reps. Maxine Waters (D-CA) and French Hill (R-AR). - The 350-home cap exempts existing portfolios — leaving the largest corporate landlords’ holdings intact and providing no rent relief to the tens of millions already paying elevated rents.

A socialist analysis would note that the bill, for all the bipartisan praise, legitimizes corporate landlordism while offering palliatives rather than addressing the housing crisis at its root.

US measles cases hit 2,104 across 41 states as elimination status nears collapse

As of June 18, the CDC reports 2,104 confirmed measles cases in 41 states across 30 separate outbreaks, with three deaths — two unvaccinated children in Texas and one adult in New Mexico — the first US measles deaths since 2015. Approximately 92% of cases involved unvaccinated individuals. The Pan American Health Organization will formally review US measles elimination status in November 2026, and the US is now widely expected to lose the designation it has held since 2000, as MMR vaccination coverage has fallen from 95.2% (2019–20) to 92.5% (2024–25). The collapse is a direct consequence of the anti-vaccine policies promoted at the highest levels of the federal government and the gutting of public health infrastructure.

First human H5N5 bird flu death confirmed; H5N1 dairy-cow mystery solved

NEJM published a correspondence report on a Washington State patient who contracted H5N5 (Eurasian lineage clade 6) and died in November 2025 — the first confirmed human H5N5 fatality globally. Separately, researchers this week identified why H5N1 attacks cows’ udders: the virus’s preferred sialic acid receptors concentrate in mammary tissue rather than the lungs. The mechanistic breakthrough could help predict future interspecies jumps and enable earlier detection, while the H5N5 fatality underscores that multiple H5 subtypes now pose human spillover risk.

Arts and culture

The cultural story of the moment is the improbable global ascent of the Quebec math-rock duo Angine de Poitrine, two anonymous musicians performing as “Khn” and “Klek de Poitrine” in costume. Their December 2025 KEXP YouTube performance accumulated 16 million views in four months, and their track “Fabienk” topped Spotify’s Viral 50: Global in April. Writing on the WSWS, Louis Girard argues their popularity is not novelty but a symptom: their fusion of Turkish, Asian, and Western microtonal math-rock fills a genuine hunger for ambitious, technically demanding music amid what he calls “an immense artistic void” created by profit-driven cultural stagnation and identity-politics restrictions on cross-cultural synthesis. The phenomenon is revealing precisely because the work’s cross-cultural ambition cuts against both market formula and the prevailing insistence that artists remain within ethnic or national “lanes.” Right-wing Quebec nationalist commentators have attacked the duo as “cultural trash imposed by the federal government” — confirming that genuine cosmopolitan synthesis provokes the nationalist reaction.

The episode illustrates a recurring theme in the WSWS’s cultural framework: that mass audiences retain an appetite for serious artistic achievement that the culture industry systematically starves. The same algorithmic viral mechanism that floods listeners with optimized product also, given the chance, propelled an obscure microtonal duo to the top of a global streaming chart — and that audiences seized on the difficult and the unfamiliar is the meaningful datum. The hostile nationalist reception, rather than the streaming numbers, exposes the political stakes: cultural production that crosses national and ethnic boundaries is increasingly cast by the right as a threat to “authentic” national identity.

Roundup: - Gaming and industry: Rockstar Games opened pre-orders for Grand Theft Auto VI (Business Wire, June 24), one of the most anticipated and expensive commercial entertainment products in history. The franchise’s satirical treatment of American capitalism sits in tension with its status as a multibillion-dollar corporate property; the scale of pre-order demand underscores how a handful of conglomerates now dominate interactive culture. - Cultural politics: The threatened Adalar Municipality in Türkiye — home to Trotsky’s Büyükada exile residence, where he wrote My Life and The History of the Russian Revolution — is the site of a WSWS-backed effort to create an international cultural center, now imperiled by the Erdoğan crackdown (see Top stories). The targeting of a site of revolutionary cultural memory is itself a political act against historical consciousness. - Music and the market: That a non-English, microtonal act could top a global chart without major-label backing points to a latent audience for serious music that the commercial apparatus neither anticipates nor cultivates — a recurring gap between mass cultural appetite and what the industry supplies.

The article calls for a Socialist Federation of the Middle East under the leadership of the ICFI and its Turkish section, the Sosyalist Eşitlik Partisi.

Mamdani-backed candidates win NYC congressional primaries: The political issues — Barry Grey

Beyond the results covered above, Grey’s analysis adds that the DSA represents “privileged and better-off layers of the middle class” rather than workers, and frames the entire operation as the channeling of working-class anger back into the Democratic Party at the precise moment that party is complicit in Trump’s program. His programmatic warning — “without an independent movement of the working class against all sections and parties of the ruling class, the initiative will pass to the far right and the fascists” — is the conclusion absent from all bourgeois and pseudo-left coverage of the primary night, and connects directly to the parallel advance of the “CIA Democrats” documented by Patrick Martin (see United States).

Nexteer workers say UAW bargaining committee is installing management-paid loyalists to oversee ratification vote — Jerry White

At Nexteer Automotive in Saginaw, Michigan, roughly 1,700 workers are voting on a fourth tentative agreement after three prior rejections. Rank-and-file workers report that Local 699 bargaining officials replaced the legitimate Election Committee with company-compensated individuals chosen by the bargaining team itself, while contract opponents face retaliation — including the earlier firing of worker Antwiane Sanders for criticizing a UAW rep at a contract rollout. The proposed deal holds starting wages at $19.50/hour, reaching only $27 by 2030 — below real wages at the same facility in 2005. UAW presidential candidate Will Lehman called it “not a legitimate vote” but a “shotgun affair designed to silence opposition.” This is an original WSWS investigation with no corresponding bourgeois coverage; the vote-manipulation allegations are exclusive to WSWS and rank-and-file sources, illustrating the bureaucracy’s methods for suppressing opposition to concessionary contracts.

Minnesota postal worker wins hostile environment case after APWU union abandons him — Matt Rigel

Steven Linell Smith, a Black USPS maintenance mechanic in St. Paul, won a 2025 federal hostile-work-environment case after documenting 32 incidents from 2017–2022 — including racial slurs, stalking, physical threats, a death threat posted on his toolbox, and management cover-up — yet lost his $37.50/hour Level 9 mechanic job anyway after the American Postal Workers Union refused to arbitrate his discrimination claims, called the case “unwinnable,” and later told him he would have to sue the union to get his job back. APWU President Dave Cook sarcastically asked Smith, “What are those civil rights that are being violated?” The article calls for postal workers to demand his reinstatement through the USPS Workers Rank-and-File Committee. Original WSWS investigation.

Australian educators discuss way forward after rejection of Labor-union sellout deal in Victoria — Our correspondents

A June 14 online meeting organized by the Committee for Public Education, bringing together teachers and student teachers from Australia, the US, and New Zealand, preceded the Victorian Education Union’s proposed four-year agreement being rejected 57.7% to 42.3% — the first contract rejection in over four decades — despite the AEU’s anti-democratic voting procedures and censorship of opposition voices. Participants demanded 40% wage increases and maximum class sizes of 15–20, called for rank-and-file committees, and connected local austerity to AUKUS military spending diverting resources from public education.

Australian state governments deliver austerity budgets, led by Labor — Mike Head

New South Wales and Queensland delivered austerity budgets praised by the corporate press for “spending restraint” — NSW limiting growth to 2.7% annually, below inflation and population growth — even as the NSW deficit hit $2.3 billion, more than double December estimates. Health funding increases fall far short of the 9%+ the Queensland Medical Association says is needed to maintain services, while the federal Albanese Labor government is cutting $37.8 billion from the NDIS over four years, stripping support from hundreds of thousands of people with disabilities. Head argues Labor and the union bureaucracy are jointly suppressing worker resistance to present austerity as inevitable.

How the German Left Party is trying to curb the radicalisation of the youth — Peter Schwarz

At the Left Party’s June 10–21 national conference in Potsdam, the party — which doubled membership to 126,000 by attracting young Germans alarmed by the far right, recession, and conscription — focused on containing that radicalization rather than directing it. It passed the “Lex Sachsen-Anhalt” resolution permitting regional CDU cooperation while defeating floor motions against it; new co-chair Luigi Pantisano won only 53% after telling Bild that CDU and AfD policies were “absolutely no different” and then rapidly retracting the statement. Schwarz argues the Left Party functions as a capitalist stabilizer in the 1990s East German PDS tradition, channeling opposition into safe parliamentary channels, with genuine change requiring an independent socialist movement.

3 IYSSE representatives elected to Berlin’s Humboldt University student parliament — Our reporters

The International Youth and Students for Social Equality won 63 votes (4.6%) in the Humboldt University student parliament election, placing all three candidates on a platform opposing the Gaza genocide, the US war against Iran, nuclear war risks, and university militarization — distinguishing itself as “the only slate with a socialist programme” after other groups either stayed silent or backed government war policies. The campaign featured a lecture by WSWS international editorial board chairman David North connecting the 1953 CIA coup against Mossadegh to present US aggression against Iran.

Pseudo-left press review

  • Jacobin/DSA — celebrating the NYC primary sweep as a “socialist” victory entirely inside the Democratic Party ballot line
  • Left Voice — the day’s sharpest class-independent line; anti-repression solidarity with Anasse Kazib and coverage of Bolivia’s state of emergency
  • PSL/Liberation News — campist framing of the US-Iran MOU casting the Islamic Republic as a resistance pole
  • Socialist Alternative — “irreversible crisis” perspective without confronting the Democratic Party trap
  • SWP UK — reform-oriented NHS and climate coverage; strongest on Kenya
  • RCP/IMT (communist.red) — anti-SNP and Brexit retrospectives; residual entrist ambiguity to watch

Jacobin / DSA

Two lead pieces from NYC primary night frame the DSA’s wins as a socialist breakthrough. “The Socialist Future Is Being Written in New York” defines the “socialist movement” by its control of the Democratic ballot line in NYC, with no class-independent framing. “Socialists Won Big in NYC Last Night and Aren’t Done Winning” presents nine of ten DSA-backed Democratic primary wins as proof of a rising socialist political force — the clearest contemporary example of channeling class opposition into bourgeois politics, conflating Democratic primary victories with building working-class power. A third piece, “José Martí Saw American Empire Coming,” is a serviceable historical-educational essay on Cuban anti-imperialism that nonetheless functions as ideological cover for a publication whose live politics are enmeshed in the Democratic Party. Assessment: Jacobin’s output is organized around legitimizing the Democratic Party as the vehicle for socialism — the antithesis of the class-independent perspective, and a direct convergence with the bourgeois press framing of the primaries as a “battle for the Democratic Party.”

Left Voice

Left Voice maintained the most consistent class-independent line of the US tendencies. “On June 28, France Puts Socialist Anasse Kazib and the Movement for Palestine on Trial” is a solidarity call for the French railway worker and NPA-R militant facing “apology for terrorism” charges, tying France’s judicial offensive to broader state attacks on Palestine solidarity organizing. “Rodrigo Paz Declares State of Emergency in Bolivia to Crush Protests” reports the Bolivian government’s emergency powers as ruling-class repression, consistent with its anti-popular-front line and offering no illusions in the Bolivian “left” government. “The Shadow of Minneapolis Hung Over the 2026 Labor Notes Conference” interviews union militants on anti-ICE struggles and situates union bureaucracy and Democratic ties as obstacles to independent action. Assessment: No major flags in these pieces; the underlying Morenoite tradition nonetheless leaves the question of building a genuine revolutionary leadership unresolved.

Liberation News / PSL

No articles were confirmed in the June 24–25 window; the most recent recoverable material reflects the tendency’s orientation. “The U.S.-Iran Memorandum of Understanding and the Limits of U.S. Imperial Power” frames the MOU as evidence of US imperialism’s declining reach — a classic campist orientation in which the adversary of US imperialism is implicitly cast as a progressive resistance pole, with no class analysis of the Iranian theocratic state. “From Abolition to Liberation: Juneteenth and the Fight for Black Liberation Today” frames Black liberation as a democratic demand to be won through coalition rather than a question of working-class power. Assessment: Campism plus national-reformist coalitionism — opposition to imperialism severed from any class-independent program.

Socialist Alternative

“World Perspectives: Trump 2.0 Plunged Into Irreversible Crisis — Step Up The Global Fightback” argues Trump’s second term has passed a point of irreversible crisis and calls for escalating resistance, but is notable for the absence of any critique of the Democrats as the graveyard of social movements; “fightback” is left oriented toward protest and union action without addressing the electoral trap. “Trans Healthcare Under Siege — Fight Texas’ ‘Detransition Clinic’” reports on Texas Children’s Hospital’s forced opening of a “detransition clinic” under settlement with the Texas AG and Trump DOJ, calling for resistance while staying within a social-movement orientation that does not ask why Democratic-governed institutions have failed trans people. Assessment: Crisis rhetoric without a programmatic break from the Democratic Party.

Socialist Worker / SWP (UK)

“Report Details Failures of Maternity Care” reports documented failures at Nottingham University Hospital Trust within a reform-oriented NHS-defense frame, offering no systemic critique beyond “the system failed.” “Soaring Temperatures Put Vulnerable People at Risk” reduces the climate crisis to a demand for “overhaul of public infrastructure” rather than an anti-capitalist program. “‘The State Does Not Care About Us,’ Says Socialist in Kenya” — the strongest of the three — covers protests marking the second anniversary of Kenya’s 2024 uprising and centers Kenyan working-class agency. Assessment: Domestically reformist, internationally stronger.

Socialist Appeal / RCP (communist.red, IMT)

“Former SNP Chief Peter Murrell Imprisoned for Embezzling £400,000” exposes bourgeois-nationalist corruption, consistent with the RCP line that Scottish nationalism is no vehicle for workers’ interests. “Ask The Communist: What Would Socialist Britain Actually Look Like?” sketches a workers’ government emphasizing nationalization and democratic planning — worth watching for residual Corbynite/Labour entrism in the IMT tradition. “10 Years After the Brexit Vote: Where Next for Britain and Europe?” frames the referendum as opening “a new period of instability,” with the IMT’s characteristic refusal to take a clear position on the national-democratic question blurring into centrist paralysis. Assessment: A sharper anti-nationalist edge than most tendencies, but unresolved on Labour and Brexit.

What the WSWS should cover today

1. US requests $87 billion more for Iran war as total military cost passes $127 billion

The Iran war cost the US military $40 billion through June 21, and the administration is seeking an additional $87 billion from Congress — against $120 billion in Arab-state infrastructure damage and an estimated $1 trillion in Iranian economic losses. These costs are borne overwhelmingly by workers, not the oil and defense interests that drove the war, and the funding request deserves direct exposure as the conversion of a war of aggression into a permanent fiscal burden on the working class.

2. World Bank cuts global growth to 2.5% as Hormuz crisis drives energy spike

The World Bank has revised 2026 global growth down to 2.5%, with emerging and developing economies facing their weakest per-capita income growth since the COVID pandemic — the proximate driver being the Iran conflict’s disruption of the strait that carries roughly 20% of global oil. The costs of the war are being transferred to working people worldwide through higher energy prices and slower growth, with the poorest nations hit hardest.

Recommended reading US News Recession 2026: what to watch, how to prepare

3. Ukrainian military abuse inquiry: 26 noncombat recruit deaths probed in Skelia regiment

Ukraine’s State Bureau of Investigation has opened a criminal inquiry into the Skelia assault regiment over at least 26 noncombat recruit deaths, plus reports of inadequate medical care and physical abuse between late 2025 and spring 2026. The scandal exposes the conditions facing conscripted working-class Ukrainians in a war that Western governments frame in purely geopolitical terms, and connects to the WSWS’s ongoing analysis of the NATO proxy war.

Recommended reading Kyiv Independent Ukraine war latest

4. Bolivia declares state of emergency to crush protests

Bolivia’s Rodrigo Paz government has invoked emergency powers against popular protests — a significant democratic-rights development in Latin America largely absent from US wire coverage. A class analysis would cut through both the government’s claims and the pseudo-left’s campist confusion over the Bolivian “left,” clarifying the independent interests of the Bolivian working class.

Recommended reading Left Voice Rodrigo Paz declares state of emergency in Bolivia

5. Housing bill exempts the largest corporate landlords as it bans further purchases

The celebrated bipartisan housing bill bars corporate investors owning more than 350 single-family homes from buying additional ones — but exempts existing holdings, leaving the corporations most responsible for the affordability crisis with their portfolios intact and providing no rent relief to the tens of millions already paying elevated rents. The reform legitimizes corporate landlordism while solving nothing for tenants.

Recommended reading Time What to know about the landmark housing bill

6. US measles cases reach 2,104 as elimination status faces November review

With 2,104 cases across 41 states, three deaths, and MMR coverage down to 92.5%, the US is widely expected to lose the measles elimination status it has held since 2000 when PAHO reviews it in November. The collapse is a direct consequence of the anti-science, anti-public-health policies of the Trump administration and the broader gutting of vaccination infrastructure — a public health catastrophe demanding socialist analysis.
