1. Supertanker hits mine in Hormuz as Houthis fire on Aden airport and Brent tops $109

The Iran war, now in its eighth month, intensified on several fronts over the past 24 hours. Iran’s Revolutionary Guard (IRGC) said Saturday that a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude caught fire after hitting a naval mine while crossing the Strait of Hormuz by an “unauthorised” route. It gave no vessel name or crew status. Trump says the US Navy’s “effective” blockade has stopped every Iranian ship from crossing the strait. In Yemen, the Houthis claimed a fresh missile strike on Aden airport. The Saudi-backed government says two missiles were fired, and Yemen’s transport minister said the attack endangered 151 passengers. Britain’s Foreign Office now advises against “all but essential travel” to Saudi Arabia.

The war is driving the oil market. Brent was just under $70 on the eve of the war in late February and has since risen by more than half (see World economy). Gulf producers exported 13.9 million barrels a day in September, 75 percent of prewar levels, according to the International Energy Agency. In Washington, Bloomberg reports that top Pentagon officials plan to delay an internal report on the US bombing of a school in southern Iran until after the November 3 midterms at the earliest. The strike killed at least 156 people, including 120 children and 26 teachers. Holding the report back shields those responsible for a possible war crime until after the vote.

Key facts: - Shipping: Twelve vessels were attacked in and around the strait between October 5 and 11, the fastest pace since the US imposed its naval blockade of Iranian ports. More ships were hit last week than in all of September. The New York Times counts more than 100 ships struck since the war began. - Saudi Arabia: Saudi officials say nearly two dozen Houthi attacks on Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport have killed 17 people and injured more than 300. A Houthi strike on Aramco’s Dhahran headquarters hit the tower’s top three floors. The CEOs of BP, Exxon, Shell, Chevron and TotalEnergies skipped the WPC Energy Congress in Riyadh. - Regional spread: Trump said on October 13 that “President Erdogan, I think, would do as I ask” if asked to intervene for Saudi Arabia. Turkey signed the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement with Riyadh and Islamabad in August and has never published its text.

2. Zelensky resumes refinery strikes as Trump waives Russia sanctions and calls Kyiv a disgrace

The open break between Washington and Kyiv deepened on Thursday and Friday. Zelensky told the Financial Times that Ukraine will again strike Russian oil refineries: “The block is out. We’re done.” That ended a three-day pause Kyiv had observed at US request after Trump announced, on October 11 and without warning Zelensky, an “energy-sector truce” between Russia and Ukraine. Zelensky said Russia had kept hitting Ukrainian energy infrastructure throughout and had killed more than 100 civilians in the past week. Trump called the decision “a disgrace.” A White House source told Axios: “Trump is really mad. He feels Zelensky is taking the U.S. for granted.” Trump has also signed an executive order suspending, “in the national interest,” the sanctions and tariffs on Russian energy revenues in the Lindsey Graham Act, which he signed only last month and which authorized duties of up to 100 percent on major buyers of Russian oil.

At the EU summit in Brussels, European governments moved to fill the gap. Zelensky put Ukraine’s 2027 war costs at €155 billion and asked for an extra €70 billion ($79 billion) on top of the €90 billion EU loan now being paid out. No decision was taken. Johannes Stern wrote on the WSWS that the summit showed “European powers intensify war drive against Russia,” with Berlin leading the push to continue the war as Washington seeks a deal with Moscow.

Key facts: - US pressure: US officials warned Kyiv that intelligence sharing would stop if the refinery strikes continued. Zelensky says Ukraine is no longer getting US warnings of Russian ballistic-missile launches. He said Starlink restrictions were “not a technical problem, it was the will.” Musk denies his company was responsible. - Diesel deal: Trump eased sanctions in return for Russia putting large volumes of diesel on world markets. Zelensky said the deal “will never be enough to offset the impact of the Strait of Hormuz” and will “put more money straight into Putin’s pocket.” - European positions: European Council President António Costa said “this is no time to withdraw support to Ukraine.” Merz said of the frozen Russian assets: “That is our affair, and we will decide on it.” Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal said: “Last winter was already hell, this one will be once again like hell.” Austria’s Christian Stocker urged caution: “There are so many figures being bandied about.” Le Pen, the frontrunner in the French presidential race, says France “can no longer finance” Ukraine. - Casualties: Ukrainian authorities said Saturday that Russian strikes on Kherson and Zaporizhzhia killed at least 17 people, including an 11-year-old girl. Strikes on Kharkiv killed three and cut power to hundreds of thousands.

3. France braces for student and Yellow Vest convergence as youth revolt spreads

The high school and university movement that began in September is set to merge today with trade unionists and the remains of the 2018–19 Yellow Vest movement. There are more than 600 demonstrations on the organizers’ list. A police source told AFP that nearly 200,000 people could take part, including 40,000 in Paris. Paris police chief Patrice Faure banned the main march through central Paris and all demonstrations near the Élysée, the prime minister’s office and the Champs-Élysées, and talks on an alternative route failed. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said police may again use stun grenades in cases of “extremely serious urban violence.” He had suspended their use after a grenade fired by police blew off a 15-year-old’s hand.

Speaking from Brussels, Macron called for “calm and respect” and said the right to protest goes with “respect for people and property.” Earlier in the week he said the students were being “exploited” by political forces ahead of the April 2027 presidential election. The revolt has spread beyond France. Thousands of students and university workers marched in Buenos Aires on Thursday against President Milei’s education cuts. Youth protests have also hit Greece and Albania (see below). Turkey’s MHP leader Devlet Bahçeli felt compelled to warn Turkish youth: “Don’t be tempted by France!”

Key facts: - Size: 480,000 joined the October 13 day of school protests. More than 6,000 students have been detained since September, according to figures cited by the WSWS and Left Voice. - Politics: Mélenchon joined a Paris demonstration. Le Pen backed the police deployed against the protests. The CGT, which kept its distance from the Yellow Vests in 2018, is now calling for a “yellow-vest-inspired” mobilization against the cost of living.

4. Albania’s Rama blames “foreign hands” as protesters burn government buildings

Riot police fought protesters outside Albania’s education ministry for a fourth straight day on Friday, and thousands marched through Tirana against corruption and conditions in the universities. Over the week, protesters set fire to several government buildings in the capital, including the prime minister’s office and the finance and justice ministries, as well as several bank branches. Police said 160 people were detained Thursday evening. Parliament canceled its session. The unrest has spread to Elbasan, Shkodra and Korça. Police are using water cannon and tear gas, and demonstrators are throwing rocks.

In a Friday video address, Prime Minister Edi Rama said “this is not a student phenomenon” and blamed “foreign hands that do not wish for Albania to be at peace.” He refused to resign, saying it “would be a betrayal of the Albanians who gave me a strong mandate to complete negotiations on European Union membership.”

Key facts: - Origins: The movement began months ago as peaceful demonstrations against the sale of part of Albania’s southern coastline to a luxury resort backed by Jared Kushner, Trump’s son-in-law. - Demands: Rama’s resignation, electoral reform and education reform. Protester Arber Lleshi called the government “our most corrupt government ever.” - Context: Rama won a fourth term last year on a promise of EU membership as early as 2028. - Government line: Rama said the protests had “absolutely nothing to do with the hardships, problems or legitimate aspirations” of the population, though he offered to discuss rising education costs.

5. India detains Cockroach movement founder as voter-purge protests grow

New Delhi police detained Abhijeet Dipke, founder of the youth-based Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), on Friday as he left a house in central Delhi. Witnesses said officers dragged him into a police car. Student activist Neha Bora was detained on her way to Connaught Place, and two other movement leaders were jailed for five days earlier in the week. A senior officer called the detentions “preventive.” Police closed Central Park, set up metal barricades and deployed thousands of extra officers. About 500 protesters still reached Connaught Place in small groups. The CJP posted on X: “This government is so scared of the youth waving national flag.”

The CJP was founded in May amid anger over leaked exam papers and youth unemployment, and it has already forced the resignation of the education minister. Its near-daily October protests demand the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, who oversaw the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of the voter rolls. Some analysts describe this as the worst crisis of Modi’s premiership since 2014.

Key facts: - Scale of the purge: More than 130 million names have been removed from the rolls. The Election Commission says it removed duplicate, deceased and fraudulent entries. - Who is removed: In West Bengal, nearly 9 million names were removed. About 2.7 million were cut as a “logical discrepancy,” and about 65 percent of that group were Muslims. Only 19 tribunals are handling 3.8 million appeals. In Hyderabad, Muslims made up 40.7 percent of entries marked for deletion against about 30 percent of the population. In Bihar, 6.8 million names were removed, and the BJP alliance’s lead grew from 0.03 points in 2020 to nearly 9 points in 2025. - Government response: Defence Minister Rajnath Singh says the protests are a Congress attempt to “seize power” and that youth are “pawns in the politics of anarchy.”

6. US signs $1 billion Apache deal with Israel as Italy investigates Katz over flotilla abuse

US officials signed a deal to supply 12 Apache helicopter gunships to the Israeli Air Force for about $1 billion, the Times of Israel reported. The same day, Israeli strikes killed four Palestinians in Gaza and hospitalized nine. Gaza City resident Mahmoud al-Naqla said his family had less than 10 minutes to leave before their neighborhood was bombed, the third time they had been hit: “We’re like the walking dead.”

In Rome, prosecutors placed Defense Minister Israel Katz, military chief Eyal Zamir and 11 other senior military, police and prison officials under investigation. The probe concerns suspected torture, kidnapping, injury and robbery of Italian activists seized from the Global Sumud Flotilla in 2025 and 2026. It began with National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir. It is now one of several parallel European cases being coordinated through Eurojust.

Key facts: - Flotilla complaints: 50 Italian citizens filed complaints. Activists from the 2026 mission report beatings in the dark, tasers, rubber bullets and at least 15 sexual assaults, including rape. One detainee told Reuters: “We were stripped, thrown to the ground, kicked.” - West Bank: About 80 masked settlers with clubs attacked Palestinian olive pickers in Abu Falah, north of Ramallah. Armed settlers then arrived and opened fire. Washington Post photojournalist Heidi Levine was beaten, clubbed, robbed and kicked, and said she “probably wouldn’t even be here” without her helmet. Netanyahu’s office said “serious questions remain” about who started the violence. - Diplomacy: ACLED says settler violence is at record levels. Britain and France have announced sanctions on goods from illegal settlements, and China’s foreign minister told Israel to halt the “forced establishment” of settlements.

Turkey seizes opposition broadcaster Halk TV and jails journalists

Hundreds of police surrounded Halk TV’s Istanbul offices on Wednesday, cut its live broadcast and arrested nearly a dozen people. The channel is one of Turkey’s most-watched news outlets. Control passed to the state Savings Deposit Insurance Fund (TMSF), which appointed İbrahim Paşalı, a columnist for the pro-government daily Yeni Şafak, as trustee. Paşalı was also installed at the opposition channel TELE1 when it was seized a year ago. The official pretext is a bribery and bid-rigging probe of owner Cafer Mahiroğlu over İzmir municipal tenders, though the prosecutor’s statement does not name Halk TV. The raid came as jailed Istanbul mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu gave his first defense statement in nearly 19 months. He is one of 436 defendants, and prosecutors seek up to 2,430 years against him.

  • Human Rights Watch says Turkey is arbitrarily arresting activists and environmentalists ahead of the COP31 climate summit in Antalya next month.
  • Sözcü editor Mustafa Balcı and journalist Şaban Sevinç were arrested for “misleading information.” İsmail Saymaz and Murat Ağırel are under house arrest with ankle monitors after reporting on the trade minister’s links to a collapsed-fund scandal that froze the savings of more than 450,000 people, mostly workers and retirees.
  • The X account of the Workers’ Party of Turkey (TİP), which has 582,000 followers, was blocked on October 14. So was Halk TV’s account, which has 3 million.
  • Barış Demir noted on the WSWS that the bourgeois opposition, the CHP and Özgür Özel’s New Party, “is incapable of defending democratic rights.”

Brazilian court orders Meta to stop favoring Bolsonaro before runoff

Judge Marco Aurélio Bellizze of Brazil’s Superior Electoral Court ordered Meta on Thursday to give both presidential candidates equal treatment immediately. The ruling bars Meta from cutting the reach of political content, restricting searches or removing posts in ways that favor either side. Meta’s AI moderation must apply identical criteria to both.   • Meta says “any suggestion that Meta favors one side is false.”
  • The runoff is on October 25. Bolsonaro, a far-right senator, outperformed expectations in the first round.

Ethiopia installs interim administration in Tigray after TPLF offensive collapses

Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed announced on Friday that a federal caretaker administration will run Tigray for six months to create conditions for “free and transparent elections.” The Tigray People’s Liberation Front (TPLF) launched a major offensive on September 23. Federal forces drove it back and took its strongholds, including the regional capital Mekelle. Abiy’s office says the TPLF “did not desire peace.”

  • Tigray did not take part in June’s federal elections, which Abiy’s Prosperity Party won by a wide margin.
  • In late September the TPLF formed an alliance with several armed anti-government groups and has moved closer to Eritrea, Ethiopia’s longtime rival.

Cameroon still without a government two months after Biya’s return

President Paul Biya, 93, returned on August 20 after about 10 weeks abroad, which set off rumors that he had died. He has still not named the government he promised on December 31, 2025. The vice presidency, restored in April by Law No. 2026/002, is still empty. A vice president would take over for the rest of the seven-year term if the presidency became vacant, and the law sets no deadline for filling the post. In June, someone delivered a forged decree appointing a vice president to the state broadcaster, and the government had the person detained.

  • BMI (Fitch Solutions) suggests the delay may be a deliberate move by Biya to keep control of the succession.
  • Cameroon faces a separatist war in its English-speaking regions and a Boko Haram–linked insurgency around Lake Chad.

China extends gold buying to a 23rd month

China’s central bank bought about 23 tonnes of gold in September, its 23rd consecutive monthly purchase and the largest in nearly three years. China now holds about 2,410 tonnes, according to the World Gold Council, compared with roughly 8,133 tonnes for the US. Analysts link the buying to Beijing’s drive to reduce its exposure to dollar assets after the West froze some $300 billion of Russian central bank reserves in 2022. Chinese household purchases of bars and coins rose 28.4 percent in the first half of the year as the property and stock markets slumped. The CBO projects US federal debt held by the public will rise from 101 percent of GDP this year to 120 percent in 2036.

Kenya confirms third Ebola case as Congo outbreak reaches eighth province

Kenya confirmed its third case of Bundibugyo virus disease on Friday.
