1. US launches new strikes on Iran after tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz

The United States carried out a fresh wave of airstrikes on Iran on July 7 after three commercial vessels were struck in the Strait of Hormuz, sharply escalating a war that began with the February 28 US-Israeli airstrike campaign. CENTCOM said it hit “over 80” Iranian targets — air defenses, radar sites, anti-ship missile batteries and more than 60 Revolutionary Guard fast-attack boats — describing the operation as retaliation “to impose heavy costs for targeting and attacking commercial shipping crewed by innocent civilians.” No casualty figures have been released.

Speaking in Ankara, Trump pronounced any broader agreement with Tehran dead: “For me, I think it’s over… It’s just a waste of time dealing with them.” The strikes proceeded without new congressional authorization. As Andre Damon noted on the WSWS, the NATO powers gathering in Turkey used the moment to “pledge escalated rearmament and warmaking activities globally.”

Key facts: - Iran’s Revolutionary Guard allegedly struck the Qatari LNG tanker Al-Rekayat (engine-room fire), the Saudi supertanker Wedyan, and a third tanker hit by drone on July 7. - The US Treasury revoked Iran’s oil-sales sanctions waiver in tandem with the strikes. - European allies — reportedly Spain, France and Italy — restricted US access to airspace and bases for the operation, dubbed “Operation Epic Fury.”

2. Iran’s slain Supreme Leader Khamenei buried amid succession uncertainty

Ali Khamenei, killed in the February 28 US-Israeli strike campaign, was given a six-day state funeral (July 4–9), with authorities projecting up to 20 million mourners in Tehran. The succession remains unresolved: his declared heir and son, Mojtaba Khamenei, has not appeared publicly since the strike, and associates say he suffered facial disfigurement and leg injuries.

Key facts: - Khamenei’s three other sons prayed beside his coffin; the new supreme leader’s continued absence has fueled speculation over control of the Iranian state. - Mourners in Najaf, Iraq joined the procession before the burial in Iran, with some crowds reportedly calling for revenge. - The funeral was staged to project strength even as the regime reels from decapitation strikes and now fresh US bombardment.

3. NATO summit in Ankara commits alliance to 5% of GDP on arms amid Iran-war rift

NATO’s Ankara summit formalized a target of 5% of GDP on defense and security spending by 2035 — 3.5% for core defense, 1.5% for related expenditure — with European allies and Canada already near 4%. Tens of billions of dollars in new arms contracts were announced. Trump arrived openly angry that European allies had refused to support his Iran operation, and used the platform to announce he is lifting US sanctions on Turkey.

Andre Damon, writing on the WSWS, characterized the gathering as an assembly of “NATO warmongers” pledging a qualitative escalation of global militarism, financed by the destruction of social spending.

Key facts: - The 5% target represents a historic transfer of social wealth into militarism as European governments plead fiscal constraints on healthcare, pensions and wages. - The summit coincided with the US strikes on Iran, exposing sharp trans-Atlantic divisions over the war.

4. Russia strikes Kyiv with second mass barrage in four days

Russia’s July 6–7 barrage on Kyiv killed at least 26 people, following a July 2 attack that killed 31 and injured 102 using 74 missiles and 496 drones. Kyiv marked a second “Day of Mourning” in a week. Trump again insisted Putin “wants to end the war,” even as the strikes came two days after their phone call.

Key facts: - Overnight July 6–7, Russia launched 123 drones; Ukraine said it downed 108. - Russian attacks nationwide killed 7 and injured 88 over the past day. - Ukraine signed new drone-cooperation deals with Estonia and the Netherlands, deepening the NATO-Russia proxy war.

Sources Al Jazeera Russian attacks on Ukraine on eve of NATO summit · Kyiv Independent Russian attacks kill 7, injure 88 as Kyiv marks Day of Mourning · CNN Kyiv ballistic missile attack

5. Israel continues near-daily killings in Gaza nine months into “ceasefire”

Israeli strikes killed at least 6 Palestinians and wounded 22 this week, including a married couple killed in a drone strike on a Gaza City apartment and two people sheltering in a tent in al-Mawasi. Since the October 10, 2025 ceasefire, Israel has killed at least 1,072 Palestinians and injured 3,463, according to Gaza’s Health Ministry.

Jean Shaoul, writing on the WSWS, documented that Israeli forces are “deliberately targeting children” near the so-called “Yellow Line” boundary — underscoring that the “ceasefire” is a managed continuation of the genocide, not its end.

Key facts: - Israel has attacked Gaza on 244 of the past 271 days — only 27 days without reported violence. - Only 55,539 of 156,000 allocated aid trucks (36%) have entered Gaza since the truce began, sustaining the siege by bureaucratic design.

Sources Al Jazeera Israeli attacks on Gaza kill at least 6 as ceasefire violations continue · CNN Bodies lie unclaimed as Gaza’s ceasefire remains unfulfilled · WSWS Israel’s war on Gaza continues, deliberately targeting children

6. China test-fires submarine-launched ballistic missile into South Pacific

China’s navy fired a long-range ballistic missile with a dummy warhead from a nuclear-powered submarine into the South Pacific Nuclear Free Zone, according to Xinhua — the second such test in the region and a demonstration of Beijing’s expanding second-strike capability. Australian FM Penny Wong called it “destabilising”; NZ FM Winston Peters said Pacific nations have “no interest in China using the South Pacific as a testing site.” Japan lodged “grave concern.”

Peter Symonds on the WSWS characterized the Western reaction as “hypocritical condemnation” from US Pacific allies who are themselves deepening military integration with Washington against China.

Key facts: - The US State Department urged China to pursue “arms control discussions” and a “regularised notification arrangement” for such launches. - The test unfolds amid an intensifying US-China great-power confrontation across the Indo-Pacific.

Sources NPR China ballistic missile in South Pacific · Al Jazeera China missile test draws criticism from Australia, New Zealand, Japan · WSWS Hypocritical condemnation from US Pacific allies over Chinese missile test

7. DR Congo Ebola deaths near 600 as unpaid health workers strike

The Democratic Republic of Congo’s Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak has driven confirmed deaths toward 600. By July 8, cases reached 1,708 with 580 deaths, up from 1,561 cases and 506 deaths on July 4. The WHO says the outbreak’s “true scale has not yet been fully established” and it “cannot say” the situation is stabilizing.

Front-line workers in Ituri — outreach teams, burial teams and security staff — went unofficially on strike after their wages went unpaid. “We spend money on transport to get to work… nothing is going right because we’re not being paid,” one worker said. The episode exposes how underfunded outbreak response is being carried on the backs of unpaid laborers in the Global South.

Bombs explode near Macron’s Damascus hotel during Syria state visit

Two IEDs — one in a car, one in a garbage can — detonated near the Four Seasons hotel in Damascus on July 7, where French President Emmanuel Macron was staying during a state visit. Syria’s interior ministry said the blast site was “outside the security perimeter” for Macron’s residence and detonated during a bomb-disposal operation. Eighteen people were injured, including four police officers; no deaths were reported.

No group has claimed responsibility. The blast followed a July 2 Damascus café bombing that killed at least nine, underscoring the deep instability of post-Assad Syria as Western powers court the new government. Macron posted that “nothing can smother the aspiration of Syrian women and men to live in a fully sovereign, safe, pluralistic, and united Syria.”

Philippine VP Sara Duterte’s impeachment trial resumes amid Senate defection

The Philippine Senate impeachment court resumed the trial of Vice President Sara Duterte on July 7–8, with House prosecutors presenting evidence under Article IV. Charges include misuse of confidential funds, failure to disclose wealth, bribery, and death threats against President Marcos Jr., his wife, and former Speaker Romualdez. Conviction requires 16 affirmative Senate votes.

Duterte’s Senate support was weakened just before the trial when ally Senator Rodante Marcoleta was arrested on plunder charges — a reminder that the proceedings represent a factional struggle between the Duterte and Marcos dynasties rather than genuine accountability. Duterte declared she “will be bloodied but unbowed.”

Massachusetts nurses and home care clinicians launch largest healthcare strike in state history

More than 4,500 nurses and clinicians represented by the Massachusetts Nurses Association struck Mass General Brigham facilities on July 8 — the largest healthcare strike in state history. About 4,000 Brigham and Women’s Hospital nurses held a one-day walkout, after which management intends to lock them out for four additional days; roughly 450 MGB Home Care clinicians began a seven-day strike. The Brigham nurses had authorized action with a 99.6% vote.

The MNA charged that “billionaire” MGB board members refused to negotiate over wages, affordable health insurance, permanent staffing and patient-care protections. As Kate Randall noted on the WSWS, the vote represented the largest strike authorization in state history — a measure of mounting militancy across the healthcare workforce, which the union apparatus is working to contain within a limited legal framework.

Democrats move to force Maine Senate nominee Graham Platner from the race

The Democratic Party moved to eject its Maine Senate nominee, Graham Platner, after Politico published an allegation by Jenny Racicot, 41, that Platner sexually assaulted her in 2021. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called on Platner to “immediately” end his campaign; Bernie Sanders, Elizabeth Warren and the Maine Democratic Party followed within hours. Platner, 41, denied the allegation and did not commit to quitting; under Maine law he must exit by July 13 for the party to name a replacement.

The collapse follows earlier revelations about a Nazi-linked Totenkopf tattoo and Platner’s background as a Blackwater/Constellis contractor. The WSWS Perspective by Jacob Crosse argues Platner’s “working-class” persona was manufactured political theater promoted by Sanders and the DSA to rehabilitate the Democrats’ damaged image while preserving support for imperialism and capitalism.

Manhattan high-rise near-collapse forces evacuation of nine buildings and a school

Two support beams on the 21st floor of the 37-story former Pfizer headquarters at 235 East 42nd Street — under conversion to luxury apartments — began buckling on the morning of July 7. At least nine neighboring buildings were evacuated, including a school with roughly 400 children, according to Mayor Zohran Mamdani. More than 150 FDNY and EMS personnel responded and a “frozen zone” was established between 40th–45th Streets and 1st–3rd Avenues. No injuries were reported, and officials said temporary shoring appeared to be stabilizing the structure. The near-collapse of a speculative luxury-condo conversion raises unexamined questions about contractor pressure and safety oversight.

Science, technology, and public health

  • US measles cases top 2,170 for 2026 — 93% of confirmed cases are outbreak-associated across 31 outbreaks nationally
  • Bundibugyo Ebola treatment trial enrolls first patients — the PARTNERS trial tests monoclonal antibody MBP134 and remdesivir against a strain with no approved vaccine
  • H5N1 bird flu continues sporadic human infections; seasonal flu surveillance and AI-chip developments round out the roundup

The US measles resurgence has reached its worst level in decades. As of July 2, 2026, the CDC reported 2,170 confirmed cases for the year across 31 outbreaks, with 93% of cases outbreak-associated — a direct consequence of collapsing vaccination rates and the gutting of public-health infrastructure. Measles was declared eliminated in the US in 2000; its return reflects the deliberate erosion of childhood immunization programs.

First clinical trial for Bundibugyo Ebola enrolls patients

The PARTNERS clinical trial opened enrolment on July 2 in the DR Congo, the first systematic evaluation of treatments for the Bundibugyo strain — for which no vaccine or approved therapy exists. The trial is testing the monoclonal antibody MBP134 and the antiviral remdesivir, alone and in combination, to assess whether they improve survival. Since the outbreak began, more than 1,400 people have been diagnosed, nearly 210 have recovered, and hundreds have died.

Arts and culture

The WSWS published two significant cultural pieces in the past 24 hours, both engaging directly with the relationship between artistic production and social crisis.

David Walsh’s critique of the New York Times’ list of “definitive” films about America takes aim at the newspaper’s selections as historically shallow and evasive. The WSWS argues that the Times’ curatorial framework — bound up with the outlook of an affluent upper-middle-class layer — is incapable of honestly reckoning with the great questions of American history: slavery, war, class exploitation and revolution. Walsh’s essay defends the principle that film, like all art, must be judged by its capacity to illuminate objective social reality rather than flatter contemporary prejudices. It is a useful corrective to the identity-driven and market-driven criteria that dominate mainstream film criticism.

Erik Schreiber’s review of Everything Is Great! — the collaboration between singer-songwriter Carsie Blanton and the band the Burning Hell — highlights a work that gives voice to genuine popular anger over war, fascism, police violence and exploitation, culminating in a call for “the overthrow of capitalism.” The WSWS treats such works seriously: not as protest branding, but as expressions, however partial, of a radicalizing mood among artists and audiences responding to a world in crisis. The review recognizes artistic sincerity while measuring the work against the depth of the social contradictions it addresses.

Roundup: - The Karlovy Vary International Film Festival (July 3–11) is underway in the Czech Republic, one of Europe’s oldest and most significant film showcases. - The 2026 Tribeca Festival announced competition winners, with top prizes to Cotton Fever (US narrative), Labrador – Autopsy of Silence (international narrative) and Jail Time Records (documentary). - Rustin Thompson’s documentary The Last Picture Shows, which traveled 10,825 miles to capture 123 movie theaters across small-town America, opened in July — an elegiac study of the decay of shared cultural space under decades of economic decline.

These developments unfold against a backdrop of arts defunding, censorship of pro-Palestinian expression, and the subordination of cultural life to corporate streaming consolidation — trends the WSWS has consistently traced to the crisis of capitalist culture.

German government adopts historic war budget — Johannes Stern

The German government has adopted what the WSWS describes as a “historic war budget,” raising defense spending by nearly one-third for 2027 while social services face deep cuts. The article situates the rearmament within the broader NATO drive formalized at Ankara, arguing that the German ruling class is reviving militarism on a scale not seen since the Nazi era, financed through an assault on the social position of the working class. Stern connects the budget to the ICFI’s warning that the return of German militarism is inseparable from the resurgence of the far right and the AfD.

UAW white paper “Trade and the American Dream”: A brief for economic nationalism — Jerry White and Tom Hall

The WSWS dissects a new UAW white paper that attributes US deindustrialization to trade rather than capitalism, providing a “left” cover for the Trump administration’s tariff program and economic nationalism. White and Hall argue the union bureaucracy’s nationalist framework pits American workers against their class brothers and sisters internationally, subordinating them to US imperialism’s trade war against China and Europe. The piece is a significant polemic against the AFL-CIO/UAW alignment with protectionism.

IBEW apparatus shuts down Philadelphia PECO strike — Landon Gourov

The IBEW leadership ended a three-day strike by PECO utility workers in Philadelphia after reaching a limited agreement, despite substantial public support for the walkout. The WSWS frames this as another instance of the union bureaucracy isolating and shutting down struggles before they can develop.

Ford fired worker over a $1.95 cookie. The UAW told him to say sorry — Milo Stevens

An electrician was terminated by Ford over an alleged $1.95 cookie theft while the UAW offered minimal defense, advising him to apologize. The case illustrates the collaboration between the company and the union apparatus against rank-and-file workers.

Keiko Fujimori declared Peru’s president: The historical roots of fujimorism — Francis Portocarrero and Andrea Lobo

Keiko Fujimori has been declared president of Peru. The WSWS analyzes fujimorism as the three-decade organizing axis of Peruvian bourgeois rule, rooted in the 1990s Fujimori dictatorship’s neoliberal shock therapy and repression, and warns of an authoritarian consolidation against the Peruvian working class.

Sri Lanka: Negombo Prison riots leave at least 27 people dead — Wasantha Rupasinghe

Riots at Negombo Prison left at least 27 dead, reflecting extreme overcrowding and inadequate resources in Sri Lanka’s prison system amid the deepening social crisis under the JVP-led government.

National Guard soldiers kill 20-year-old Tyrin Johnson in Memphis — Jacob Crosse

Jacobin / DSA

Jacobin’s lead piece, “DC’s Next Mayor Is a Socialist Ready to Fight Trump,” sympathetically profiles Janeese Lewis George’s Democratic mayoral primary win, implicitly treating the Democratic ballot line as the vehicle for socialist advance. This is the clearest expression among the six tendencies of the DSA’s project of channeling radicalization back into the Democratic Party — the “graveyard of social movements” — rather than toward independent working-class political action. Two further pieces are more critical of the establishment: “Turkey’s Key Role in NATO Is No Contradiction” traces Turkish authoritarianism to NATO membership itself, and “In France, Lawfare Is Used to Silence Pro-Palestine Lawmaker” documents repression of an EU pro-Palestine MP without proposing any remedy beyond publicity. Overall orientation: a left-liberal reformism that fuses genuine anti-imperialist reporting with a strategic dead end inside the Democratic Party.

Left Voice

Left Voice published “New York City’s Rent Freeze: Who Benefits, and What’s Next?” and “Mamdani Halts NYPD Expansion, but NYC’s Repressive Machine Remains Intact,” both arguing that the Democratic Party is structurally incapable of real reform and that Mamdani’s administration cannot deliver on policing or housing. Its “Trump’s World Cup Scandal and the Business of U.S. Imperialism” frames the tournament as imperial image-laundering. Left Voice’s formal rejection of the Democrats is more consistent than most, but its perspective remains largely movementist and “tenant-organizing,” counterposing pressure and independent activism to the fight for a revolutionary party and the ICFI’s program of building rank-and-file committees.

PSL / Liberation News

The PSL’s statement “New York is ready for socialism” announces an independent candidate, Andre Easton, against incumbent Democrat Ritchie Torres over AIPAC funding and Iran-war support — notably running its own candidate rather than endorsing a Democrat, while still crediting DSA-aligned primary wins as evidence of rising socialist sentiment. Its “Solidarity Statement with the Communist Party of Turkey (TKP) on the anti-NATO protests” brands NATO “the largest terrorist organization in the world.” This is the day’s clearest example of campism: the PSL condemns US/NATO imperialism one-sidedly while remaining silent on the reactionary conduct of Russia and China, substituting a bloc with “anti-imperialist” bourgeois and Stalinist forces for an independent working-class internationalism.

Socialist Alternative

Socialist Alternative’s most recent output (dated July 3) includes “Prairieland Convictions: A Warning To The Movement & A Call To Action,” covering draconian 30-to-100-year sentences for anti-ICE protesters prosecuted as “terrorists,” and “Albania’s ‘Flamingo Revolution’ Takes On Trump Family Corruption.” Both reject reliance on either wing of the bourgeois establishment and call for mass mobilization and an independent workers’ party. The anti-lesser-evilism is formally clean, but the perspective substitutes generalized calls for “mass movements” and “political strikes” for the concrete task of building a revolutionary leadership.

SWP (UK)

Socialist Worker’s “State repression will not silence the Palestine movement” and “Marxism Festival debates how to stop international far right” blame Britain’s Labour government for complicity in Gaza and for “authoritarian oppression” of Palestine activists, calling for direct action over electoral remedies. “As Farage triggers by-election, kick out the far right fraudster” demands extra-parliamentary campaigning in Clacton rather than a Labour vote. The SWP’s rhetoric is militant, but its “united front” activism repeatedly dissolves the working class into cross-class protest coalitions.

Socialist Appeal / RCP (IMT)

The rebranded RCP (“The Communist,” communist.red) published “Nigel Farage resigns in political stunt,” “Fight the right! Fight racism! Fight for revolution!” and “Palantir is sinking its claws into the British state.” All three reject “lesser-evil” appeals to vote against Farage, tie Starmer’s Labour to Palantir and Peter Thiel, and call for “overthrow of the system itself.” The sharply anti-reformist posture is used chiefly as a recruitment pitch for the RCP, abstracted from the strategic lessons of the Fourth International and the actual class struggles of the day.
