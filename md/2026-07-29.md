### 1. Iran fires ballistic missiles at US bases; Washington and Riyadh strike Iraq in response

The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps launched multiple ballistic missiles from Iranian territory at US forces in the Middle East at approximately 5:45 p.m. Eastern time on Tuesday. US Central Command said all missiles were intercepted and described the salvo as an "attempted surprise attack." The IRGC said its target was US military positions in Jordan. The attack ended a several-day pause in US bombing that Trump had announced over the weekend, claiming Tehran had requested it and warning that strikes would resume absent a new ceasefire agreement.

Within hours, CENTCOM and the Saudi Arabian Armed Forces conducted joint precision strikes across eastern Iraq against what the US described as Iran-aligned militia logistics and weapons sites. CENTCOM stated the strikes were a response to more than 30 IRGC-directed aerial drone attacks on US forces and Saudi energy infrastructure over the preceding 72 hours. The joint US-Saudi character of the operation is a significant escalation: it formally enlists the Gulf monarchies as co-belligerents in the war on Iran.

Key facts:
- Iran's foreign ministry stated Monday, "We have no negotiations with the United States at present," directly contradicting Trump's account of ongoing talks
- Iranian and Omani deputy foreign ministers met in Tehran on shipping through the Strait of Hormuz; fewer than 10 commodity ships transited the strait Monday morning, against roughly 100 commercial vessels on a typical prewar day
- Tehran has made an Israel–Hezbollah ceasefire in Lebanon a precondition for wider talks
- The strikes on Iraq were carried out by US and Saudi fighter aircraft against "multiple terrorist logistics and weapons sites"

The WSWS has argued that the Iran war and the war against Russia are not separate conflicts but converging theaters of a single imperialist offensive — a thesis reinforced by the simultaneous presence in Washington of the Ukrainian and Israeli heads of government.

### 3. Kospi suffers worst two-day collapse on record as AI chip trade unwinds

South Korea's benchmark index triggered circuit breakers for a second consecutive session Wednesday, falling as much as 8.2% intraday and briefly dropping below 5,300 after a 10.84% plunge on Tuesday. The two-day decline exceeds 18% — the worst two-day drop in the index's history — and the Kospi has now lost more than 30% over the month of July. The trigger was SK Hynix's quarterly report: the company posted another record quarterly profit but missed analyst expectations, and its US-listed shares fell below their IPO price.

The rout is not confined to semiconductors. Chipmakers led the decline — SK Hynix -14.48%, Samsung Electronics -13.58%, SK Square -15.42% — but selling spread across the Korean market to Hyundai Motor (-9.55%), HD Hyundai Heavy Industries (-6.68%), Kia (-6.21%), LG Energy Solution (-5.86%) and KB Financial (-3.88%), indicating a broad withdrawal by retail investors rather than a sectoral rotation.

Key facts:
- SanDisk fell 14.2% in the parallel US session; the Nasdaq Composite closed down about 0.2% at 24,932.08
- Credit default swap prices on technology-sector debt reached record levels
- The Nikkei 225 gave up early gains to close down 1.7% at 61,290.70 Wednesday
- Investor anxiety centers on whether hyperscalers will rein in AI infrastructure capital expenditure ahead of major US tech earnings this week

Writing on the WSWS, Nick Beams identifies the structural mechanism behind the panic: "circular financing," in which chipmakers extend financing to the very customers purchasing their product, so that demand is manufactured by the supplier. He links the unwinding to the emergence of Chinese open-weight AI models that undercut the assumed US monopoly on which the sector's valuations rest.

### 4. Magnitude 6.8 earthquake kills at least 13 in Kumamoto

A shallow earthquake struck Kumamoto Prefecture on Kyushu just before 4:30 p.m. local time Tuesday, roughly 9 miles north of the city of Kumamoto. The US Geological Survey measured it at Mww 6.8 with an epicenter 5 km east of Uto City at a depth of about 6 miles; the Japan Meteorological Agency put the magnitude at MJMA 7.1. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi confirmed Wednesday that at least 13 people had died.

More than 350 people were hospitalized, nearly a dozen fires broke out, and a building partially collapsed. The Aeon shopping mall sustained major damage. Roughly 300,000 residents were ordered or advised to evacuate and 48,170 households lost power. Kumamoto Airport closed. The quake ruptured segments of the 81-km Hinagu Fault and the 64-km Futagawa Fault, with a 27-km section of the Futagawa zone slipping 3.5 meters — the same fault system responsible for the 2016 Kumamoto disaster.

Key facts:
- Hundreds of emergency calls were logged within the first hour
- The event was part of a broader series of tremors across Kyushu on Tuesday afternoon
- Peter Symonds notes on the WSWS that Japan's recurring seismic catastrophes are compounded by decades of subordinating building standards, evacuation infrastructure and nuclear siting to profit — with the 2011 Fukushima disaster, which killed roughly 18,500, the paradigm case

### 5. Temporary Protected Status expires for 300,000 Haitians as DHS guts asylum interviews

Temporary Protected Status for more than 300,000 Haitians lapsed on July 28 following a Supreme Court ruling that cleared the administration to terminate the designation, after lower-court orders had delayed the action until July 27. One day later, the Department of Homeland Security issued a rule permitting asylum officers to refer applications directly into deportation proceedings without conducting an interview — a mechanism that can be applied to as many as 444,724 pending cases plus an estimated 132,000 new applicants annually.

Roughly 200,000 Haitian TPS holders are in the US workforce, including approximately 15,000 agricultural workers and 13,000 nursing assistants. In Indiana alone, some 7,000 Haitian workers who contribute an estimated $209 million a year to the state economy lost work authorization overnight. ICE is reported to be preparing an expanded operational tempo against Haitian communities following the ruling; the agency arrested more people in June than in any month in its history, and July is on pace to exceed that record.

Key facts:
- Haiti remains under conditions of gang control, mass displacement and famine that were the original basis for the designation
- The elimination of the asylum interview removes the last procedural check before removal for hundreds of thousands of pending applicants
- Jacob Crosse writes on the WSWS that the measure constitutes the mass criminalization of an entire national group, and calls for the mobilization of the working class in defense of immigrants independently of the Democratic Party

### 6. Ebola deaths in DR Congo pass 1,400 in fastest-spreading outbreak ever recorded

The Democratic Republic of the Congo reported 3,262 confirmed Ebola cases and 1,437 deaths as of July 27 — an epidemic that has more than doubled in size within a single month. The WHO recorded 1,561 cases and 506 deaths on July 4; by July 15 the cumulative total stood at 2,124 cases and 828 deaths; the death toll passed 1,000 on July 22. The outbreak, caused by the Bundibugyo strain and now extending into Uganda, is the fastest-spreading Ebola epidemic on record.

The joint WHO–Africa CDC response plan is budgeted at $518 million and faces a funding shortfall exceeding $400 million. Health workers in the affected zones have threatened strike action over unpaid wages and inadequate protective equipment. Case detection has risen in part because community trust in treatment centers has improved — meaning the official figures, grim as they are, likely still understate transmission earlier in the outbreak.

Key facts:
- 3,262 confirmed cases and 1,437 deaths as of July 27 (case fatality ratio approximately 44%)
- $518 million response plan, more than $400 million unfunded
- Spread has crossed into Uganda; ECDC is maintaining a joint DRC-Uganda outbreak assessment
- Occurring against the backdrop of the dismantling of US global health financing

### 7. Iberian and French wildfires displace more than 300,000

Wildfires driven by a prolonged European heatwave have forced the evacuation of more than 300,000 people across southwestern France and central Spain, with roughly 250,000 displaced in France, more than 75,000 in Spain and a further 30,000 ordered to shelter in place. The French fire, which began in the Gironde department west of Bordeaux, has burned nearly 104,000 acres. In Spain, roughly 190,000 acres have burned across the Ávila and Toledo areas west of Madrid.

Carlos Novillo, emergency management chief for the Madrid regional government, said the fire near the capital was "at its peak and is currently beyond the capacity of firefighters to contain." Spain has deployed 2,737 personnel, 426 ground vehicles and 21 aircraft, supplemented by crews from Greece, Italy and Portugal. Temperatures were forecast to rise again this week with no rain expected.

Key facts:
- Several sites tied to France's defense and aerospace industry and its nuclear deterrent lie near the Gironde fire perimeter
- Pyrocumulonimbus clouds — fire-generated thunderstorm systems — have formed over both fire complexes
- The scale of evacuation is among the largest weather-driven displacements in modern European history

### India's "Cockroach" movement calls off protests after education minister resigns

The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), the satirical youth formation that has led weeks of nationwide demonstrations against the Modi government, announced it was ending its protest campaign after Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned Saturday. Wasantha Rupasingha and Keith Jones argue on the WSWS that the movement's rapid demobilization on the basis of a token concession was engineered by the opposition parties and the Stalinist CPI(M) and CPI, who worked to keep it inside the framework of establishment politics — leaving the underlying conditions of youth unemployment and privatized education untouched.

## Arts and culture

### Betye Saar, 1926–2026

Betye Saar died in her sleep in Los Angeles on July 26, four days before her hundredth birthday. Born in Los Angeles in 1926, she trained in design and did not turn to fine art until she was 35 — a late start that reflected the near-total exclusion of Black women from the art institutions of mid-century America. Her decisive work came in the aftermath of the assassination of Martin Luther King Jr., when she began constructing assemblage boxes from found objects, family heirlooms and the debris of American racist iconography.

*The Liberation of Aunt Jemima* (1972) remains her best-known piece: the mammy figure of commercial packaging armed with a rifle. It is a work of genuine dialectical force — it does not simply denounce the racist image but seizes it, turns it against its makers, and in doing so registers the revolutionary temper of the period in which it was made. Saar's later assemblages, working with washboards, birdcages and Jim Crow-era memorabilia, sustained an engagement with historical memory that refused both sentimentality and abstraction. She was inducted into the American Academy of Arts and Letters this spring. Her death removes one of the last living links to the Black Arts Movement of the 1960s, a movement whose most serious figures understood their work as bound up with a broader social transformation — a conception now largely displaced in the art market by a depoliticized identity branding that Saar herself publicly criticized.

## Pseudo-left press review

- [Jacobin / DSA](#jacobin-dsa) — two of three lead articles frame the political situation as an internal Democratic Party contest, with Mamdani's New York administration as the progressive counter-model to Trump
- [Left Voice](#left-voice) — the sharpest reporting of the day on Chinese strike activity, coupled with a podcast that relocates the Epstein question from class rule to "patriarchy"
- [Liberation News / PSL](#liberation-news-psl) — calls for workplace walkouts against ICE while simultaneously channeling a Chicago ballot-access fight into intra-Democratic terrain
- [Socialist Alternative](#socialist-alternative) — presents the ministerial resignation in India as a vindication of mass action, without examining who demobilized the movement
- [Socialist Worker / SWP (UK)](#socialist-worker-swp-uk) — Callinicos advances declining US hegemony as the master category of world politics, displacing the class question
- [Socialist Appeal / RCP (IMT)](#socialist-appeal-rcp-imt) — attacks the CPI(M) for reformism from the left while treating Labour as a lesser evil against Reform in Britain

### Jacobin / DSA

**"The Left Is Rising, So Centrist Dems Are Cynically Woke Again"** analyzes the recurrence of identity-based rhetoric among Democratic centrists as an electoral maneuver against insurgent candidates. The framework is entirely internal to the Democratic Party: the question posed is which faction will prevail in primaries, not whether the working class requires a party of its own. The article's implicit premise — that "the left" is rising *within* the Democratic Party — is precisely the illusion that has absorbed and neutralized every insurgent movement of the past decade.

**"Trump and Mamdani Are Running Polar Opposite Bureaucracies"** presents the New York mayor's administrative appointments and management practices as the progressive alternative to Trumpian government. This is national reformism in its administrative variant: the state apparatus is treated as a neutral instrument whose character is determined by who staffs it, rather than as an organ of class rule. Absent from the analysis is any account of how a Democratic mayor is to be insulated from the demands of the financial institutions that dominate the city's budget.

**"'Medics for the People' Turns Health Care Into Organizing"** covers the Belgian Workers' Party's free clinic network as a form of base-building outside electoral channels. It is the most class-oriented of the three, though the PTB's own parliamentary orientation and its support for coalition arrangements with Belgian bourgeois parties go unexamined.

*Assessment:* Two of three lead pieces treat the Democratic Party as the arena of political struggle. The one article about independent working-class organization concerns a party in another country.

### Left Voice

**"China's Upsurging Strike Trend"** documents the rise in strike activity across Chinese manufacturing and delivery sectors under conditions of severe state repression, siding unambiguously with the workers against both the CCP apparatus and private capital, and rejecting any characterization of China as a workers' state. This is the most substantive labor reporting from any tendency this cycle.

**"India's Massive Gen Z 'Cockroach' Protests Show How to Fight Modi and the Right"** presents the student movement as a model for confronting the far right "from below." The article does not address the movement's demobilization by the CPI(M) and the opposition parties — the decisive political question — and thereby converts a defeat into an object lesson in success.

**"#AllThatsLeftPod: The Epstein Files, Capitalism, and Patriarchy"** locates the impunity of the "Epstein class" in the intersection of capitalism and patriarchy. The dual-systems framing dissolves a question of state protection of a ruling-class network into a transhistorical category of gender oppression.

*Assessment:* Genuine strike reporting coexisting with an analytical framework that repeatedly substitutes "patriarchy" and "the right" for the class analysis of the capitalist state.

### Liberation News / PSL

**"Mass action and workplace walkouts are needed to defend TPS holders and defeat ICE's surge"** correctly criticizes the passivity of the mainstream progressive response and calls for workplace action, framing the Haitian crisis as a product of US intervention. The call for walkouts is not accompanied by any explanation of how they are to be organized against the opposition of the AFL-CIO apparatus.

**"The Machine Strikes Back: Illinois Democrats against democracy and socialism"** attacks the Chicago Democratic machine for blocking a socialist alderman's ballot access. The grievance is legitimate; the framework treats the goal as securing a place on the Democratic ballot line rather than building an independent party.

*Assessment:* Militant phraseology on immigration attached to an electoral practice that remains inside the Democratic Party.

### Socialist Alternative

**"Student Protests Force Modi Into Retreat"** hails the Indian education minister's resignation as proof that mass mobilization is superior to parliamentary opposition. The conclusion drawn — that the movement won — obscures that the movement was terminated in exchange for a resignation while every substantive demand on unemployment and education funding went unaddressed.

**"Zohran Says Netanyahu Not Welcome In NYC: Kick Him Out With Mass Action!"** applies critical-support pressure on Mamdani and the DSA, arguing that declarations are insufficient and demanding workplace action against Netanyahu's visit. The tension in the line is characteristic: the tendency is skeptical of Democratic officeholders in practice while orienting its readers to pressuring them.

*Assessment:* Mass action as a pressure tactic on left Democrats, not as the basis for independent political power.

### Socialist Worker / SWP (UK)

**"Trump's war on Iran reaches crisis point"** presents Washington as being forced into climbdown by domestic and regional opposition. **"Credibility of the US empire is shrinking fast"**, Alex Callinicos's column, generalizes this into the proposition that declining American hegemony is now the organizing theme of world politics. The framework substitutes inter-state power balances for class analysis: a declining imperialism is not thereby a less dangerous one, and the SWP's schema offers no account of the international working class as the agent of opposition to war. **"'We are all inspired by India's Cockroach rebellion'"** is a London solidarity report.

*Assessment:* Anti-imperialism conceived as opposition to American power rather than to the capitalist state system as such.

### Socialist Appeal / RCP (IMT)

**"'Be realistic, demand the impossible': RCP Summer Camp sets out the tasks ahead"** reports on cadre training, linking British imperialism to Gaza and campus militarization while stressing extra-parliamentary work — but treating Labour as a lesser evil against Reform UK. Under the Burnham government this "lesser evil" position amounts to political cover for the party now administering austerity and rearmament.

**"'Victory is the fuel, not the finish line!' — Solidarity with the youth of India!"** explicitly attacks the CPI(M) for failing the masses, a sharper class-independence line than any other tendency took on India.

*Assessment:* Correct polemic against Stalinist reformism abroad, combined with adaptation to Labourism at home.

**Cross-tendency notes:** The Indian student movement appeared in Left Voice, Socialist Alternative, SWP and RCP coverage — the single most widely covered story across tendencies. Only the RCP addressed the role of the Stalinist parties in ending it. No direct attacks on the ICFI or WSWS surfaced in today's scan. The convergence with bourgeois press framing is sharpest on India, where the mainstream outlets and most of the pseudo-left both report the resignation as a victory.
