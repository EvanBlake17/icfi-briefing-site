### 1. NATO summit opens in Ankara as Trump presses allies on spending and US drawdown

The 36th NATO summit opened July 7 in Ankara, with all 32 member states in attendance alongside non-members Ukraine (President Volodymyr Zelenskyy) and South Korea (President Lee Jae-myung). Syria's Ahmed al-Sharaa is holding a bilateral meeting with President Donald Trump. The central agenda is the implementation of last year's pledge to raise defense and security spending to 5% of GDP by 2035 — 3.5% on core military outlays and 1.5% on security-related infrastructure.

Trump dismissed Germany's rearmament as "ridiculous," to which Chancellor Friedrich Merz responded that it represents "the greatest effort we have ever made to strengthen our defence capabilities." Two senior US officials said a Pentagon force-posture review "very well may lead" to a reduced US military presence in Europe, pushing more of the war burden onto European treasuries. Zelenskyy is pressing Trump for additional Patriot air-defense batteries as Russian strikes intensify.

The summit is being held under police-state conditions. As the WSWS noted in today's Perspective, the Ankara Governorate imposed a 13-day blanket ban on demonstrations from June 28 to July 10 — precisely bracketing the summit — during which "more than 100 people were unlawfully detained during home raids conducted in 18 provinces." Turkey, which fields NATO's second-largest army, "increased its military spending by 7.2 percent over the previous year and by 94 percent over the past decade."

Key facts:
- The AfD in Germany was the only party at its congress calling for the full 5%-of-GDP military spending target
- The summit is expected to translate spending pledges into concrete new arms-procurement contracts for European defense contractors
- Imprisoned Istanbul mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu, "selected by 15.5 million voters," has been jailed since March 2025

### 2. Russia launches massive missile and drone strike on Kyiv

Russia fired 74 missiles and 496 drones at Ukraine overnight in one of the largest single barrages of the war. Ukrainian air defenses intercepted 48 missiles and 476 drones, but 25 ballistic missiles and 12 drones struck 33 locations across Kyiv, killing at least 22 people and injuring dozens. A nine-story residential building containing 64 apartments was destroyed, and more than 130 sites were damaged citywide, including a publishing warehouse where an estimated 800,000 books were incinerated.

"Russia has once again deliberately attacked ordinary civilians and residential buildings," Zelenskyy said. The strike underscores the human toll of the NATO-Russia proxy war even as territorial lines barely move: Russia's net territorial gain in the first half of 2026 was just 97 square kilometers, according to the Institute for the Study of War.

Key facts:
- Ukraine's military estimates Russia suffered roughly 39,490 casualties in June alone
- Total Russian casualties since the 2022 invasion are estimated at 1.4 million (CSIS, July 1)
- The scale of the barrage came as Zelenskyy travels to Ankara to plead for more air-defense systems

### 3. Hamas moves to dissolve Gaza government as post-ceasefire death toll passes 1,000

Hamas announced it will dissolve its Gaza government and hand administration to a Palestinian technocratic committee under the US-brokered ceasefire framework, a bid to pressure Israel as the plan stalls. The gesture comes amid relentless Israeli violence: strikes killed at least six Palestinians and wounded more than 20 on July 6 alone, including attacks on a displaced-persons' tent and a vehicle in Khan Younis.

Since the October 2025 "ceasefire" began, at least 1,072 Palestinians have been killed and 3,463 wounded by continued Israeli strikes, according to Gaza's Ministry of Health. Residents describe digging cesspits amid latrine shortages and rats infesting aid parcels as the humanitarian catastrophe deepens.

### 4. Israel approves 13 new West Bank settlements in drive to sever Palestinian territory

Israel's security cabinet approved the construction of 13 settlements in the Binyamin regional bloc, beginning with 4–6 settlements plus the legalization of existing outposts for state funding. The plan targets two corridors — northwest of Jerusalem and west of Ramallah along Route 60, and territory extending to the Jordan Valley — engineered to sever Palestinian territorial continuity and isolate East Jerusalem.

Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich declared a "revolution" in settlement expansion. The pace has accelerated sharply: from an average of eight new outposts per year between 2012 and 2022 to 32 in 2023, 62 in 2024, and 86 in 2025, per the Palestinian Centre for Israeli Studies. Palestinian officials called the move "a dangerous escalation" violating international law.

### 5. Amnesty documents RSF crimes against humanity in El Fasher as Sudan fractures

In July, the RSF and allied factions announced a parallel government based in Darfur, deepening the country's fracture toward de facto partition. The conflict continues to draw comparatively little Western media attention relative to Gaza and Ukraine.

### 6. Super Typhoon Bavi devastates Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands

Category 5 Super Typhoon Bavi made landfall on the US Pacific territories with the eye passing directly over Rota (population 1,900) and maximum sustained winds of 180 mph. Local officials reported "major damages," though the full extent remained unclear amid communications failures. Widespread power outages, flash floods, and flipped vehicles were reported across the territory.

The disaster compounds an existing emergency: roughly 1,300 families were still living in tents from an earlier typhoon when Bavi struck. As with Guam, US disaster response in overseas territories is chronically underfunded relative to the mainland — a recurring pattern for the several million people who live under US sovereignty without full political representation.

## Science, technology, and public health

- [Measles cases hit 2,170](#science-technology-and-public-health) — 31 active outbreaks in 2026; 93% of cases outbreak-associated, nearing a post-elimination record
- [NB.1.8.1 "Nimbus" drives summer COVID wave](#science-technology-and-public-health) — Estimated 43% of US cases; 10 states report high wastewater activity, South and West at highest risk
- [World Weather Attribution ties heatwave to climate crisis](#science-technology-and-public-health) — Europe's "most severe ever recorded" heat; ~20,400 estimated excess deaths

**Measles surges toward record.** The CDC updated the 2026 US measles count to 2,170 confirmed cases as of July 2, with 31 outbreaks reported this year and 93% of cases (2,019 of 2,170) outbreak-associated. The figure puts 2026 on track to exceed prior post-elimination records with months remaining, a direct consequence of collapsing vaccination rates and the anti-vaccine policies embedded in federal public health under the current administration.

**COVID-19 summer wave.** The NB.1.8.1 "Nimbus" subvariant — a descendant of the JN.1 Omicron lineage — accounted for an estimated 43% of US cases by late June. It is highly transmissible but does not appear to cause more severe illness than recent strains. National wastewater activity remains low, but 10 states report high or very high levels, including Alabama, California, Florida, Louisiana, and Texas. The CDC's summer outlook flags the South and West as most likely to see a surge, citing lower recent population immunity.

**H5N1 bird flu.** A(H5) remains widespread in wild birds, poultry, and US dairy cattle, with sporadic human cases in dairy and poultry workers. Globally, 12 human H5N1 infections were reported outside the US from August 2025 to June 2026, three of them fatal (Bangladesh, Cambodia, India). The CDC has moved to streamline — critics say weaken — its H5N1 reporting.

**Climate science.** A World Weather Attribution study found this summer's European heatwave the region's "most severe ever recorded" and human-driven climate change "unequivocally to blame," with such heat now tens to hundreds of times more likely than in 2003. Temperature records fell across at least 13 countries; France recorded 44.3°C at Pissos on June 23, its hottest day since measurements began in 1947. A preliminary rapid-modelling study puts the continent-wide toll near 20,400 excess deaths — among Europe's deadliest heat events since 2003. Scientists writing in *Nature* warned that today's extremes are effectively a preview of an ordinary mid-century summer at current emissions.

**Technology.** State-surveillance contractor Palantir is drawing scrutiny in Britain over its expanding role in the NHS and predictive policing, part of the broader fusion of AI firms with the repressive apparatus of the state — a dynamic paralleled in US immigration enforcement data systems.

## Arts and culture

The most politically pointed cultural release of the day is *Everything Is Great!*, a collaborative album by American songwriter Carsie Blanton and Canadian band the Burning Hell, reviewed by Erik Schreiber on the WSWS. The record is praised for its "pointed and amusing" anticapitalist and antifascist songwriting — songs that, unusually for contemporary popular music, openly call for the overthrow of capitalism. Schreiber's review recognizes the genuine artistic and satirical achievement while raising a serious critical question: whether the artists' radicalism is grounded in worked-out socialist theory or in a gentler, more impressionistic sensibility that mistakes moral revulsion at inequality for a program to end it. It is a useful example of the WSWS approach — taking cultural work seriously as a reflection of social reality rather than as lifestyle content, and measuring its politics against the requirements of a conscious movement.

Two of the day's cultural stories concern the intersection of sport, nationalism, and state power. Trump personally telephoned FIFA President Gianni Infantino to nullify US player Folarin Balogun's red-card suspension via an unprecedented invocation of Disciplinary Code Article 27 — a move UEFA branded "unprecedented, incomprehensible and unjustifiable." Coming ahead of the World Cup final, it exemplifies the fusion of a corrupt sporting bureaucracy with an increasingly authoritarian executive that treats international institutions as personal instruments. In New Zealand, the *NZ Herald*'s belated, glowing profile of Sam Haines — a 22-year-old killed fighting for Ukraine's neo-Nazi Azov Brigade despite no prior military training — drew a WSWS rebuke as war propaganda that whitewashes a fascist formation. Six New Zealanders have now died in the conflict.

## Pseudo-left press review

- [Jacobin / DSA](#jacobin--dsa) — Defends DSA against Chait while framing socialism as electoral capture of the Democratic Party
- [Left Voice](#left-voice) — Correctly opposes tying labor to the Democrats, but channels the AfD fight into street protest
- [PSL / Liberation News](#psl--liberation-news) — Uncritical solidarity with the Stalinist TKP's anti-NATO protests
- [Socialist Worker (SWP)](#socialist-worker-swp) — Militant anti-war rhetoric coupled with left pressure on Labour figures
- [The Communist (RCP/IMT)](#the-communist-rcpimt) — Growth-obsessed party-building amid a Palantir exposé

### Jacobin / DSA

Jacobin's July output is dominated by electoral strategy. "Jonathan Chait Doesn't Understand the Socialists He's Attacking" defends the DSA and the Michael Harrington tradition against a liberal critic, but does so on Harrington's own terms — as a left wing operating *within* the Democratic Party's orbit. Alongside profiles of DSA candidates (Claire Valdez for Congress, assembly and municipal races), the through-line is the Mamdani model: socialism realized through Democratic primary victories. This is the essence of the pseudo-left's role — subordinating a radicalizing base to the graveyard of the Democratic Party while dressing electoral opportunism in socialist language. The class interest expressed is that of a privileged layer seeking careers within the existing political order.

### Left Voice

Left Voice offers a sharper posture. Tatiana Cozzarelli's July 3 piece rightly attacks an argument (by Levin) that "transforms a debate about Amazon organizing into an argument for binding the labor movement more tightly to Democratic Party politics" — a correct polemical target. Josefina Martínez and Matías Maiello's July 5 report celebrates tens of thousands blocking the AfD convention. Yet Left Voice's Morenoite framework channels the struggle against fascism into militant protest and united-front activity with the very reformist forces it criticizes, rather than the independent political mobilization of the working class against all capitalist parties. It criticizes Democratic-Party integration while remaining organizationally oriented to the same milieu.

### PSL / Liberation News

The PSL issued a "Solidarity Statement with the Communist Party of Turkey (TKP)" praising its multi-city anti-NATO mobilizations in Ankara, Istanbul, and İzmir. The statement's uncritical embrace of the TKP — a Stalinist party with a nationalist program — typifies the PSL's method of substituting cheerleading for existing bureaucratic and nationalist formations for an independent revolutionary perspective. Anti-NATO militancy is welcome, but the PSL offers no criticism of the TKP's political limitations or of Stalinism's historic role, reflecting its own campist orientation toward states and parties opposed to Washington.

### Socialist Worker (SWP)

The UK SWP's site ran "Labour plans to steal £6.8 billion from other sectors to feed the war machine" and coverage of Israel's systematic denial of Palestinian water access — both correctly identifying militarism and colonial oppression. But the SWP's method remains one of pressuring Labour figures (e.g., approvingly noting Andy Burnham may drop Palantir's NHS contract) and building protest movements, rather than a break with Labourism. Its anti-war stance coexists with a perspective of left reform of the existing bourgeois-workers' parties.

### The Communist (RCP/IMT)

The British RCP's *The Communist* is consumed with party-building metrics ("Forward to 1,400 members," a countdown to its National Congress) alongside a substantive exposé of Palantir's infiltration of the British state. The recruitment triumphalism substitutes organizational growth for a worked-out revolutionary program, and the group's post-2024 rebranding away from the IMT reflects a turn toward abstract "communist" propagandism disconnected from the actual struggles of the working class.
