### 3. Famine confirmed in Al Fasher and Kadugli as 19.5 million Sudanese go hungry

UNICEF and UN agencies confirmed that famine — IPC Phase 5, the highest classification — has taken hold in two new regions of Sudan: Al Fasher in North Darfur and Kadugli in South Kordofan, both cut off by fighting between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces. An estimated 19.5 million Sudanese, roughly two in every five people, are now at crisis food insecurity or worse.

The collapse is the product of a war that has displaced nearly 9 million people internally since April 2023, combined with a near-total breakdown of global humanitarian funding. UN officials warned that two-thirds of all global hunger is now concentrated in just 10 conflict-hit countries.

Key facts:
- 825,000 children under five are projected to suffer severe acute malnutrition in 2026.
- 40% of Sudan's health facilities are no longer functioning; 17 million lack safe drinking water.
- Global humanitarian funding has collapsed by 59%, leaving agencies unable to respond at scale.
- 14 localities in Sudan are classified at risk of famine, with conditions now confirmed in two.

### Medicaid work requirements threaten millions

The Medicaid work-requirement provisions of the "One Big Beautiful Bill" (signed July 4, 2025) require 74.9 million enrollees to document 80 monthly hours of work or community service — at an effective rate of $6.25–$6.75/hour, below the federal minimum wage — or lose coverage. Nebraska, Montana and Iowa have already implemented the rules; 43 states face a January 1, 2027 deadline.

- The CBO projects $326 billion in federal savings over 10 years and 5.2 million coverage losses by 2034; the CBPP warns up to 14.9 million are at risk.
- The bill cuts Medicaid by $793 billion over 10 years.
- CMS administrator Mehmet Oz justified the rules by claiming recipients spend "6.1 hours watching television."
- As the WSWS documents, the administrative burden is the mechanism: savings come from attrition among already-working or legally-exempt people, then are redistributed upward through tax cuts.

US measles cases have surpassed 2,030 confirmed infections across 30 active outbreaks as of June 4 — nearly matching all of 2025's record 2,288. Active outbreaks continue in Florida, Pennsylvania, Utah and Virginia; 93% of cases are outbreak-associated, 92% involve unvaccinated individuals, and 127 patients (6%) have been hospitalized. The most significant cultural story of the week is the institutional rupture at the Kennedy Center, addressed above as a top story. "Ragtime," E.L. Doctorow's panorama of class, race and immigrant life at the turn of the 20th century, finally won Best Musical Revival after losing the top prize in its 1998 run, with Joshua Henry and Caissie Levy taking the lead acting awards — a belated recognition of a work that grapples seriously with American social history.

The death of **Marjane Satrapi at 56** is a major loss. The Iranian-French author of *Persepolis* — the autobiographical graphic novel and Oscar-nominated animated film recounting a childhood under the Islamic Republic and exile in Europe — gave international readers one of the most humane and unsentimental portraits of life under repression. Her death is especially poignant as US and Israeli bombs fall on the country whose people she rendered with such care; her work stands as a rebuke to the dehumanization that accompanies war. The Élysée Palace called her "an artist devoted to freedom, whose work carried a universal message."
