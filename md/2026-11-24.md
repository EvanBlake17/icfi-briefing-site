### Pennsylvania reports its ninth measles death as state and federal counts diverge

The Pennsylvania Department of Health confirmed the state's ninth measles death of 2026, an unvaccinated resident of Centre County. The state now has 1,638 confirmed cases in 46 counties and 330 hospitalizations, up from 1,589 cases and 315 hospitalizations in last week's update. The nine deaths have occurred in five counties, three of them in Lancaster.

State figures and the CDC's count are now far apart. Wisconsin reports two deaths, New York one child, and Virginia its first measles death in more than three decades. With Pennsylvania's nine, that makes at least 13. The CDC lists only six 2026 deaths and does not say where they occurred. The gap follows its switch, under HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., from state reports to slower National Center for Health Statistics mortality data. HHS said it was "saddened" and noted that the CDC had given Pennsylvania nearly $4 million in one-time emergency funding, which is earmarked for 2027.

Key facts:
- **National:** 5,838 confirmed cases in 47 jurisdictions as of November 19, the highest since 1991 and more than 2.5 times the 2,289 cases in all of 2025. About 96 percent are linked to outbreaks.
- **Age:** 18 percent of cases are children under five. Children and teens aged 5 to 19 account for 41 percent.
- **Coverage:** Kindergarten MMR coverage for 2025–26 is 92.4 percent, below the 95 percent needed for herd immunity.
