### 1. Brazil votes in a dead-heat runoff between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro

Brazilians vote today in the second round between President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (PT, Broad Front) and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro (PL). Both closed their campaigns on Saturday with rallies: Lula, 80, on Copacabana Beach and Avenida Paulista, and Bolsonaro in Minas Gerais, with hundreds of motorcyclists in yellow national team shirts, and in Rio's Barra da Tijuca. Voting is electronic and results usually come within hours of polls closing. Governors in five states and the Federal District are also on the runoff ballot.

The final CNT/MDA survey, released Saturday, has Bolsonaro at 46.3 percent and Lula at 45 percent. A week earlier the same pollster had it 46.8 to 45.9. Lula attacked his opponent's ties to Washington on the final day: "The people must choose either someone who kisses the national flag or someone who kisses the American flag." One organizer at a rally told AFP voters were "stuck choosing between the worst, the horrible, and the lesser evil." As Tomas Castanheira wrote on the WSWS this week, Lula is offering "essentially the same fiscal agenda" as Bolsonaro, "in a form designed to avoid social upheaval."

Key facts:
- **Electorate:** almost 160 million are eligible to vote and voting is compulsory. More than 33 million people (21 percent) did not vote in the first round on October 4, when Bolsonaro led 47 to 45 percent, a margin of 2.2 million votes.
- **Lula's record:** his approval fell as low as 24 percent this term over crime and the cost of living. He raised Bolsa Família by 15 percent, cut fuel taxes, and backed legislation the Senate approved cutting the maximum workweek from 44 to 40 hours.
- **Bolsonaro's program:** El Salvador-style megaprisons, designating transnational gangs as terrorist organizations, and trying 16-year-olds as adults. He admitted taking millions of dollars from banker Daniel Vorcaro to fund a film about his father and is under investigation for corruption, money laundering and tax evasion.
- **Washington's role:** Trump designated Brazil's largest drug-trafficking groups as terrorist organizations shortly after receiving Flávio at the White House. The Lula government called it a pretext for intervention. Trump imposed 50 percent tariffs last year, citing the prosecution of Jair Bolsonaro, who is serving 27 years under house detention for the coup attempt. A new round of tariffs followed this year.
- **Stakes for the region:** the Seattle Times/AP headline frames the vote as one with "US influence over Latin America at stake," and ABC (Australia) writes that Trump "could soon be one step closer" to "restoring" US power in the hemisphere.

### 2. Trump says a US attack on Iran could come as early as Sunday

Trump said on Saturday that Washington is weighing an attack on Iran as soon as Sunday, while also floating a "major deal." He said the US might have to "finish the job." This reverses the line the White House gave on October 23, that the US "will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the midterm elections." At a Florida rally on Friday, Trump said the military "may do another little Epic Fury," the codename for the bombing campaign that opened the war on February 28.

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian blamed Washington for the collapse of talks on an interim agreement, calling the Americans "cowards" who "messed everything up," but said both sides should return to the deal and "conclude it." Government spokeswoman Fatemeh Mohajerani said Tehran would not "wait for permission to defend itself." The IRGC warned through Fars that even limited strikes would bring a response "several times greater."

Key facts:
- **Evacuations:** Washington has reportedly ordered many diplomatic staff to leave Saudi Arabia, and departures from other countries in the region are expected (Al Jazeera).
- **Intelligence memo:** an October 23 memo from Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton to Rubio says Iranian lethal plotting against US citizens has "reached advanced stages in both the Middle East and certain other countries" (ABC News, first reported by Axios).
- **Shipping:** the IRGC said seven tankers were attacked or hit mines overnight into Thursday, and two supertankers struck mines on Friday. UKMTO separately reported a tanker hit by an unknown projectile in Hormuz.
- **Iranian deterrent claims:** Iranian media say Tehran has activated a "dead hand" doctrine allowing attacks from all 31 provinces if more senior figures are assassinated. The IRGC has threatened seabed internet and communications cables if civilian infrastructure is hit again.
- **Istanbul meeting:** foreign ministers of Türkiye, Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Egypt, joined by the UK, France, Germany and Italy, met without the US or Iran. The four regional states called Israeli occupation "the main source of instability in the region and beyond." Turkish FM Hakan Fidan said military intervention "is by no means a solution."
- **Leadership:** Mojtaba Khamenei, who succeeded his assassinated father in March, has not appeared in public since. Judiciary chief Mohseni Ejei and speaker and chief negotiator Ghalibaf have reportedly met him this week.
- **Iranian commentary:** analyst Reza Nasri, who has ties to the Foreign Ministry, wrote that Iran has reached a verdict of "enough is enough" and will not negotiate under bombardment.

### 3. Second weekend of mass protests sweeps France, Italy, Spain and Albania

Demonstrations against the cost of living, school conditions and governments took place across southern and western Europe on Saturday, a week after the October 17 riots. The scale in France was close to the previous weekend's even though routes were banned and the security deployment was the largest yet. The youth-led movement that began with high school blockades in mid-September now openly calls for Macron's resignation and denounces the billionaires.

**France:** the Interior Ministry counted 410,000 marchers nationwide, 110,000 of them in Paris. The CGT says both figures are almost double. Police arrested "nearly 1,000" people and carried out more than 3,000 checks in Paris. Officers fired tear gas and water cannon at nightfall, and the authorities said 500 "rioters" clashed with police. The government allowed only a route on the city's southeastern rim. It banned protests not organized by unions in Paris and all protests in Montpellier, Rouen and Nice. Police used tear gas on 150 people in Lyon. At least 24 metro stations closed. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez spoke of "an extremely elevated level of violence," while the government's line was "the marches went well." Student representatives said Friday's meeting with Macron had resolved nothing. He promised more teachers and an overhaul of the Parcoursup admissions system. The movement has called another protest for next Saturday.

**The far right:** the National Rally's congress in Orléans re-elected Jordan Bardella as party president with nearly 99 percent. Le Pen and Bardella were due to launch their presidential campaign before about 10,000 supporters on Sunday. Thousands of counter-protesters chanted antifascist slogans in the city.

**Italy:** protests took place in eight cities against the Meloni government. Milan peaked at about 10,000, mostly young people, with hundreds of soldiers deployed. ANSA reported clashes in Turin and Rome. Police reported nine arrests and 371 people briefly detained nationwide, after about 150 house raids in Lombardy on Saturday morning. The administrator of the Movimento 17 Ottobre Instagram page, a 30-year-old Italian-Moroccan journalist, is under investigation for "inciting crimes." With most of those arrested under 18, Justice Minister Carlo Nordio said Italy needs to expand youth detention capacity.

**Spain:** about 25,000 marched from Atocha to Gran Vía in Madrid to defend the state pension system before next month's general election. They demanded continued inflation indexing and an end to the gender pension gap.

**Albania:** in Tirana, police used tear gas, water cannon and rubber bullets on thousands of protesters. More than 300 protesters and police were injured, the worst clashes so far, and dozens were arrested. Protesters threw stones and rolled burning bins at the Education Ministry. They also set fire to the red cushions Prime Minister Edi Rama had laid outside government headquarters as a symbol of "dialogue." Rama blamed "foreign actors," Russia and Iran. Some placards denounced opposition leader Sali Berisha as well.

### 4. Israel drives 2,000 Palestinians from their homes in Hebron and seals the West Bank for election day

The Hebron operation that began on Friday turned into a mass expulsion overnight. Soldiers announced evacuation orders over loudspeakers late at night. Hebron Mayor Yousef al-Ja'bari says 2,200 people were forced out, and Defense Minister Israel Katz put the figure at "about 2,000." Katz said they "will not be allowed to return to their neighbourhood until we've finished dealing with all the Hamas issues in Hebron." The city of about 200,000 is sealed, and no one can enter or leave. The army has turned Palestinian homes into military posts (Anadolu) and is bulldozing buildings. Soldiers fired a stun grenade at an Al Jazeera crew filming demolitions near the Farsh al-Hawa checkpoint.

The army will bar Palestinian workers from most of the West Bank on Sunday and Monday and close all crossings on Tuesday, when Israel votes. Netanyahu would not say whether he would dismantle the Palestinian Authority but promised it a "big surprise." Al Jazeera's Nour Odeh says Likud and its coalition, which trail in the polls, are trying to "capitalise" on the attack.

Key facts:
- **Raids:** at least 15 areas were raided on Saturday, including Jenin, Nablus, Ramallah, al-Bireh, Birzeit, East Jerusalem and Bethlehem. The Palestinian Prisoner's Society counted about 70 arrests. In Halhul, nine people, six of them children, were treated for tear gas inhalation.
- **Guterres:** the closures "severely restrict Palestinians' freedom of movement, access to basic services and livelihoods."
- **UN Human Rights Office (Mayy El Sheikh):** "yet another episode in a never-ending stream of suffocating measures."
- **B'Tselem:** "collective punishment and acts that could constitute war crimes."
- **Israeli election:** thousands marched from Rabin Square to Hostages Square in Tel Aviv on Saturday at the October Council's call for a state commission of inquiry into October 7. The government offers only a "political" commission appointed half by the coalition and half by the opposition. The last polls before the gag period show neither Netanyahu's bloc nor Gadi Eisenkot's with a majority.

### 5. BC Conservatives win their first majority in nearly a century as Eby resigns

The British Columbia Conservatives, led by interim leader Lorne Doerkson, were projected to win a majority in Saturday's snap provincial election. CBC calls it the party's first majority "in nearly a century." NDP Premier David Eby, whose decision to call the vote two years early the Vancouver Sun says "backfired," resigned as party leader on election night. Doerkson promised to get BC "back on track." Some Vancouver Island seats were still close as of the latest reports, and no final seat counts were available.

Eby called the election to exploit opposition to Trump's tariffs and annexation threats, and the Conservatives' split after they fell three seats short in 2024. As Penny Smith wrote on the WSWS before the vote, the NDP campaigned on a 2026 budget that cuts 15,000 full-time public-sector jobs by 2028–29 and withdraws $1.4 billion from social housing. That left the field to a party campaigning on tax cuts, more police and doubling gas production.

Key facts:
- **Conservative platform:** tripling the basic personal exemption to $32,000, a "never raise taxes" pledge, more police, and an "Energy Superpower Plan" to double gas production by 2032.
- **NDP legacy:** Eby's "Balanced Measures Mandate" set a flat 3 percent annual raise for more than 450,000 public employees. His "Look West" strategy targeted $200 billion in major-project investment and a 35 percent share of federal defence vessel contracts.
- **Splinter parties:** CentreBC and Sturko remained "positive" despite the night's results (Maple Ridge News). The Greens under Emily Lowan had campaigned to "hold the balance of power."

### 6. Serbia and Bulgaria go to the polls

**Serbia:** polls are open from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. in the snap parliamentary election Aleksandar Vučić called in September, after almost two years of student-led protests that began when the Novi Sad station canopy collapsed in November 2024, killing 16. Eighteen lists are competing for 250 seats. Vučić resigned the presidency to run for prime minister and is promising higher pensions and public-sector wages, more health spending and subsidies for young couples. The Student List is led by cardiologist Ilija Srdanović and fields about 250 mostly unknown candidates, among them professors, actors and farmers. Its program centers on corruption and education. An Ipsos poll published Thursday gave Vučić's SNS 47 percent and the students 40 percent. An October 20 poll had the students ahead, 44.5 to 42.7. Verisk Maplecroft's Mario Bikarski calls it "a watershed moment," and Al Jazeera compares a Vučić defeat to Orbán's loss in April. Vučić claims the students are financed by "foreign agents" in Croatia and Montenegro.

**Bulgaria:** 27 candidates are running for the largely ceremonial but directly elected presidency. Acting President Iliana Iotova, backed by Rumen Radev, who won the parliamentary election that analysts called Kremlin-friendly, leads former caretaker PM Andrey Gyurov 41 to 28 percent (Alpha Research). Gyurov signed a security agreement with Kyiv while in office. Iotova's camp calls for a diplomatic end to the Ukraine war and, according to analysts, an end to arms deliveries. She says her opponents favor "a war of attrition" when "we are all the ones who are most exhausted." Turnout is expected below the 50 percent threshold, so a November 1 runoff is likely. Drone strikes on merchant ships off the Bulgarian coast, which likely killed about two dozen seafarers, dominated the campaign. Radev has not explicitly blamed Moscow but said the outcome "benefits the Russian Federation."

### Ukraine says Starlink is restored after Musk admits throttling it

More than a week of restrictions had hobbled Ukrainian drone operations. Zelenskyy wrote: "The speeds are now where they need to be." Musk admitted that SpaceX had throttled the service. He claimed Ukraine's military violated its terms by using it for offensive drone strikes, said that "loophole" was closed last week, and warned the front would collapse if Starlink were switched off. Trump denied responsibility while defending Musk. The episode shows how much a single billionaire's private company controls the communications of a frontline army.

Ukraine hit the Togliattikauchuk petrochemical plant, a petrochemical plant and Ozon warehouse in Omsk, and the Proton electronics plant in Oryol, where the governor reported three dead. Crimea had power cuts.
- **Casualty count:** the *Kyiv Independent* reports that a media investigation has now identified almost 283,000 Russian soldiers killed in the war by name.

### Houthis fire on Aden and Saudi Arabia as Riyadh airport reopens

Yemen's Transport Ministry says two Houthi ballistic missiles hit Aden airport, the fourth such attack this month, along with the coastguard headquarters. The Houthis claimed strikes on the al-Nasr camp and Aden's naval base. Saudi civil defense said two missiles were fired toward Mecca and Medina on Saturday, which the Houthis called a "huge lie." ### Ancient proteins put a face on the Denisovans

A team led by Qiaomei Fu at the Chinese Academy of Sciences reports in *Nature* that it extracted and sequenced 18 ancient proteins from a skull and a jaw fragment excavated at Xujiayao in northern China in 1976–79. The fragments had been assigned to different lineages. The key marker is a collagen variant, COL1A2-996K, found in Denisovans but not in Neanderthals or modern humans, which "classifies Xujiayao" as Denisovan. 3D reconstructions show low, flat skulls with thick bone, heavy brow ridges and flat faces, combining older hominin traits with ones shared with Neanderthals and later humans. The result means the Denisovans, until now known mainly from Siberia and Tibet, lived across East Asia, and other unassigned East Asian fossils may belong to the same lineage. This is one of the most significant advances in paleoanthropology this year, and it comes from protein analysis rather than DNA, which rarely survives at these ages.

### Paris museums close and Art Basel cancels as the state turns the city into a security zone

On Saturday the Louvre and the Musée d'Orsay closed, and Art Basel Paris, the main commercial event of the city's art season, cancelled its weekend program. At least 24 metro stations were closed at police request, and shopkeepers boarded up their windows. The shutdowns came from the government's security operation against the student and worker protests, not from the demonstrators.

The same week, Claude Monet's *Iris* sold for about €50 million in Paris, the most expensive artwork ever auctioned in France (France 24). The two stories belong together. Marchers chanting against the billionaires were kept to the outer boulevards, while in the city center the art market set records for the class whose wealth, the WSWS Perspective noted, has soared (the ten richest people's fortunes rose 40 percent in a year). Art Basel's cancellation shows how far the social crisis now reaches into institutions that have treated Paris as a secure luxury market. The AKAA art and design fair went ahead with work by African artists, the same week a racist gunman targeted people of African origin in Trappes.

### Jonathan Groff turns to the Nickel Mines school massacre

The *Washington Post* reports that Jonathan Groff's next Broadway project is a solo play based on the October 2006 shooting at the West Nickel Mines Amish schoolhouse in Lancaster County, Pennsylvania, where a gunman killed five girls before killing himself. The Amish community's public forgiveness of the killer's family became a national story at the time. A star best known for musical theater (*Hamilton*, *Merrily We Roll Along*) is taking on a story of mass violence in a country where shootings like Saturday's in Tallahassee are routine. Whether the play examines the social conditions behind such violence or settles for a redemptive narrative about forgiveness will decide its value. The creative team and dates have not yet been announced.

### Culture roundup

- **Broadway:** the *Purple Rain* stage musical, based on Prince's 1984 film, has a new creative team: director Saheem Ali (*Fat Ham*, *Merry Wives*) and book writer Peter Duchan. It is now aimed at Broadway in 2027. It is another catalogue project in a season dominated by brand-name adaptations, as producers rely on known properties under financial pressure.
- **Nordic Council Prizes:** the 2026 winners in literature, children's literature, film, music and environment will be honored in Helsinki on October 26, and the evening closes with a screening of the winning film.
- **Art and astronomy:** researchers have matched van Gogh's *The Starry Night* to the actual night sky over Saint-Rémy in 1889. The painting records what the artist saw from his asylum window, not only what he imagined.
- **Netflix in Korea:** Netflix is marking a decade in Korea with the stars of *Squid Game* and *KPop Demon Hunters* (Variety). *Squid Game*'s portrait of debt and social desperation remains among the company's most-watched series worldwide.
- **Unverified:** a report that Irish folk musician Dónal Lunny (Planxty, the Bothy Band) had died could not be confirmed and is not treated as fact here.

### Jacobin / DSA

- **"A New Israeli Government Won't Stop the Settlements"** ([Jacobin](https://jacobin.com/2026/10/israel-palestine-elections-settlements-occupation), interview with Diana Buttu by Elias Feroz). Buttu rightly rejects any illusion that an Eisenkot government would end settlement expansion, and she calls Israel's system a "racial hierarchy." But her program is addressed entirely to states and bourgeois institutions: governments should move from statements to trade cuts, sanctions, expulsion from the UN and an arms embargo. She relies on the Genocide Convention and the ICJ, which she admits is "too slow," and on Palestinian Authority elections on November 28 to "restore representation." The working class, Israeli, Palestinian or international, does not appear anywhere in the piece.
- **"Israel's Rogue State Behavior Is Somehow Only Worsening"** ([Jacobin](https://jacobin.com/2026/10/israel-gaza-iran-crimes-settlers), Branko Marcetic). The article catalogues settler attacks on journalists and European diplomats, ceasefire violations in Gaza and Lebanon, and threats against Iran. It treats Israel as a "rogue" ally that Washington should treat as it would an adversary. That turns US imperialism's attack dog into an aberration from US policy, when it is the instrument of that policy, as the Hegseth visit and the joint post-midterm strike plans show. The Democratic Party is not mentioned once, 10 days before the midterms.
- **"Brazil's Far-Right Trajectory Is a Warning for Us All"** ([Jacobin](https://jacobin.com/2026/10/brazil-far-right-social-disintegration-exploitation), Rodrigo Nunes, a book excerpt). Nunes draws on dependency theory and Paulo Arantes to present Brazil as the "vanguard of disintegration." On the day before a runoff in which the PT's Broad Front has adopted Bolsonaro's penal and fiscal agenda, the essay says nothing about Lula, the PT or the vote. Coming after Jacobin's "Lula Must Win" earlier this week, the silence works as cover for the PT.

*Assessment:* moral condemnation of Israel combined with appeals to the "international community," and theoretical generalities on Brazil that leave the PT untouched. The class question is left out of every piece.

### Left Voice

- **"International Port Strike on October 30 Against the Arms Trade and the War Economy"** ([Left Voice](https://www.leftvoice.org/october-30-port-strike-against-war-economy/), Mariona Tasquer). The article promotes coordinated action at about 57 ports in Italy, France, Greece, the Basque Country, Turkey and Morocco against arms shipments and the militarization of ports, together with demands on wages, pensions and AI-driven job cuts. The action is important and deserves WSWS attention. But Left Voice explicitly endorses "the leading role of labor organizations" in each country. It raises no criticism of the union apparatuses organizing it, including the question of why the action is confined to a single day. It treats the strike as a self-contained Palestine-solidarity action rather than connecting it to the broader European strike wave and the fight against the bureaucracies that keep each national struggle separate.

*Assessment:* after this week's sharp language on Mamdani, Left Voice reverts to cheerleading for a union-led protest day. Its criticism of the bureaucracy is selective, applied to the Democratic Party in New York but suspended for "militant" unions.

### Socialist Worker / SWP

- **"Hundreds of anti-racists demonstrate and organise against the far right"** ([Socialist Worker](https://socialistworker.co.uk/anti-racism/hundreds-of-anti-racists-demonstrate-and-organise-against-the-far-right/), October 24). The article reports Stand Up To Racism marches in Sheffield, Newcastle, Cardiff (led by Jeremy Corbyn, with Plaid Cymru Senedd members), Morecambe, Wakefield and Herne Bay, and a 1,000-strong London summit of Together Against The Far Right. That alliance includes SUTR, the Palestine Solidarity Campaign, the Green Party, NGOs and the unions. The speakers included Unison general secretary Andrea Egan and NEU leader Daniel Kebede. The strategy is to "build roots in local areas" ahead of a "500,000-strong" unity march on March 28 next year.
  - **Political line:** a cross-class popular front against fascism that includes union general secretaries and a bourgeois party, the Greens. It does not explain how the Labour government's austerity and militarism feed Reform. This is the national, protest-based strategy the SWP pursued against the BNP and EDL, now repeated as the far right leads the polls.

*Assessment:* the SWP's "revolt" rhetoric on France sits alongside a domestic practice of alliances with union general secretaries and bourgeois parties. Rank-and-file independence from the bureaucracy, which it argues for in France, has no place in its work in Britain.

### Socialist Appeal / RCP

- **"Europe: A mirror to Britain's future"** ([communist.red](https://communist.red/europe-a-mirror-to-britains-future/), Adam Booth, October 22). Booth surveys the French, German (against conscription), Spanish (housing) and Italian movements as signs of a "world revolution" that will reach Britain. He predicts that the "Burnham bounce" will collapse under the pressure of austerity and calls Burnham a "wolf in sheep's clothing." He welcomes the Spanish unions' general strike call, which they made "under pressure from below," as an inspiring example of mass action. The article says nothing about Your Party, Corbyn, Reform or a British union strategy. Its only conclusion is that a revolutionary party is needed, meaning the RCP.
  - **Political line:** "revolution is coming" impressionism with no transitional program, no analysis of the union apparatus as an instrument of the state, and no international perspective beyond adding up national movements. It treats the union-called one-day strikes as victories for the rank and file rather than as maneuvers to keep control of the movement.

*Assessment:* the IMT's typical mix of revolutionary forecasts and party-building, with no fight against the bureaucracies that stand between the European youth movement and the working class.

**Convergences and flags:** none of the four tendencies mentioned the ICFI or WSWS today. Jacobin and the SWP converge with the liberal press in framing Israel and the far right as threats to "democracy" and "international law" rather than products of imperialist crisis. None of the articles reviewed addressed the DOJ's moves to contest the midterm results in California and Alaska.
