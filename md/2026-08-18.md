### 1. US-Iran deal deadline expires as Trump threatens Oman

The 60-day deadline established by the 14-point Memorandum of Understanding signed by Donald Trump and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian on June 17 expired Monday with no permanent agreement, leaving the United States and Iran deadlocked over the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, the US naval blockade, and the disposition of frozen Iranian funds. Trump had already declared the MOU "over" on July 8. On Monday he said he was "not in a hurry" to conclude a deal and threatened to "bomb" Oman, which has served as the principal intermediary in the talks.

The economic consequences were immediate. Brent crude settled near $91 per barrel, its highest level of the year, as vessel traffic through the Strait fell to near a standstill. At least one cargo ship was struck, reportedly resulting in a crew casualty and engine-room damage.

Key facts:

- Tehran and Washington had committed in the June MOU to reach a "final deal in maximum 60 days." No concrete framework for ending the war was announced as the window closed.
- Trump ruled out extending the shipping ceasefire covering the Strait of Hormuz and threatened Iran with "economic isolation."
- The conflict has now run nearly six months. Higher oil prices have fed directly into inflation expectations, cutting the market-implied probability of a September Fed rate cut to roughly 30 percent from about 50 percent a week earlier.
- Iran's foreign minister separately condemned a drone attack on the office of Iraqi Kurdistan Region Prime Minister Masrour Barzani, a sign of how the conflict continues to metastasize across the region.

### 3. Ebola outbreak in DR Congo becomes deadliest in the country's history

The World Health Organization confirmed Monday that the Ebola Bundibugyo outbreak in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo has become the deadliest in the country's history, with 2,325 deaths recorded — surpassing the 2,299 deaths of the 2018-2020 outbreak. There are 4,945 confirmed cases. The outbreak is the fastest-growing on record.

The speed is without precedent. Deaths reached 2,000 within three months; by comparison, the 2014-16 West Africa epidemic took five months to reach 1,000 deaths. UN officials describe the outbreak as killing approximately one person every 30 minutes.

Key facts:

- The outbreak is spreading roughly five times faster than previous Ebola epidemics, according to WHO comparisons.
- Only the 2014-2016 West Africa outbreak (28,616 cases, 11,310 deaths) remains larger in absolute terms — but the current outbreak's fatality rate per unit time far exceeds it.
- The epidemic is unfolding in Ituri and surrounding provinces, regions devastated by decades of armed conflict, mass displacement, and the collapse of health infrastructure.
- The Bundibugyo strain has no licensed vaccine equivalent to the Ervebo product used against Zaire ebolavirus, a critical gap in the response.

The scale of this catastrophe — over 2,300 dead in an outbreak that has received a fraction of the attention devoted to far smaller events in the imperialist centres — is a direct index of the subordination of global public health to the profit system and the collapse of international coordination since the COVID-19 pandemic.

### 5. Russia jails opposition politician Lev Shlosberg for 11 years over anti-war posts

A court in Pskov on Monday sentenced Lev Shlosberg, deputy head of the liberal Yabloko party, to 11 years and one month in prison for "public actions aimed at discrediting the use of the Armed Forces of the Russian Federation." The 63-year-old's offence consisted of posting a February 2022 *Daily Mirror* front page on social media and a video arguing for an end to the fighting in Ukraine. Shlosberg is among the few Kremlin opponents who remained inside Russia after the post-2022 crackdown on dissent.

The verdict came one week after Russia's Supreme Court barred Yabloko from contesting the next State Duma election, on August 10.

Key facts:

- Amnesty International characterised the sentence as part of "mounting pressure" on Yabloko and demanded Shlosberg's immediate release.
- The sentence follows by one week the 15-year sentence handed down by a Pervomaisk court in Ukraine against 27-year-old Trotskyist Bogdan Syrotiuk, leader of the Young Guard of Bolshevik-Leninists, on "high treason" charges for calling for the unity of Russian and Ukrainian workers against the war.
- Taken together, the two cases demonstrate that both belligerent regimes are criminalising anti-war speech, and that the war is being prosecuted against the working class on both sides of the front.
- As Andre Damon writes on the WSWS today, the *New York Times* ran a front-page story with a "nine-minute produced audio version" and four assigned journalists on a Ukrainian prisoner held by Russia — while publishing not "one line" on Syrotiuk.

### 6. Indonesia earthquake death toll climbs as survivors go without aid

The death toll from the magnitude 7.7 earthquake that struck off Flores Island continued to rise, with at least 53 confirmed dead by government count and WSWS reporting at least 68. The quake struck at 5:58 a.m. local time Saturday, with an epicentre roughly 68 km north-northwest of Ende, and has been followed by 341 aftershocks.

The disaster's toll is a product of construction standards, not seismology. As Robert Campion reports on the WSWS, 80 percent of Indonesian buildings are erected without engineering oversight and only 11 percent meet earthquake standards — while the Prabowo government cut the national disaster agency's funding by 4.5 percent in the same period defence spending rose 30 percent.

Key facts:

- Casualties: 36 seriously injured and 77 with minor injuries, per Deputy Health Minister Benjamin Paulus Octavianus.
- Approximately 5,000 people evacuated; 157 homes destroyed and nearly 200 damaged.
- Over 3,500 military and police personnel deployed with helicopters and a rescue vessel — the state's response organised as a military operation rather than a civil relief effort.
- Survivors report receiving no government aid days after the quake.

### Zambia's Hichilema re-elected with 61.4 percent

Zambia's electoral commission chairperson Mwangala Zaloumis declared Hakainde Hichilema president-elect on August 18, with 2,965,326 votes — 61.4 percent of roughly 5 million cast — against opposition leader Brian Mundubile's 1,856,217. No run-off was required. It was Hichilema's seventh run for the presidency; Mundubile's unexpectedly strong showing capitalised on continuing hardship despite an official "economic turnaround."

- The "turnaround" registered in GDP figures has meant sharply higher costs for maize meal, fuel and electricity for the urban poor and Copperbelt mineworkers — the source of the opposition vote, which has no independent working-class expression.

### California certifies the largest gig-driver union in the world — with drivers still classified as contractors

The California Public Employment Relations Board verified that the California Gig Workers Union has met the support threshold to represent rideshare drivers statewide. Following a 30-day waiting period, the union can be certified as the collective bargaining representative for Uber and Lyft drivers — potentially the largest union of gig workers anywhere, covering roughly 800,000 people.

The critical fact is what the enabling law, AB 1340, does not do. Drivers remain classified as independent contractors. They gain a right to sectoral bargaining over wages and conditions while being denied employee status, minimum wage protections, overtime, unemployment insurance and workers' compensation. Delivery drivers for DoorDash and similar platforms are excluded entirely. The arrangement — negotiated between the Newsom administration, SEIU, and the platform companies themselves — institutionalises a second-tier legal category for a mass of low-paid workers, with a dues-collecting apparatus attached.

That a generic costing pennies delivers outcomes comparable to drugs priced at hundreds of dollars monthly is an indictment of a pharmaceutical research system organised around patentability rather than therapeutic value.

### Salzburg Festival remains without an artistic director after political purge

The Salzburg Festival — the most prestigious classical music and theatre festival in Europe — has completed its 2026 season under an interim director, seven months after the abrupt removal of Markus Hinterhäuser. The board announced in March that Hinterhäuser would leave "effective immediately," citing "irreconcilable differences of opinion," and placed him on paid leave until his contract expired at the end of September. Karin Bergmann has served as interim artistic director since.

The official account is a contract dispute: Hinterhäuser was initially offered a third term running from October 2026 to 2031, then presented with a one-year extension he declined to accept before the board's deadline. As Verena Nees reports on the WSWS, that account obscures years of political pressure over Hinterhäuser's refusal to blacklist Russian artists — above all the conductor Teodor Currentzis and soprano Anna Netrebko — and his removal followed shortly after a *Der Spiegel* article questioning his conduct. Several sponsors withdrew funding over the dismissal, and a group of businesspeople and politicians sent a letter of solidarity to the board.

Whatever the internal politics, the outcome is unambiguous: the leading festival of the German-speaking world has been decapitated, and the principle that artists are to be judged by their work rather than their passport has been abandoned. Nees draws the parallel to the Nazi *Gleichschaltung* of Austrian cultural life after 1938 — a comparison that will be attacked, but which accurately identifies the mechanism at work: cultural institutions being brought into line with a war drive.

### Tupac Shakur murder trial opens 30 years on

Opening statements began Monday in Las Vegas in the trial of Duane "Keffe D" Davis, 63, charged with murder with a deadly weapon in the September 7, 1996 killing of Tupac Shakur. Shakur's death at 25 remains one of the most significant cultural events of the 1990s. His best work registered, with real artistic power, the social devastation of American inner cities under Reagan and Clinton — mass incarceration, the destruction of industrial employment, police violence — even as it was distorted by the commercial machinery that packaged that devastation for sale.

LAUSD saw mass student walkouts against ICE raids earlier this year. The article argues that surveillance infrastructure introduced under a pedagogical justification will be used against student political organising, and links the policy to the broader militarisation of schools and the criminalisation of protest.

### Brazil: Railway workers strike exposes privatization disaster in São Paulo — Guilherme Ferreira, Brunna Machado

A strike on three São Paulo commuter rail lines operated by the private concessionaire Trivia Trens forced state intervention after the operator's service collapsed within three days, with fires and dangerous overcrowding on platforms. The strike was driven by fears of layoffs under Governor Tarcísio de Freitas's privatization drive.

The politically decisive fact the authors establish is the financing: the privatization programme of a far-right, Bolsonaro-aligned governor is being underwritten in part by BNDES, the Lula government's own national development bank. This is a concrete refutation of the Workers Party's claim to represent a defence of public services against the right. The union leadership steered the dispute into the labour courts and accepted a settlement redistributing rather than protecting jobs — the same mechanism documented in the Workers Struggles digest.

### Once again in defense of the American Revolution: A reply to a Brazilian historian — Tomas Castanheira, Tom Mackaman

A polemical reply to historian Mário Maestri defending the American Revolution as a genuine bourgeois-democratic revolution that abolished monarchy and feudal property relations on the North American continent. The authors contrast 1776 with Brazil's 1822 independence, which preserved both slavery and monarchy — a comparison that establishes the objectively progressive content of the American Revolution against claims that it was merely a slaveholders' rebellion. They argue that 1776 set in motion social and ideological forces completed by the Civil War, and that the "pseudo-left" dismissal of bourgeois-democratic gains — a line shared with the *New York Times*' 1619 Project — disarms the working class ideologically by denying that any historical progress has occurred and that any is possible. Useful for editorial discussion: this is the Brazilian front of the same historiographical struggle the WSWS has waged against Nikole Hannah-Jones and the racialist school in the United States.

### Fiji's Vatukoula goldminers receive a paltry payout after 35-year battle — John Braddock

Former Vatukoula gold mine workers, whose strike began in February 1991, have received approximately US$11,320 each following the Fijian government's announcement of a US$4.2 million settlement in October 2024. By the time payment arrived, roughly 190 of the miners had died without compensation. Braddock attributes the outcome to the union bureaucracy's refusal to mobilise international solidarity, confining the struggle to appeals to successive Fijian governments across three and a half decades — a case study in the fate of national-reformist strategies for a small working class facing a transnational mining operation.

### Trotsky's years on Prinkipo and the journey of *My Life* in Turkish — Ulaş Sevinç

A speech from the fourth International Commemoration of Leon Trotsky, held August 16 on Prinkipo (Büyükada), the island where Trotsky lived in exile from 1929 and wrote *My Life*. Sevinç traces the book's suppression in Turkish: it went untranslated for decades, a direct consequence of Stalinist domination of the Turkish Communist Party and its role in cutting Turkish workers and intellectuals off from the Left Opposition. The speech argues that Trotsky's analyses of fascism and of war — developed in significant part during the Prinkipo years, alongside the *History of the Russian Revolution* — retain immediate relevance to conditions in Turkey and internationally.

### 645 million go hungry as global military spending hits a record $2.9 trillion — Marc Wells

A UN 2026 food security report finds 645 million people chronically hungry and 2.69 billion unable to afford a nutritious diet, at a time when global agricultural capacity is more than adequate to feed the world population. In the same period global military spending reached $2.887 trillion. Wells sets the two figures against one another to establish that hunger is not a problem of production but of social organisation, and concludes that only the conquest of political power by the working class and the reorganisation of production on a planned, international basis can resolve the contradiction between capacity and want. The juxtaposition — a record military budget alongside a record number of hungry people — is a usable framing for the SEP's anti-war campaign.

### New York City workers respond to Mamdani's firing of Haitian workers — WSWS reporters

Interviews with MTA workers and Brooklyn and Queens community members following Mayor Zohran Mamdani's announcement that the city would dismiss Haitian municipal employees whose Temporary Protected Status was terminated by the Trump administration. Workers expressed anger at both the federal action and, pointedly, at Mamdani's compliance — the "democratic socialist" mayor implementing the deportation machinery's employment consequences within weeks of taking office. Several called for rank-and-file organising and general strike action to defend immigrant workers, independent of the union officials and the Democratic Party. The article is the most concrete refutation available of the perspective advanced by the DSA and its periphery that the capture of municipal office constitutes a road to socialism.

## Pseudo-left press review

- [Jacobin / DSA](#jacobin-dsa) — advises the Democratic Party on how to recapture defecting Trump voters through "economic populism," treating the party as the vehicle for working-class politics
- [Left Voice](#left-voice) — presents Bolivian "self-organization" as the international model against the far right while orienting to the US "shift to the left" inside Democratic primaries
- [Liberation News / PSL](#liberation-news-psl) — frames Democratic primary results as evidence of "the rise of socialism," measuring political progress by ballot-box outcomes within the ruling party
- [Socialist Alternative](#socialist-alternative) — combines calls for "left independent candidates" with sustained orientation to DSA-aligned Democratic campaigns and to the union apparatus
- [Socialist Worker / SWP (UK)](#socialist-worker-swp-uk) — building support for the new Corbyn-Sultana left party while covering strikes as pressure on that project
- [The Communist / RCP (IMT)](#the-communist-rcp-imt) — abstract propagandism for "world revolution" alongside identity-framed campaigning, with no orientation to the actual struggles of the day

---

### Jacobin / DSA

**"Donald Trump's Coalition Is Coming Apart"** (August 17) presents polling data showing Trump "waverers" rising from 20 percent in February to over 25 percent in July, with poor, non-white and young voters defecting fastest. The decisive finding is buried: fewer than 5 percent of 2024 Trump voters have switched to the Democrats; 56 percent of waverers remain unsure and 22 percent favour independents. Jacobin's own data thus documents a mass of workers breaking from both capitalist parties. The article's conclusion is nonetheless a strategy memo for the Democrats — that they can recapture these voters by "demonstrating commitment to economic populism and fighting corruption." The class content is explicit: Jacobin functions as a policy consultancy for a wing of the Democratic Party, and the independent political movement its own polling implies is precisely what it exists to prevent.

**"The Trump Administration Has Been Spying on Labor Unions"** reports DHS infiltration of meetings of the CWA and SEIU in Minnesota — genuinely important factual reporting. But the framing treats state surveillance as a Trump-specific aberration rather than the continuation of an apparatus built under Obama and Biden, and the implied remedy is electoral: replace the administration. **"Capitalism Is Killing Summer"** applies a similar method to public pools and municipal recreation — real austerity, described without any conclusion beyond restored public spending.

**Assessment:** Jacobin's output today is oriented entirely toward the 2026 midterms and the internal problems of the Democratic Party. The word "socialism" functions as a brand for a redistributive programme to be enacted by that party.

### Left Voice

**"Self Organization in Bolivia Shows How to Fight the Far Right Internationally"** and **"The Next Chapter in the Bolivian Struggle"** (both August 16) advance the Morenoite method: the elevation of spontaneous mass organisation — councils, blockades, neighbourhood committees — into a strategy in itself, without the construction of a revolutionary party contesting for power. Bolivia is presented as a "model," which evades the historical record of exactly how the MAS and the union bureaucracy demobilised these same structures in 2005-06 and again in 2019-20.

**"A New Political Moment? Trumpism's Crisis, the Midterms, and Shifts to the Left in the U.S."** (August 13) locates the "shift to the left" in Democratic Party primary results — the same framework as Jacobin, with a more radical vocabulary. **"Sanders-Backed Flanagan Wins Minnesota Senate Primary, Continuing Progressive Sweep"** (August 12) treats a Sanders-endorsed Democratic primary victory as a datum of working-class radicalisation. Left Voice formally criticises the Democratic Party while treating its internal contests as the arena where the class struggle is measured.

**Assessment:** Radical phraseology attached to an orientation indistinguishable in practice from the DSA milieu.

### Liberation News / PSL

**"Flanagan wins big in primary as Minnesotans reject AIPAC and ICE"** and **"Desperate Democratic establishment narrowly defeats Hong in Wisconsin – but it won't stop the rise of socialism"** (both August 12) construct an entire political perspective out of Democratic primary arithmetic. A Democratic candidate's victory becomes a popular rejection of ICE; a Democratic candidate's defeat becomes proof that socialism is rising. The PSL, which formally maintains a Marxist-Leninist self-description and runs its own presidential tickets, functions in practice as a left auxiliary of the Democratic Party's progressive wing at the local level.

**"A year of federal occupation in D.C. shows what's at stake in our struggle"** (August 11) documents the federal deployment in Washington but poses the alternative as "the movement" and street mobilisation, never as the political independence of the working class or the fight for power.

**Assessment:** Militant on the streets, adaptive at the ballot box — a combination that channels genuine anger back into the Democratic Party.

### Socialist Alternative

**"Democratic Socialist Francesca Hong Concedes In Wisconsin—What Way Forward?"** (August 15) draws the lesson that a defeated DSA-backed Democratic primary campaign points toward "left independent candidates," a conclusion also advanced in **"Support Left Independent Candidates—Build An Alternative To The Two Corporate Parties"** (August 2). The formulation is significant for what it omits: no party, no programme, no international perspective — only better candidates. **"Interview: Union Nurse In Maine On Organizing Against ICE Brutality"** (August 17) presents the existing union structures as the natural vehicle for resistance, with no examination of the union apparatus's integration into the Democratic Party and the state.

**Assessment:** Perpetual oscillation between running inside the Democratic Party and calling for independents, with the trade union bureaucracy treated throughout as an ally.

### Socialist Worker / SWP (UK)

**"Speaking out for the new left party"** develops the SWP's orientation to the Corbyn-Sultana formation — a left-reformist parliamentary project that the SWP joins while offering tactical advice, reproducing the pattern of its Respect and Left Unity interventions. **"Strikers fighting for their safety against climate catastrophe"** and coverage of the indefinite strike begun August 10 by Institute of Cancer Research workers provide useful strike reporting, but the strikes are consistently framed as pressure to be applied on Labour and the union leaderships rather than as the basis for organising independently of them.

**Assessment:** Movementist agitation subordinated to a parliamentary regroupment project.

### The Communist / RCP (IMT)

**"Jason Arday: Killed by the racist establishment media"** (August 17) frames the death of the Cambridge academic through the category of racism as an autonomous force, a formulation that substitutes racial for class analysis. **"Air 'con': Climate culture war heats up as temperatures rise"** (August 17) and **"Artificial intelligence or artificial scarcity?"** (August 14) offer general propaganda against capitalism without any concrete programme for the workers involved. **"Defend the Cuban revolution! Defeat US imperialism!"** (August 13) uncritically defends the Cuban state apparatus, abandoning any Marxist analysis of the Stalinist bureaucracy's role.

**Assessment:** The RCP's recruitment-oriented propagandism combines abstract calls for "world revolution" with adaptation to identity politics and to Castroism.

**No direct attacks on the ICFI or WSWS were noted in the past 24 hours.** The most significant convergence with the bourgeois press remains the shared treatment of Democratic Party primaries as the measure of American political radicalisation — a framework Jacobin, Left Voice, PSL and Socialist Alternative all share with the *New York Times*.

### 1. Ebola in the Congo becomes the deadliest outbreak in the country's history

The Bundibugyo outbreak has now killed more than 2,300 people and is spreading five times faster than any previous Ebola epidemic, killing roughly one person every 30 minutes — and it has received a fraction of the coverage devoted to far smaller health events in the imperialist centres. This demands treatment as a class question: the collapse of health infrastructure in eastern Congo is the direct product of a century of imperialist plunder, the destruction of the state by proxy warfare over cobalt and coltan, and the dismantling of global pandemic coordination since COVID-19.
