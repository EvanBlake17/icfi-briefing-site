### 2. IMF forecasts outright contraction across the Gulf as it warns Big Tech's AI debt could trigger a crisis

The International Monetary Fund's October World Economic Outlook, titled "Resilience under Strain, Urgent Choices," was released at the annual meetings in Bangkok. It raised global growth slightly to 3.1%, from 3.0% in the previous outlook. But it projects inflation rising to 4.7% after the oil shock from the war on Iran. The Middle East and Central Asia is now forecast to shrink 0.3% in 2026, down from 1.9% growth projected in April, the only region in the world expected to contract. The Fund describes a "split global economy." A 29% jump in AI-related trade lifts total world trade growth to 8.3%, while energy importers face higher bills and weaker demand. It warns that the expansion depends on "a small number of firms, sectors and economies," and that the global economy could "soon test the limits of resilience."

The IMF's Global Financial Stability Report, released Tuesday, singles out the debt-financed AI buildout as a systemic risk. It warns that "circular" financing among AI firms creates "significant 'wrong-way risk'" if a key firm fails. Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva said global debt-to-GDP is set to exceed 100%. She warned that high debt in the advanced economies limits governments' ability to address the cost of living and could produce unrest "maybe on the street," and she called for "consolidation plans." As Nick Beams wrote on the WSWS, the IMF's prescription for the major economies is the austerity it has imposed on poor countries for decades.

Key facts:
- **Gulf collapse:** Projected 2026 GDP is Kuwait −16.7%, Iraq −16%, Qatar −15.6%, Bahrain −11.3%, Iran −7.6% and Saudi Arabia −3.3%. The Saudi decline is partly offset by exports through the East-West pipeline.
- **Major economies:** US +2.3%, China +4.5%, eurozone +1.1%, emerging Asia +5.1%, sub-Saharan Africa +4.5%, Latin America +2.3%.
- **AI leverage:** Analyst Bret Jensen, quoted by Common Dreams, puts the debt of the five largest hyperscalers (Oracle, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, Alphabet) at more than $1.3 trillion on balance sheet, plus about $3 trillion in off-balance-sheet commitments.
- **Austerity on the poor:** An Oxfam report released Monday finds that 24 of 28 countries with IMF loans have been required to impose austerity. Bolivia must cut up to 8.5% of GDP, roughly twice its health budget. Sierra Leone's required adjustment is more than five times its annual health budget.
- **Bond market:** Pimco's CIO Dan Ivascyn calls a 6% US 10-year yield "feasible." The US now spends roughly one dollar in five of federal revenue on interest.

### Cholera triples in three weeks in war-torn Yemen

The World Health Organization reports that suspected cholera cases in Yemen have tripled in three weeks, from 217 in the week of September 6–12 to 647 in the week of September 27–October 3. There have been 8,034 suspected cases and 27 deaths so far in 2026, a quarter of them in the past four weeks. Five southwestern governorates, Abyan, Lahij, al-Dhalea, Taiz and Aden, account for 92% of cases, and the disease has reached remote camps in Hadramout. Regional emergency director Annette Heinzelmann warned that "Families fleeing conflict are now facing another threat from diseases that can be prevented and treated." The 2017 epidemic, during the previous Saudi war, infected hundreds of thousands and killed at least 2,000.
