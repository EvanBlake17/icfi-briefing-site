### 1. Iran sets six conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Supreme National Security Council, headed by Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, published a six-point list of conditions the United States must meet before Tehran will permit the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, closed since the outbreak of the US-Israeli war on Iran on February 28. The announcement came as Iranian and Omani negotiators signalled they were close to a separate technical agreement on managing traffic through the waterway — an agreement which, Iranian officials stressed, would not by itself reopen the strait.

President Masoud Pezeshkian publicly backed a deal, telling state media that "now is the best time for an agreement" and arguing that Iran negotiates from a position of strength: "There is cohesion, strength, and unity in the country, and as far as I know, Iran is considered victorious and powerful in this war." The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps confirmed that reopening remains contingent on US acceptance of the six conditions and is formally separate from the Oman track.

Key facts:
- The six conditions: an end to US threats and "insults to national and religious values"; a permanent halt to attacks on Iran and its allies in Lebanon, Palestine, Yemen and Iraq; lifting of the US naval blockade and withdrawal of US forces; compensation for damage from "two imposed wars"; lifting of sanctions; and release of frozen Iranian assets.
- These terms go substantially beyond the June 17 US-Iran memorandum, which envisaged phased reciprocal steps. Tehran now presents them as preconditions.
- An unnamed US official said the naval blockade would be lifted only once "commercial shipping through Hormuz was restored without impediments" — an explicitly conditional formulation that inverts Iran's sequencing.
- The UAE reported that a commercial vessel was targeted in the same period, underscoring that the waterway remains effectively closed to normal traffic.
- Roughly a fifth of world seaborne oil normally transits Hormuz; Brent closed the week at about $83.55/bbl.

### 2. US Senate passes sweeping Russia-Iran sanctions bill 86-11

The Senate approved sweeping new sanctions legislation targeting Russia and Iran by 86 votes to 11, the largest bipartisan war measure of the year. Republicans voted 49-1, with Rand Paul of Kentucky the sole GOP dissenter and three absent. Democrats split 37-10.

The bill, originally authored by the late Senator Lindsey Graham and Senator Richard Blumenthal (D-Connecticut), extends Iran sanctions for five years and authorises measures against Russia's "shadow fleet" of oil tankers — a provision carrying the direct risk of naval interdiction of Russian-linked shipping. Blumenthal framed the vote as a message to Moscow: "Today we say to Vladimir Putin: You will not conquer Ukraine."

Patrick Martin's WSWS Perspective column dissected the Democratic minority's position, noting that "not one of them opposed the sanctions. Their objection was to the tariff provision" attached to the bill. When an amendment to strip the tariff language came to the floor it failed 32-64, with sixteen Democrats joining forty-eight Republicans to defeat it. Martin's conclusion: "twenty of them registered a toothless protest and then voted for the war."

Key facts:
- Bernie Sanders was among the ten Democratic "no" votes; none of the ten based their opposition on the sanctions or the war.
- The shadow-fleet provisions authorise action against tankers carrying Russian crude, raising the prospect of physical interception at sea by US or NATO vessels.
- The five-year Iran sanctions extension arrives while the US is in active military confrontation with Tehran over Hormuz.
- Martin situates the legislation within the WSWS's standing analysis that "the purpose of the US-NATO war in Ukraine ... is to prepare the conquest and dismemberment of Russia itself by the imperialist powers."

### 3. Saudi Arabia, Türkiye and Pakistan sign Mecca mutual defence pact

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif signed the "Mecca Joint Defence Agreement" on August 7, establishing a collective-defence clause stipulating that "any armed attack against any one of the three States shall be regarded as an attack against them all." Turkish officials spent Saturday insisting the pact is not directed at Iran.

The agreement binds together NATO's second-largest standing army, the world's largest oil exporter and the only Muslim-majority state with nuclear weapons. It was signed hours after a senior Saudi official told Reuters the kingdom was bracing for further attacks from Iranian-aligned forces in Yemen and Iraq.

Key facts:
- Houthi forces claimed a fresh attack on a Saudi Aramco oil site over the weekend, continuing a campaign that began July 25 with strikes on Yanbu and the 400,000 bpd Jazan refinery on the Red Sea coast.
- Jazan's integrated gasification complex and tank farm were damaged on July 27 and the refinery shut down; a fire broke out again at the site early Sunday, with no injuries reported.
- Also on July 27, drones targeted Abqaiq, the world's largest crude stabilisation facility, in Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province.
- These are the first Houthi strikes on Saudi territory since 2022 and open a second front alongside the Red Sea shipping campaign.
- The Atlantic Council characterised the pact as "an inflection point in the emerging multipolar order in the Middle East and South Asia" — an admission from within the US foreign-policy establishment that Washington's regional clients are hedging against American decline.

### 4. Car bomb in Cauca a day after Colombia's Trump-backed president is sworn in

A car bomb detonated Saturday at a toll plaza on the Pan-American Highway in southwestern Colombia, one day after Abelardo de la Espriella — a right-wing lawyer and political newcomer endorsed by Donald Trump — was sworn in as president in Cali. Two security guards suffered minor injuries. The army attributed the attack to two FARC dissident groups that rejected the 2016 peace accord, saying the vehicle had been abandoned by a man who fled on a motorcycle.

The department said it "stands ready to support the de la Espriella Administration's efforts to defeat narcoterrorist and criminal organizations" — the same formula used to justify the Venezuela campaign.

Key facts:
- De la Espriella campaigned explicitly on "defeating narco-terrorism" and reversing the reformist agenda of outgoing president Gustavo Petro.
- On August 4, SOUTHCOM announced the conversion of Joint Task Force–Southern Spear into a permanent Joint Task Force–Western Hemisphere (JTF-WHEM), described by commander Gen. Francis Donovan as "SOUTHCOM's action arm for confronting threats across the hemisphere."
- SOUTHCOM says 18 hemispheric nations will collaborate in the expanded structure, which shifts operations from maritime interdiction to inland land operations.
- The pledge follows the US military operation that deposed and detained Nicolás Maduro and dozens of lethal strikes on vessels accused of drug smuggling.

### 5. US payrolls fall 23,000 in July, first decline since February

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Friday that nonfarm payrolls fell by 23,000 in July against a consensus forecast of a gain of 83,000 — the first monthly decline since February 2026. Revisions cut a combined 103,000 jobs from the May and June figures. The unemployment rate registered 4.1 percent, below the 4.2 percent expected, a divergence that reflects labour-force withdrawal rather than strength in hiring.

Financial markets responded to the deterioration in employment as good news: the S&P 500 closed at a record on expectations that the Federal Reserve will move toward rate cuts. The inverse relationship between the jobs data and equity prices is the clearest possible statement of whose interests the market represents.

Key facts:
- July's decline breaks a five-month run of positive payroll prints and comes amid tariff-driven cost increases across manufacturing and construction supply chains.
- The scale of the downward revisions — 103,000 over two months — means the previously reported spring "recovery" in hiring largely did not occur.
- The unemployment rate falling while payrolls contract indicates workers exiting the labour force, not being absorbed into it.
- Contract expirations concentrated this month include 24,600 National Nurses United members at Kaiser in Northern California and 20,000 Verizon workers in the Northeast and mid-Atlantic.

### 6. British Columbia declares state of emergency as 20,000 flee Okanagan fire

Premier David Eby declared a provincial state of emergency Saturday as the Bald Range wildfire tore through communities along Okanagan Lake, destroying homes, trapping residents and forcing more than 20,000 people to evacuate. First reported Friday evening, the fire grew to roughly 50 square kilometres within hours and covered the 15 kilometres from the small community of Faulder to Summerland at extraordinary speed.

Key facts:
- The entire town of Summerland, population about 12,000, was ordered evacuated, along with roughly 8,000 people in and around Peachland.
- The Bald Range fire had burned about 9,500 hectares (23,500 acres) by Saturday.
- More than 100 wildfires are burning across British Columbia, nearly half classified as out of control, amid hot, windy conditions and prolonged drought.
- In the United States, a pilot and a crew member were killed in a helicopter crash while fighting a fire in Utah.
- In southern France, a wildfire near Narbonne burned approximately 100 hectares as an extreme heat episode gripped western Europe.

The concentration of catastrophic fire in the Okanagan — the same corridor devastated by the McDougall Creek fire — reflects the failure of every capitalist government to fund forest management, evacuation infrastructure and firefighting capacity commensurate with a warming climate, while military budgets expand without limit.

### Turkish parliamentary committee approves PKK dissolution bill

A Turkish parliamentary committee approved on Friday a 12-article bill, the "Law on Strengthening National Solidarity and Social Integration," submitted by the ruling AKP on August 5 with the signatures of 360 lawmakers. The bill would suspend criminal investigations and court proceedings for five to ten years for PKK members not directly implicated in acts of violence, facilitate the return of supporters from abroad, and create oversight bodies for the dissolution process. It offers no amnesty.

The AKP, its MHP nationalist allies and the pro-Kurdish DEM Party all backed the measure; most opposition parties have voiced support. As Barış Demir wrote on the WSWS, the bill excludes pre-2005 convictions — which excludes Abdullah Öcalan himself — and demands the PKK's liquidation, formalised in the party's May 2025 self-dissolution, "offering nothing in return."

### Explosive-laden drone found beside Ukrainian aircraft at Leipzig airport

German investigators confirmed that Semtex and PETN were used to arm a drone discovered next to a Ukrainian Antonov aircraft at Leipzig/Halle Airport, one of NATO's principal logistics hubs for weapons shipments to Ukraine. The detonator failed, permitting forensic recovery. An airport worker discovered the device and kicked it out of the air. Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt called it a "new quality of threat" and a "hybrid attack scenario."

- A DHL cargo aircraft separately aborted a landing after colliding with an unidentified object at 400 metres and landed damaged at Hanover, after the airport's counter-drone system detected two drones.
- German officials publicly attributed the Leipzig incident to Russia without producing evidence. Peter Schwarz noted on the WSWS that officials privately consider Ukrainian involvement, drawing the parallel to the Nord Stream sabotage.
- The incident echoes the 2024 parcel fires at Leipzig, Warsaw and Birmingham, over which five defendants are on trial in Vilnius.
- Berlin is using the episode to accelerate a "rearmament" of drone defence and broader military expansion.

### US measles cases near 35-year high with elimination status under review

The CDC reported 2,465 confirmed measles cases in the United States as of August 6, 2026 — already exceeding the 2,289 recorded in all of 2025 and approaching levels not seen since the early 1990s. Some 94 percent of cases (2,309 of 2,465) are outbreak-associated; two-thirds are in people aged 19 and under; and 93 percent of patients were unvaccinated or of unknown vaccination status.

A national verification committee meets in mid-August to determine whether the United States has lost the measles elimination status it has held since 2000. Loss of that status would be a formal admission that the destruction of public health infrastructure, the gutting of vaccination programmes and the promotion of anti-vaccine ideology at cabinet level have reversed one of the signal public health achievements of the twentieth century.

### COVID "Nimbus" variant dominant, growing in 43 states

The NB.1.8.1 "Nimbus" Omicron subvariant is now the dominant strain nationwide. As of August 5, COVID-19 infections were growing or likely growing in 43 states and declining in none. CDC forecasting projected weekly hospital admissions climbing from roughly 1,200 to 1,600 by early August. Severity appears unchanged from prior strains, but the late-summer surge is straining a respiratory surveillance system that has been progressively defunded.

### H5N1 reaches Australian seabirds in first mass-mortality event

Australia confirmed H5N1 in a mass-mortality event involving 49 dead and 35 sick terns off Adelaide — the country's first major wild-bird die-off from the panzootic, which had until now largely spared the continent. Victoria has logged 53 positive cases across 37 investigations as of August 8. In the United States, CDC surveillance showed no unusual human influenza activity as of August 7, though the virus remains widespread in wild birds and continues to circulate in dairy cattle herds.

### Study finds exercise guidelines drastically understate the dose needed

An observational study published in the *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, following participants for an average of 7.8 years and recording 1,233 cardiovascular events including 156 heart attacks and 92 strokes, found that meeting the standard 150 minutes per week of physical activity was associated with only an 8-9 percent reduction in cardiovascular risk. Substantial protection — greater than 30 percent risk reduction — required 560 to 610 minutes per week, a level achieved by only 12 percent of participants. The findings suggest current guidelines require major revision toward higher and more individualised targets. They also underline a class question: 9 to 10 hours of weekly exercise is not available to workers on twelve-hour shifts or holding multiple jobs.

### EU AI Act enforcement begins

As of August 2, 2026, the EU AI Office and national authorities began enforcing the AI Act. Article 50 transparency obligations — including labelling of AI-generated content — are now in force, and high-risk system obligations covering credit scoring and insurance pricing are legally binding for any company operating in the EU, including the major US AI firms. Non-compliance can trigger fines up to €15 million or 3 percent of global annual turnover, whichever is higher. Enforcement capacity remains the open question: the AI Office is small relative to the firms it regulates, and the framework accepts the private ownership of the technology as given.

## Arts and culture

**William Orbit (1956-2026).** The death of the English producer and musician William Orbit at 69, announced this week after he died at home on July 23, removes one of the more genuinely inventive figures in late-twentieth-century popular music. Born William Wainwright in Hackney to a working-class London family, Orbit came out of the early-1980s independent scene and spent a decade making ambient and electronic records under the name Strange Cargo before his production work on Madonna's *Ray of Light* (1998) won three Grammys, including Best Pop Album. His achievement there was not commercial packaging but the opposite: he brought the textures of British ambient techno — long decaying reverbs, filtered analogue synthesis, an unhurried sense of space — into the centre of American mass-market pop, and in doing so gave a performer then widely written off as a spent commercial force the most artistically serious record of her career. His work on Blur's *13* (1999) served a similar function, opening up the band's guitar music to electronic dissolution at the moment Britpop's nationalist self-congratulation was collapsing. He also produced Beth Orton's debut and reworked Barber and Vivaldi on *Pieces in a Modern Style*. Orbit's career is a reminder that the most durable popular art of the period came from figures working at the margins of the industry rather than its promotional centre.

## Pseudo-left press review

- [Jacobin / DSA](#jacobin--dsa) — combines a correct anti-imperialist line on Cuba with a technocratic regulatory programme on inequality that never leaves the framework of capitalist reform
- [Left Voice](#left-voice) — the sharpest formal case against the "dirty break," but reduces class independence to an electoral-organisational question
- [Liberation News / PSL](#liberation-news--psl) — celebrates a Democratic primary result as an anti-Zionist victory, channelling opposition to genocide back into the party prosecuting it
- [Socialist Alternative](#socialist-alternative) — anti-Democratic in form, reformist in programme; police "accountability" demands framed within the capitalist state
- [Socialist Worker / SWP (UK)](#socialist-worker--swp-uk) — pressures the new Burnham Labour government to spend differently rather than opposing Labour as a party of British imperialism
- [Socialist Appeal / RCP (IMT)](#socialist-appeal--rcp-imt) — revolutionary phraseology on the British state combined with an inter-tendency polemic against the SWP that avoids programmatic questions

### Jacobin / DSA

**"After Blocking Fuel, the US Turns to Cuba's Food Supply"** develops an anti-imperialist critique of the blockade, adopting the "silent Gaza" framing. The article is factually useful and its opposition to the blockade is unambiguous. Its limitation is structural: Jacobin criticises US foreign policy in the abstract while its entire domestic orientation is toward the Democratic Party, which has administered the blockade through every presidency including Obama's and Biden's. No call is made for industrial action, and no conclusion is drawn about the party Jacobin's political milieu works within.

**"Don't Just Tax the Rich — Attack Inequality at the Root"** argues that taxation alone cannot resolve inequality and that structural regulation of markets and finance is required. This is a technocratic social-democratic programme: the "root" of inequality is located in insufficiently regulated market design rather than in the private ownership of the means of production. There is no call for independent working-class action; the implied agent is a reforming legislature.

**"The Fight for Abortion Rights Was Won in the Streets"** credits mass mobilisation, mutual aid and telehealth networks rather than electoral channels for preserving abortion access post-*Roe*. This is the more left-sounding pole of Jacobin's output, but the "streets" here are invoked as pressure on Democratic legislators, not as the basis of an independent political movement.

*Assessment:* Jacobin's output continues to combine correct criticisms of imperialism and inequality with a programme that presupposes the Democratic Party as the vehicle. The function is to keep radicalising layers of workers and youth oriented toward that party.

### Left Voice

**"Kshama Sawant's Third-Place Finish Shows the Space for a Party of Our Own"** treats Sawant's independent run as evidence for the viability of a workers' party outside the Democratic ballot line. **"August Primaries: Progressive Challengers Continue to Threaten Democratic Party Establishment"** argues the "dirty break" strategy fails because the Democratic Party absorbs and neutralises its progressive wing. **"SOUTHCOM Announces New Military Campaign, Expanding Land Operations in Latin America"** correctly identifies the narco-terrorism pretext as cover for building regional repressive capacity.

Left Voice's formal opposition to work inside the Democratic Party is the clearest on the American pseudo-left. The problem lies in what class independence is taken to consist of: an electoral and organisational break, rather than a struggle over programme and historical perspective. The Morenoite tradition Left Voice descends from has repeatedly constructed "broad" parties on precisely this basis — the FIT-U in Argentina being the operative model — that then function as left pressure on bourgeois governments. Nothing in this output addresses the theoretical questions on which the Fourth International split.

*Assessment:* Correct on the immediate electoral question, unserious on the programmatic foundations that would make an independent party a revolutionary one.

### Liberation News / PSL

**"Michigan primary: A huge defeat for AIPAC and the Israel Lobby"** presents a Democratic primary victory over an AIPAC-backed candidate as a strategic blow against Zionism. This is the clearest instance in the day's scan of channelling mass opposition to genocide back into the party that armed and funded it. The framing also carries an analytical error the WSWS has repeatedly identified: locating the source of US Middle East policy in a "lobby" rather than in the strategic interests of American imperialism, which displaces class analysis with a conspiracy of influence.

**"PSL Statement: Defend our Haitian neighbors! We've stopped ICE before and can do it again!"** calls for mass direct action in defence of Haitian TPS holders and demands ICE's abolition — a genuinely mobilising orientation. **"One month after Lorenzo's murder, the fight for justice continues"** covers organising around an ICE killing in Houston.

*Assessment:* PSL combines real street mobilisation with an electoral orientation that consistently returns to Democratic primaries. The two are not in tension for the PSL because it has no conception of political independence as a matter of programme.

### Socialist Alternative

**"Support Left Independent Candidates—Build An Alternative To The Two Corporate Parties"** rejects both capitalist parties and backs Sawant's independent campaign. The programme advanced — Medicare for All, rent control, abolishing ICE — remains entirely within the framework of capitalism; the "alternative to the two corporate parties" is a left-reformist party, not a revolutionary one. **"Justice For Corey Ruiz—Fight Police Brutality With A One-Day Citywide Shutdown"** calls for a Madison, Wisconsin shutdown after a police killing, demanding the officer be jailed and the department defunded — militant tactics attached to demands that presuppose the capitalist state can be made to police itself.

*Assessment:* Anti-Democratic in form and reformist in content, with SAlt's characteristic substitution of tactical militancy for programmatic clarity.

### Socialist Worker / SWP (UK)

**"Burnham commits to increase defence spending by billions"** attacks the new Labour prime minister for funding Trident renewal after invoking "difficult choices" on social care. The criticism is correct and the juxtaposition damning, but the implicit demand is that Labour govern differently rather than that workers break from it. **"Thousands imprisoned 'arbitrarily' despite Labour's prisoner release scheme"** defends prisoners amid the capacity crisis, largely by correcting the "Labour is soft on crime" media narrative. **"Why the left should stand against immigration controls"** addresses itself to "the left" as a milieu — the SWP's habitual mode of front-group recruitment.

*Assessment:* The SWP's orientation remains pressure politics directed at Labour and at an undefined "left," consistent with a tradition that abandoned the theory of permanent revolution for state capitalism and Labour entrism.

### Socialist Appeal / RCP (IMT)

**"Another government, same system – Britain needs a revolution!"** correctly states that replacing Labour leaders changes nothing and calls for overthrowing the system — the sharpest formal revolutionary language of the day. **"Stand Up to Opportunism – Why the SWP gets everything wrong on racism"** is an inter-tendency polemic against the SWP's Stand Up To Racism front. **"From 'beer and sandwiches' to 'champagne and canapés'"** covers union leadership changes, contrasting rank-and-file interests with bureaucratic accommodation.

The RCP's revolutionary rhetoric coexists with an organisational practice of rapid recruitment on the basis of general anti-capitalist sentiment, and with a Grantite lineage of decades of Labour entrism that today's phraseology does not settle accounts with.

*Assessment:* No article in the day's scan attacked the ICFI, Trotskyism or the WSWS directly; the sharpest inter-tendency polemic was Socialist Appeal against the SWP over anti-racism method. Notably, none of the six tendencies took an openly campist position on the Iran war, and none drew the connection between the Senate sanctions vote and the war drive against Russia.
