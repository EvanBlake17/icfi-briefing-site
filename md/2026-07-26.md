### US pauses Iran bombing after 13 nights as White House weighs "major" escalation

For the first time in two weeks, the US military announced no new strikes on Iran on Friday and Saturday, following 13 consecutive nights of bombardment. The pause came after a White House meeting at which Vice President JD Vance and Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen. Dan Caine reportedly raised concerns about further escalation. Trump told reporters Friday that he thinks Iran would "love to make a deal" but that he does not "think they're ready to," while separately telling Axios he was weighing "a massive attack, bigger than ever before."

Andre Damon reported on the WSWS that over 1,000 sites have been struck in the two-week escalation, on top of roughly 13,000 sites hit during the opening campaign of February 28 to April 8, and that a former Trump envoy is now publicly demanding a ground invasion of Iran.

Key facts:

- Iran's health ministry reports 59 killed and 666 wounded since the war resumed in late June. A missile strike on Ahvaz killed four and injured five.
- The Pentagon revised its official US war death toll down from 18 to 14 and its wounded count from 482 to 462, attributing the change to "a temporary data disruption" (spokesman Sean Parnell). Retired Maj. Gen. Paul Eaton called it "statistical sleight of hand."

- Approximately 50,000 US troops are in the region alongside two carriers and nearly 20 warships in the Arabian Sea. B-2 and B-52 crews are on alert; F-16s have been moved from Germany and F-35s from Britain; more than 150 additional medics have been sent to Landstuhl.
- US gasoline is near $4.10/gallon and diesel is up over 60 percent year-on-year.

### Wildfires force more than 250,000 evacuations across France and Spain

Wildfires driven by a prolonged heatwave across the western Mediterranean have produced what French officials describe as the largest peacetime evacuation in the country's history. Some 57,000 people were moved overnight in the Gironde, bringing that department's total to 167,000 and the combined Gironde–Landes figure to 197,000. French Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said about 98,000 hectares have burned in France so far in 2026, a national record, with fires still uncontained in the Gironde, the Landes and the Var.

Key facts:

- At least 15 civilians and three firefighters have been killed across the region as of July 25.
- Two fires west of Madrid merged on July 24 into what the regional government calls the worst wildfire in the Madrid region's recorded history.
- Fires have burned across Spain, Portugal, France, Greece, Italy and Turkey; smoke has disrupted the Tour de France.
- Scientists attribute the scale to a punishing sequence of heatwaves that has left Mediterranean soils tinder-dry — the same mechanism identified in recent European heat-mortality research.

The disaster is unfolding while European governments direct hundreds of billions into rearmament. Firefighting capacity, forest management and rural emergency services across southern Europe have been degraded by successive rounds of austerity.

### ICC member states vote to remove chief prosecutor Karim Khan

The Assembly of States Parties of the International Criminal Court voted on July 24 to remove chief prosecutor Karim Khan over findings of "serious misconduct and serious breach of duty" — the first removal of a sitting ICC prosecutor in the court's history. Khan, who issued the arrest warrants against Benjamin Netanyahu and Yoav Gallant, had been on leave since May 2025 over allegations of sexual misconduct with a female aide, which he denies.

Key facts:

- The vote was 82 in favor of removal, 13 against, 15 abstentions, out of 125 member states.
- Khan's chief lawyer said the decision was "unsupported by evidence," reached through a process in which Khan was "never given a fair opportunity to be heard," and "in defiance of the conclusions of independent judges."
- The Trump administration has sanctioned ICC prosecutors and officials and has stated its intention to "dismantle the ICC altogether."

Whatever the merits of the misconduct findings, the removal decapitates the office that issued the Netanyahu warrants at precisely the moment when the imperialist powers are demanding the warrants be shelved. The episode underscores the impotence of international juridical bodies against the interests of the major powers — the central point of Joseph Kishore's WSWS analysis of Mamdani's retreat on the same question (see WSWS coverage below).

Iranian missiles have struck the tankers Al Bahyah and Mombasa B and the Qatari-flagged LNG carrier Al Rekayyat, and multiple Greek-owned Dynacom tankers were hit on July 19–20.

- The World Bank has warned global growth could fall to 1.3 percent from 2.9 percent if the disruption persists.
- The FAO warns the conflict will deepen global hunger through energy and fertilizer prices (see International developments).

### US measles cases hit 35-year high

The CDC recorded 2,318 confirmed measles cases in the United States as of July 23, surpassing the 2,289 cases recorded in all of 2025 with more than five months of the year remaining. It is the highest annual count since 1991 and the second consecutive record-breaking year since elimination was declared in 2000.

Key facts:

- 45 jurisdictions have reported cases. 35 new outbreaks began in 2026, and 93 percent of confirmed cases (2,153 of 2,318) are outbreak-associated — 782 from 2026 outbreaks and 1,371 from outbreaks that began in 2025.
- 93 percent of those infected are unvaccinated; the overwhelming majority are under 20.
- No deaths have been recorded so far in 2026.
- Officials identify summer travel as a high-risk transmission window and warn the count will continue climbing.

The resurgence follows the dismantling of federal vaccine advisory structures, the gutting of local public health budgets, and the promotion of anti-vaccine positions from within the Department of Health and Human Services itself.

### Ebola outbreak in DR Congo becomes fastest-spreading on record

The Bundibugyo ebolavirus outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has passed 1,000 deaths faster than any previous Ebola epidemic. Ministry of Health figures as of July 21 record 2,536 confirmed cases, 1,033 confirmed deaths and 506 recoveries — a case fatality rate of 40.7 percent among confirmed cases. The 1,000-death threshold was crossed in 67 days; the 2014–16 West Africa epidemic took 142.

- Ituri province accounts for nearly 90 percent of confirmed cases and more than 80 percent of deaths. Cases have also been reported in North Kivu, South Kivu, Haut-Uele and Tshopo, plus 20 cases and two deaths in Uganda and one case in France.
- Contact tracing reaches 82 percent of identified contacts, short of the WHO's 90–95 percent target; Africa CDC reports that fewer than 9 percent of contacts that *should* be traced are actually monitored. WHO modeling suggests true case numbers are two to four times the confirmed count.
- The joint WHO–Africa CDC $518 million response plan faces a funding shortfall of over $400 million.
- Ituri has been in armed conflict for more than 30 years; over 900,000 people there live in displacement camps.
- Africa CDC director-general Jean Kaseya: "People are dying, because there are no vaccines, no medicine and no funding."

Benjamin Mateus notes on the WSWS that US biodefense funding has prioritized Zaire ebolavirus countermeasures over Bundibugyo, leaving no licensed vaccine or therapeutic for the strain now circulating — a direct consequence of organizing pharmaceutical research around national-security priorities rather than human need.

### Nolan's *The Odyssey* opens to record numbers and divided criticism

Christopher Nolan's adaptation of Homer, released July 17, has taken more than $264 million globally in its opening weekend, surpassing *Oppenheimer*. Made for $250 million — the most expensive production of Nolan's career — and shot entirely on IMAX 70mm cameras, it stars Matt Damon as Odysseus and Anne Hathaway as Penelope, with Tom Holland, Robert Pattinson, Lupita Nyong'o, Zendaya and Charlize Theron. Nolan has said his next film is "at least" three years away.

That a studio committed a quarter of a billion dollars to a version of an ancient epic about a soldier's ruinous decade-long return from a war of conquest is itself significant. The Homeric material is saturated with the human costs of war, the destruction of households, and the moral corrosion of the returning warrior — themes with obvious contemporary resonance. Whether Nolan's treatment engages them or converts them into spectacle is the substance of the critical division. Writing in *Jacobin*, Eileen Jones argues the film drains classical mythology of its vitality, reducing the poem to technically overwhelming but emotionally inert set-pieces. The scale of the commercial success — in a period of collapsing theatrical attendance — also indicates a real audience appetite for serious material treated seriously, against the industry's own franchise-driven assumptions.

### A *T. rex* sells for $50.1 million as fossils become financial assets

A 12.5-foot *Tyrannosaurus rex* skeleton nicknamed "Gus," recovered in South Dakota from 2021 by commercial collector Thomas Heitkamp, sold at Sotheby's New York for $50.1 million to an anonymous buyer — a record for any paleontological specimen. Seven bidders competed. The previous record was $44.6 million for a stegosaurus in 2024; "Stan," another T. rex, went for $31.8 million in 2020 and now resides in Abu Dhabi. The last time a major specimen went to a public institution was 1997, when the Field Museum acquired "Sue" for $8.36 million.

The Society of Vertebrate Paleontology warned that private ownership forecloses research: "Modern research techniques are still revealing new information from fossils collected more than a century ago." Philip Guelpa's WSWS review draws the connection between the transformation of irreplaceable scientific material into a speculative asset class for the ultra-rich and the simultaneous starvation of publicly funded research.

Pharmacists now handle prescriptions, immunizations and insurance administration with staffing levels that a UFCW survey found produced medication errors reported by 11.9 percent of respondents. Vons pharmacist Craig Chally: "The current workload is not sustainable. Safe staffing protects patients." The article notes the UFCW's practice of authorizing strikes and then allowing deadlines to pass, and the deliberate separation of the pharmacists from the far larger grocery workforce in the same chains.

### Sturgis Hospital closes in rural Michigan — Riley Ward

Sturgis Hospital, serving a town of 11,000, closed June 19 after more than a century of operation with less than three days' notice. Three hundred workers lost their jobs and health insurance simultaneously with the public announcement. A nurse with 38 years' service: "I honestly thought that I'd wake up and this was a bad dream."

The financial trajectory: 2024 revenue of $15.4 million against $23.2 million in expenses, an $8 million operating deficit, and negative net assets of $48 million. The hospital had already cut 140 positions over the preceding decade, shuttered birthing and hospice programs in 2018, and closed all 84 inpatient beds in 2023 before terminating emergency services. Residents now face 20–30 minute drives, with the nearest facility out-of-network for most Michigan insurance plans. Ward frames the closure as the local expression of a national policy: rural healthcare, unprofitable by construction, is being liquidated while Medicaid is cut and military spending expands.

### 5 workers killed in helmet factory fire in Sri Lanka — Wasantha Wijesiri and Dehin Wasantha

Five workers died in a fire at the Nimato helmet factory in the Katuwana Industrial Zone, 25 km southeast of Colombo, on July 21. The blaze began around 9:45 a.m. and engulfed the three-storey building within minutes. Four young workers from rural agricultural districts — two aged 19, two aged 24 — died along with 54-year-old production supervisor Mario Edward. Bodies required DNA identification.

The factory stored PVC and polystyrene foam without containment, had failed to renew its Environmental Protection Licence after 2024, and had no emergency response plan. The Central Environmental Authority had instructed management to remedy violations as recently as July 2; nothing was done. The nearest fire brigade was 15 km away and took nearly an hour to arrive; the industrial zone, employing some 2,000 workers, has no fire station. The authors locate the deaths in the systematic dismantling of inspection: 25 qualified engineers are responsible for nearly 30,000 factories nationwide, under an IMF austerity program that treats regulation as a cost to be eliminated.

### Break the collusion between the union and management at Volkswagen! — VW Action Committee

## Pseudo-left press review

- [Jacobin / DSA](#jacobin-dsa) — endorses Mamdani's Netanyahu arrest pledge as a model of socialist practice while its Latin American edition argues for a popular front with Peronism
- [Left Voice](#left-voice) — militant phraseology on Iran and ICE combined with adaptation to Mamdani's administration and to the Democratic Party's electoral orbit
- [Liberation News / PSL](#liberation-news-psl) — anti-imperialist rhetoric organized around defence of bourgeois nationalist states rather than the international working class
- [Socialist Alternative / ISA](#socialist-alternative-isa) — calls for "mass movements" and left electoral vehicles without addressing the union apparatus that suppresses them
- [Socialist Worker / SWP (UK)](#socialist-worker-swp-uk) — strike solidarity coverage that never poses the question of the trade union leaderships
- [Socialist Appeal / RCP (IMT)](#socialist-appeal-rcp-imt) — orientation to the Labour and union left and to recruiting from the Reform milieu

### Jacobin / DSA

**"Mamdani Is Right. Netanyahu Belongs Behind Bars"** (Ben Burgis) argues the New York mayor correctly identified Netanyahu as a genocidaire warranting arrest. The article treats the arrest of a head of government by a municipal executive as a serious political programme, and passes over Mamdani's immediate retreat — the actual political content of the episode. The framework is juridical: genocide is presented as the crime of an individual, remediable through the courts of the imperialist powers, rather than as the policy of the Israeli state backed by the full weight of American imperialism.

**"Yes, We Really Should Abolish the Senate"** (Ben Burgis) defends the abolition of the Senate as a democratic demand. The argument remains within the framework of constitutional engineering: the crisis of American democracy is presented as a design flaw in an eighteenth-century document rather than as the political expression of an oligarchy that has outgrown any democratic form.

**"India's 'Cockroach' Protest Movement Faces State Repression"** (Sajad Hameed, Suhail Bhat) documents state violence against students demanding educational accountability. The sympathetic reporting is not connected to any perspective for the movement beyond pressure on the Indian state — the Indian working class does not appear as an agency.

The day's output is oriented to the consolidation of the Mamdani administration as proof of the DSA's strategy of working through the Democratic Party. Note also that WSWS published a direct reply to *Jacobin América Latina*'s Martín Mosquera on the Popular Front question (summarized above) — a polemic that goes to the heart of *Jacobin*'s function in Latin America.

### Left Voice

**"Mamdani Halts NYPD Expansion, but NYC's Repressive Machine Remains Intact"** offers a partial criticism of the administration while presenting its measures as gains to be built on. The article does not draw the conclusion that a socialist cannot administer the capitalist state's repressive apparatus — it asks for more from the administration rather than opposing it.

**"ICE Killings in Houston and Maine Should Reignite a Combative Resistance to Trump's War on Immigrants"** calls for militant mass mobilization. The perspective is a "combative resistance" of movements and unions, not the construction of a revolutionary party; the union apparatuses that have blocked every serious mobilization are addressed as potential allies.

**"Chevalier's Win Reflects Growing Support for Immigrants, but Full Rights Will Be Won from Below"** treats a Democratic electoral result as evidence of a leftward shift, then appends the caveat that rights are won "from below" — the standard formula by which the PTS tradition combines adaptation to bourgeois electoralism with radical rhetoric.

Left Voice also carries **"Build a Mass International Movement against the Imperialist War in Iran"** and **"Betrayed by Its Allies, Hamas Accepts Imperialist Control of Gaza."** The orientation for the day: verbally uncompromising on the war, politically oriented to the movements and left Democratic milieu at home.

### Liberation News / PSL

The PSL's Iran material — including its statements that "Trump's unprovoked war against Iran is based on lies" and "Why Trump failed in his criminal war against Iran" — combines opposition to the bombing with political defence of the Iranian bourgeois-clerical regime, which is presented as an anti-imperialist actor rather than as a capitalist government whose suppression of the Iranian working class is the precondition for imperialism's advances.

Its ICE coverage argues that ICE terror "can be stopped — and restarted — at any moment" and that "it's up to the working class to end it for good." The formulation is correct in the abstract; the practical content is mobilization behind coalitions in which the PSL subordinates itself to Democratic-aligned organizations. On Cuba, the PSL's line remains unconditional defence of the Havana government, obscuring the bureaucracy's own preparations for capitalist restoration.

Overall orientation: Stalinist campism, in which the class struggle is replaced by a bloc of states against Washington.

### Socialist Alternative / ISA

Socialist Alternative's principal current statement, **"Middle East War: Capitalism Offers No Way Out,"** estimates over 10,000 dead and 50,000 injured in the war, overwhelmingly in Lebanon and Iran. The analysis correctly identifies capitalism as the source of the conflict but the conclusion is a call for mass movements and left electoral formations. There is no accounting for the role of the trade union apparatuses in suppressing anti-war action, and no international revolutionary perspective — the ISA's own history of splits is testament to the absence of a programmatic foundation.

### Socialist Worker / SWP (UK)

**"Get behind the cancer research strikers"** and **"East End Homes workers 'not backing down' in fight against pension theft"** provide useful factual reporting on live disputes. In neither case is the conduct of the union leadership examined; solidarity is called for in a way that leaves the apparatus's control of the disputes untouched. This is the constant of the SWP's industrial line: rank-and-file militancy as pressure on the bureaucracy rather than a struggle against it.

**"The spectre of socialism haunting America"** treats the growth of the DSA and the Mamdani administration as an advance for socialism, converging with *Jacobin* on the central question. **"Governments look the other way as thousands face nightmare fires"** attributes the wildfire catastrophe to right-wing politicians' cuts rather than to the capitalist system as such — an appeal for better government policy.

### Socialist Appeal / RCP (IMT)

**"Unison's new left leadership: A balance sheet so far"** assesses the new left leadership of the public-sector union UNISON. The framework is that the correct leadership can transform the union apparatus — the position the WSWS has opposed for decades, and which the IMT's own account of UNISON's record quietly refutes.

**"Reform voter to revolutionary communist"** and **"Farage's inroads in the unions: How should the labour movement fight Reform?"** address the growth of Reform UK in working-class areas. The recruitment orientation is notable, but the political answer offered is a militant version of the "labour movement" — that is, the same Labour Party and union structures whose betrayals produced the space Farage occupies.

**"Hundreds dead: Nottingham NHS inquiry exposes deadly maternity care crisis"** documents the maternity scandal but frames it as a failure of NHS management and funding, not of the Labour government's own austerity commitments.

The day's orientation is to the union and Labour left, combined with a recruitment appeal to workers moving right — a combination that expresses the IMT's abandonment of any independent revolutionary perspective in favour of national-reformist pressure politics.
