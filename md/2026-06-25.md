### 1. Iran ceasefire frays as Tehran rejects new Hormuz shipping routes

The fragile ceasefire ending the 2026 Iran war (Operation Epic Fury, Feb. 28–June 17) came under severe strain on June 25 as Iran declared newly proposed Strait of Hormuz shipping routes "unacceptable and dangerous," warning vessels against transiting without Iranian clearance. The declaration directly contradicts the US interpretation of the June 17 Islamabad Memorandum, signed by Trump at Versailles following the G7 and countersigned by Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian in Tehran. The 60-day window to negotiate a final settlement is now less than a month from expiry, and the dispute over who controls passage through the world's most important oil chokepoint has become the central flashpoint that could reignite open war.

The standoff over Hormuz — through which roughly a fifth of global oil trade passes — has direct consequences for global energy markets and, through them, for working people facing surging fuel and food prices worldwide. Iran closed the strait on June 20, citing Israeli truce violations; some 150 freight vessels were stalled during earlier blockades. On June 24, Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz signed an order designating the Central Bank of Iran a terrorist organization, a provocative measure that further inflamed an already brittle truce. The interlocking of the US-Iran ceasefire with the unresolved Israel-Lebanon war means that continued Israeli operations in Lebanon are the specific violations Iran invokes — making a durable settlement in one theater impossible without the other.

Key facts:
- The war killed an estimated 3,468–6,000 Iranian military personnel, 39 Israelis, 3,912 Lebanese, and 16 US soldiers; Arab states incurred roughly $120 billion in damages, and Iran faces an estimated $1 trillion in economic losses.
- US military costs reached $40 billion by June 21; Trump has requested an additional $87 billion from Congress, pushing the projected total above $127 billion in roughly four months.
- The IAEA inspections dispute (see International developments) is a parallel obstacle, as US hawks demand verified denuclearization before any sanctions relief.

The WSWS has covered the war throughout as a US-Israeli imperialist war over regional dominance and control of oil transit routes, in which the enormous human and financial costs are borne by workers in Iran, the Arab states, and the imperialist powers alike, while the oil and defense interests that drove the conflict reap the gains.

### 2. Ukraine strikes Crimea energy grid and air defenses in major escalation

Ukraine's Special Operations Forces carried out simultaneous drone operations across occupied Crimea, striking four aircraft hangars at Saky airfield, destroying two S-400 components and two Pantsir-S1 systems near Kerch, and hitting Hvardiiske airfield. Separate strikes on the Simferopol Power Station and Sevastopol's main electric substation caused outages affecting roughly half of Crimea. The SBU declared that "Russian occupiers are losing control of Crimea's skies," and characterized the peninsula as a "zone of constant losses" for Russian forces.

The operations represent the deepest sustained pressure on Russian rear-area infrastructure in months. Russia responded by repositioning air defense systems to protect Moscow and the Kerch Bridge — a redeployment that thins coverage across occupied territory and reflects the increasing strain on Russia's air-defense network. Moscow's largest oil refinery is unlikely to resume operations before year-end after earlier drone strikes, per Reuters, and a gas processing plant in Orenburg Oblast was also hit, signaling a Ukrainian strategy of degrading Russia's energy and military-industrial capacity far behind the front.

At the same time, a significant domestic crisis is emerging inside the Ukrainian military. Ukraine's State Bureau of Investigation opened a criminal inquiry into the Skelia assault regiment over at least 26 alleged noncombat recruit deaths, alongside reports of inadequate medical care and physical abuse between late 2025 and spring 2026 — a window into the conditions facing conscripted working-class Ukrainians fighting a war that Western governments frame in purely geopolitical terms. The WSWS covers the conflict as a NATO proxy war against Russia, while opposing both the imperialist war drive and the conduct of the Ukrainian state toward its own working class.

### 5. US appeals court clears nationwide fast-track deportations without hearings

A 2–1 panel of the DC Circuit Court of Appeals reversed a lower-court injunction, allowing the Department of Homeland Security to implement expedited removal nationwide — deporting undocumented migrants without court hearings, no longer only near the border. The two majority judges, Walker and Rao, are both Trump appointees; the dissenter was an Obama appointee. The ruling clears the way for one of the most sweeping expansions of summary deportation authority in US history.

Key facts:
- The lower court (Judge Cobb, a Biden appointee) had blocked the expansion in August, finding plaintiffs presented "substantial evidence" the process carried a high risk of error — including evidence of people who had lived in the US for over two years wrongly subjected to expedited removal.
- The ACLU condemned the ruling, saying fast-track deportations subject people to "an unfair system" that "undermines the fundamental principle that people receive due process."
- Expedited removal strips migrants of immigration court hearings and the right to legal representation, leaving life-altering decisions to frontline DHS officers.

The decision represents a major erosion of due process rights, advancing the administration's mass-deportation program by removing judicial review from the overwhelming majority of removal cases. The WSWS covers the attacks on immigrant rights and due process as part of a broader assault on democratic rights that ultimately threatens the entire working class.

### 6. Türkiye launches mass dragnet ahead of Ankara NATO summit

Ahead of the 36th NATO Summit scheduled for Ankara on July 7–8, the Erdoğan government has declared a de facto state of emergency in the capital. The Ankara Governorate banned all demonstrations, press statements, hunger strikes, and leafleting from June 28 to July 10, while 209 people were detained in nationwide raids targeting anti-war activists, journalists, academics, lawyers, and party representatives. The crackdown extended to elected CHP officials, with the Interior Ministry removing the mayors of Adalar and Silifke — Ali Ercan Akpolat and Mustafa Turgut — by decree, without any court ruling.

The removal of Akpolat carries particular historical weight. Büyükada (Prinkipo Island), in the Adalar municipality, is where Leon Trotsky lived in exile from 1929 to 1933, writing *My Life* and *The History of the Russian Revolution* and issuing the 1933 call founding the Fourth International. Since 2023 the WSWS has collaborated with Akpolat on annual international commemorations of Trotsky and on a project to transform his house into an international cultural center — a project the repression now directly threatens. The Socialist Equality Party (SGP) in Germany held a rally outside the Turkish Embassy in Berlin demanding Akpolat's release; the embassy refused to accept the SGP's open letter, a refusal that the WSWS Perspective calls confirmation of the "political nature of the operations."

Key facts:
- The dragnet follows İmamoğlu's March 2025 arrest — which came as the Istanbul mayor pulled ahead of Erdoğan in polls — and last month's removal of the CHP's national elected leadership under Özgür Özel.
- Erdoğan prepares to host Trump and other NATO heads of state while brokering the flow of Azerbaijani oil to Israel and participating in Trump's "Board of Peace" for Gaza.
- The WSWS Perspective by Ulaş Sevinç notes that "more than 90 percent of Türkiye's population oppose the war against Iran and the US bases in the country."

The crackdown is covered by AP, Reuters, The Guardian, and the *New York Times* — but without the class analysis, the Trotsky/Büyükada dimension, or the WSWS's application of the theory of permanent revolution (summarized below in WSWS coverage).

### Trump cancels housing bill signing, holds it hostage to voter ID demand

Congress passed the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act by overwhelming margins — Senate 85–5, House 358–32 — billed as the largest housing affordability legislation in over 30 years. Hours later, Trump abruptly posted on social media canceling the signing ceremony, conditioning it on Congress also passing the "Save America Act," a strict federal voter-ID bill. The maneuver creates a new standoff immediately after a rare moment of bipartisan consensus, leveraging a popular bill to extract a measure that would restrict ballot access.

Key facts:
- The bill bars corporate investors owning more than 350 single-family rentals from purchasing additional ones, eliminates the manufactured-home chassis requirement (cutting $5,000–$10,000 per unit), creates a small-dollar mortgage program, and offers local permitting incentives.
- It was led by Sens. Elizabeth Warren (D-MA) and Tim Scott (R-SC) and Reps. Maxine Waters (D-CA) and French Hill (R-AR).
- The 350-home cap exempts existing portfolios — leaving the largest corporate landlords' holdings intact and providing no rent relief to the tens of millions already paying elevated rents.

A socialist analysis would note that the bill, for all the bipartisan praise, legitimizes corporate landlordism while offering palliatives rather than addressing the housing crisis at its root.

### US measles cases hit 2,104 across 41 states as elimination status nears collapse

As of June 18, the CDC reports 2,104 confirmed measles cases in 41 states across 30 separate outbreaks, with three deaths — two unvaccinated children in Texas and one adult in New Mexico — the first US measles deaths since 2015. Approximately 92% of cases involved unvaccinated individuals. The Pan American Health Organization will formally review US measles elimination status in November 2026, and the US is now widely expected to lose the designation it has held since 2000, as MMR vaccination coverage has fallen from 95.2% (2019–20) to 92.5% (2024–25). The collapse is a direct consequence of the anti-vaccine policies promoted at the highest levels of the federal government and the gutting of public health infrastructure.

### First human H5N5 bird flu death confirmed; H5N1 dairy-cow mystery solved

NEJM published a correspondence report on a Washington State patient who contracted H5N5 (Eurasian lineage clade 6) and died in November 2025 — the first confirmed human H5N5 fatality globally. Separately, researchers this week identified why H5N1 attacks cows' udders: the virus's preferred sialic acid receptors concentrate in mammary tissue rather than the lungs. The mechanistic breakthrough could help predict future interspecies jumps and enable earlier detection, while the H5N5 fatality underscores that multiple H5 subtypes now pose human spillover risk.

### Science roundup

- **COVID-19:** As of June 22, infections are declining or stable in 47 states; weekly hospitalizations projected at ~870 (week ending June 20); wastewater SARS-CoV-2 activity rated "very low"; test positivity 0.75%. CDC scenario modeling flags the South and West — which had mild winters — for a possible summer uptick, making the next 4–6 weeks a key surveillance window.
- **Tuberculosis (NEJM, June 24):** A pragmatic South African trial led by Francesca Conradie found a six-month shortened regimen non-inferior for rifampicin-resistant TB — a disease accounting for roughly 400,000 new cases globally per year. A proven short course could dramatically improve adherence and cut costs in high-burden settings.

## Arts and culture

The cultural story of the moment is the improbable global ascent of the Quebec math-rock duo **Angine de Poitrine**, two anonymous musicians performing as "Khn" and "Klek de Poitrine" in costume. Their December 2025 KEXP YouTube performance accumulated 16 million views in four months, and their track "Fabienk" topped Spotify's Viral 50: Global in April. Writing on the WSWS, Louis Girard argues their popularity is not novelty but a symptom: their fusion of Turkish, Asian, and Western microtonal math-rock fills a genuine hunger for ambitious, technically demanding music amid what he calls "an immense artistic void" created by profit-driven cultural stagnation and identity-politics restrictions on cross-cultural synthesis. The phenomenon is revealing precisely because the work's cross-cultural ambition cuts against both market formula and the prevailing insistence that artists remain within ethnic or national "lanes." Right-wing Quebec nationalist commentators have attacked the duo as "cultural trash imposed by the federal government" — confirming that genuine cosmopolitan synthesis provokes the nationalist reaction.

The episode illustrates a recurring theme in the WSWS's cultural framework: that mass audiences retain an appetite for serious artistic achievement that the culture industry systematically starves. The same algorithmic viral mechanism that floods listeners with optimized product also, given the chance, propelled an obscure microtonal duo to the top of a global streaming chart — and that audiences seized on the difficult and the unfamiliar is the meaningful datum. The hostile nationalist reception, rather than the streaming numbers, exposes the political stakes: cultural production that crosses national and ethnic boundaries is increasingly cast by the right as a threat to "authentic" national identity.

Roundup:
- **Gaming and industry:** Rockstar Games opened pre-orders for *Grand Theft Auto VI* (Business Wire, June 24), one of the most anticipated and expensive commercial entertainment products in history. The franchise's satirical treatment of American capitalism sits in tension with its status as a multibillion-dollar corporate property; the scale of pre-order demand underscores how a handful of conglomerates now dominate interactive culture.
- **Cultural politics:** The threatened Adalar Municipality in Türkiye — home to Trotsky's Büyükada exile residence, where he wrote *My Life* and *The History of the Russian Revolution* — is the site of a WSWS-backed effort to create an international cultural center, now imperiled by the Erdoğan crackdown (see Top stories). The targeting of a site of revolutionary cultural memory is itself a political act against historical consciousness.
- **Music and the market:** That a non-English, microtonal act could top a global chart without major-label backing points to a latent audience for serious music that the commercial apparatus neither anticipates nor cultivates — a recurring gap between mass cultural appetite and what the industry supplies.
