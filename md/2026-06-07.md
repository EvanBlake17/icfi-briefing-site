### 1. Iran-US ceasefire collapses into fresh strikes as talks deadlock

The April 8 ceasefire between the United States and Iran has effectively collapsed into a renewed round of strikes across the Persian Gulf, with peace negotiations described by Iranian officials as "at a deadlock." The escalation marks the most dangerous phase of the war since the spring.

Iran launched seven ballistic missiles at Kuwait and Bahrain early Saturday; six were intercepted, but at least one person was killed and five wounded. Over the preceding two days, the US shot down six Iranian one-way attack drones near the Strait of Hormuz, then struck Iranian coastal radar sites at Goruk and on Qeshm Island. Iran's Foreign Ministry called the US strikes "a clear violation of the 8 April ceasefire and an act of military aggression."

Key facts:
- Mohsen Rezaei, adviser to Iran's supreme leader, told CNN that negotiations are "at a deadlock" and "the ball is in Trump's court," demanding the US unfreeze billions in Iranian assets as a "trust-building" measure.
- GCC Secretary General Jasem Mohamed Albudaiwi condemned the Iranian missile attacks as "treacherous terrorist acts" and a "dangerous escalation."
- The US Treasury is separately planning to use seized Iranian assets to fund reconstruction for Gulf allies — effectively financing the war's aftermath with confiscated Iranian wealth.
- The strikes near the Strait of Hormuz threaten the world's single most important oil chokepoint, with immediate consequences for global shipping and energy markets.

### 2. Israel escalates Lebanon assault and moves to annex Gaza

Israel has sharply escalated its war on Lebanon while declaring its intention to permanently occupy southern Lebanese territory and to expand its conquest of Gaza — a dual drive that human rights organizations describe as ethnic cleansing.

Lebanese health authorities have recorded at least 3,516 people killed and more than 10,000 wounded since the war began March 2; 32 people were killed in a single day this weekend. More than 1 million people — over 20% of Lebanon's population — have been forcibly displaced. Prime Minister Netanyahu declared southern Lebanon's occupation "permanent" and described the new security zones as "a fundamental change." He simultaneously announced that Israel controls 60% of Gaza and intends to expand further, while Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich demanded "full conquest" and Jewish settlement.

Key facts:
- Israel and the Lebanese government agreed to renew a ceasefire late last week, but Hezbollah immediately rejected any deal that does not begin with full Israeli troop withdrawal.
- Two Israeli soldiers were killed in separate incidents in southern Lebanon this weekend; 30 Israeli soldiers have been killed in Lebanon since the Iran war began three months ago.
- A US House War Powers resolution to withdraw American forces from support of Israeli operations failed 324-92, with Democratic leadership actively opposing withdrawal.
- As Andre Damon wrote on the WSWS, the assault constitutes an open drive to annex Gaza, carried out "with the full complicity" of both US parties.

### 3. Russia-Ukraine war intensifies as NATO chief visits Kyiv

### 5. Armenia votes in pivotal parliamentary election

Armenians voted today in a parliamentary election widely seen as a referendum on the country's geopolitical orientation between the European Union and Moscow. Polling stations opened at 08:00 local time for elections to the 9th National Assembly, with results expected this evening.

Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan's Civil Contract party led early polls with roughly 30% support, though a later survey projected it could capture nearly 65% of decided voters — a potential landslide. Key facts:

- This is the first national election since Azerbaijan's violent expulsion of all ethnic Armenians from Nagorno-Karabakh in 2023 — a humiliation that has reshaped Armenian politics.
- The outcome will determine whether Armenia continues its pivot toward the EU or reverses toward Moscow's orbit.

### 6. Peru holds knife-edge presidential runoff

Peruvians voted today in a mandatory presidential runoff that polls show is effectively tied, capping a chaotic electoral cycle in a country that has had nine presidents in a decade. The winner will be sworn in July 28.

The latest Ipsos poll (May 31) put Keiko Fujimori at 38% and Roberto Sánchez at 35%, with 15% undecided. Fujimori — in her fourth consecutive presidential run — promises a 60-day state of emergency to fight crime and "defeat terrorism." Sánchez advocates a new constitution, police reform, and reparations for victims of her father Alberto Fujimori's regime.

Key facts:
- In the April 12 first round, Fujimori took just 17% and Sánchez 12%, with over 11.7% of ballots cast blank — a stark measure of voter alienation.
- Voters ranked crime (63%) and corruption (62%) as their top concerns.
- The extraordinary instability — nine presidents in ten years and three impeachments — reflects the inability of any Peruvian bourgeois formation to address profound social inequality.

### 7. US House passes war powers rebuke of Trump's Iran war

The US House of Representatives passed a war powers resolution on June 3 directing Trump to end US military hostilities with Iran — the first time such a measure has cleared either chamber since the war began over three months ago. H.Con.Res.38 passed 215-208.

Four Republicans broke with their party to support the measure: Tom Barrett (MI), Warren Davidson (OH), Brian Fitzpatrick (PA), and Thomas Massie (KY). The vote is largely symbolic — the Republican-led Senate has blocked all previous war powers efforts, and Trump would veto any binding bill.

Key facts:
- Trump attacked the vote on social media: "Yesterday, in a meaningless vote, the House voted, 4 bad Republicans and all of the Dumocrats, to limit my War Powers, right in the middle of my final negotiations."
- The Trump administration has openly questioned the constitutionality of the War Powers Act itself, signaling a deepening collapse of any congressional check on the executive's war-making power.

### Germany fails to win UN Security Council seat

Germany received only 104 votes in the UN General Assembly secret ballot — 23 short of the two-thirds majority — while Portugal (134) and Austria (131) won the two available Western European seats for 2027-2028. It is Germany's first Security Council electoral defeat ever. Foreign Minister Wadephul conceded that "the fact that Germany must always assume a special responsibility for Israel in the Middle East conflict may also have cost votes," while Austria's constitutional neutrality appealed to non-aligned states in Africa, Asia, and Latin America. As Peter Schwarz noted on the WSWS, the defeat reflects international opposition to Germany's aggressive foreign-policy turn — backing of the US war on Iran, military leadership in Ukraine, and European rearmament. Notably, all domestic opposition parties — Greens, Left Party, and AfD — criticized the failure while sharing the government's commitment to expanding German military power.

### Senate passes $70 billion ICE and border bill

Senate Republicans overcame deep internal divisions to pass a roughly $70 billion immigration enforcement package — the Secure America Act — early Friday by a vote of **52-47**, funding ICE and Customs and Border Protection through the end of Trump's term in 2029. All Democrats present voted no, joined by Republican Lisa Murkowski of Alaska, the lone GOP defector on final passage.

In a further win for Trump, Republicans approved the bill without killing the **$1.8 billion Justice Department "anti-weaponization" fund** he supports — a vehicle to compensate people claiming to have been "victimized" by the federal government. An amendment to permanently ban the fund failed. The bill allocates $3.45 billion specifically for surveillance technology and biometric systems, supplementing roughly $170 billion previously appropriated, and now moves to the House for final passage.

### US measles cases hit 2,030

The CDC reported **2,030 confirmed measles cases** across 40 jurisdictions as of June 4 — with 93% (1,890) outbreak-associated and **30 active outbreaks** in 2026. The Spartanburg County, South Carolina outbreak alone accounts for roughly 990 cases. The US is on pace to exceed 2025's full-year total of 2,288, marking the worst sustained measles resurgence in decades — a public-health collapse directly tied to the gutting of vaccination infrastructure and anti-science policy.

### H5N1 review finds asymptomatic and human-to-human spread

A systematic review in *JAMA Network Open* identified **18 asymptomatic human H5N1 infections** and at least **one confirmed case of human-to-human transmission** (Vietnam, a household contact with no poultry exposure). As of May 27, the US has documented 71 total human H5N1 cases since 2024, with 2 deaths. The findings undercut the long-standing assumptions that H5N1 is always severe and never spreads person-to-person — assumptions that underpin current pandemic risk assessments.

### GLP-1 drugs linked to lower breast cancer risk

A retrospective cohort study of **111,646 women** ages 45-80 with BMI ≥25 found those on GLP-1 receptor agonists (Ozempic/Wegovy) were about **30% less likely to develop breast cancer** than non-users. Presented at the 2026 ASCO Annual Meeting, it is the largest study to date linking the drug class to cancer prevention — though its observational design means causation is not established.

### Trump signs frontier AI executive order

A June 2 executive order requires AI developers to give the federal government **30 days of advance access** to frontier models before public release to assess cybersecurity risks; agencies have 60 days to build a voluntary compliance framework. Separately, the DoD has labeled **Anthropic a "supply chain risk"** ahead of its Mythos model release. The voluntary structure has drawn both industry praise and skepticism about enforceability.

### COVID at record low; new antiviral approved

COVID activity is at its lowest since spring 2021 — hospitalizations at **0.2 per 100,000**, test positivity **0.79%**, with 41 states showing declining transmission. The FDA approved **ensitrelvir (Xocova)** as post-exposure prophylaxis, reducing symptomatic COVID after exposure by **67%** in trials — a meaningful addition for immunocompromised and high-risk populations. An AI-designed pan-coronavirus vaccine also passed its first-in-human phase I trial, generating immune responses across multiple coronaviruses and proving safe and well-tolerated.

## Arts and culture

The death of **Marjane Satrapi** at 56 is the week's most significant cultural loss. The Iranian-French graphic novelist and filmmaker died June 4 in Paris; her family cited grief following the recent death of her husband. Satrapi's autobiographical *Persepolis* series introduced a generation of Western readers to life under the Islamic Revolution through the eyes of a child — a work of enormous political and humanistic power that refused both Western Islamophobia and apologetics for the Iranian theocracy. Her co-directed animated film adaptation won the Cannes Jury Prize and earned an Oscar nomination. Her death comes amid the US-Iran war, lending fresh poignancy to a body of work that insisted on the humanity of ordinary Iranians against the abstractions of geopolitics.

The death of the great tenor saxophonist **Sonny Rollins** at 95, covered in the WSWS section below, similarly marks the passing of an artist whose work was inseparable from the democratic and anti-racist struggles of his era.

In the United States, the courts continue to push back against the Trump administration's assault on cultural institutions:

- A **federal judge ordered Trump's name removed from the Kennedy Center facade**, ruling that "Congress gave the Kennedy Center its name, and only Congress can change it" — a rebuke to the administration's drive to brand federal arts institutions as personal property.
- A separate ruling **permanently barred the administration from terminating over $100 million in NEH grants**, finding the cancellations violated the First and Fifth Amendments. Yet queer art curators report widespread and accelerating museum self-censorship, illustrating how political pressure reshapes culture even without formal censorship orders — a chilling effect more corrosive than any single decree.

Other developments:

- The **Venice Biennale** opened its 54th International Theatre Festival on June 7 under the artistic direction of actor **Willem Dafoe** — a rare instance of a major screen actor shaping a prestigious global performing-arts platform.
- The **Tribeca Film Festival** (June 3-14) marked its 25th anniversary with a record 103 world premieres, featuring more female filmmakers, first-time directors, and represented nations than any prior year, opening with an Earth, Wind & Fire documentary and closing with Alicia Keys' *Girl From Hell's Kitchen*.
- English actor **Anthony Head**, beloved as Giles in *Buffy the Vampire Slayer* and a recurring villain in *Ted Lasso*, died at home June 5 from complications of pneumonia.
