### 1. Iran announces "offensive" doctrine as US and Israel finalize post-midterm strike plans

### 3. UN declares the 1.5°C target lost as Burnham approves North Sea gas

The UN Environment Programme's annual Emissions Gap Report says the chance to hold warming to 1.5°C, the central goal of the Paris Agreement, is gone. Under current policies the world is heading for **2.8°C** by 2100, or **2.6°C** if every new national pledge is met. The report says the overshoot can still be limited, and warming brought back below 1.5°C by the end of the century, with emissions cuts relative to 2025 levels of 18 percent by 2030, 45 percent by 2035 and 69 percent by 2040. UN spokesman Stéphane Dujarric said: "Overshoot is not surrender."

On the same day, Prime Minister Andy Burnham's Labour government approved gas extraction from the Jackdaw field in the North Sea, calling it a "pragmatic" step toward energy security. Protesters outside the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero demanded that ministers reject the much larger Rosebank oil field, which is still pending.

Key facts:
- The US emits more than any country except China. The report attributes US emissions growth to electricity demand, partly from data centers, and to pro-fossil-fuel policies.
- The US withdrawal from Paris worsened the projections. The **State Department said the US does not endorse** the report's analysis or conclusions.
- Renewable capacity has grown three times faster than expected since Paris, and solar seven times faster
- UNEP's Anne Olhoff: "We've spent decades postponing cuts to emissions and now we're facing the consequences"
- Jackdaw will create about **27 direct jobs**, according to the Greener Jobs Alliance (cited by *Socialist Worker*). Labour's 2024 manifesto pledged no new North Sea licences.
- Robin Wells of Fossil Free London: "Time and time again, we see prime ministers choose petrol companies over people"

### 5. California state workers stage first strike in the union's 44-year history

Thousands of California state workers struck for one day on Wednesday, October 21, rallying at government buildings in Sacramento, San Francisco, Los Angeles, Oakland and San Diego. SEIU Local 1000 represents about 100,000 nurses, counselors, IT specialists and office workers. They demand a larger raise than the state has offered, higher health-care contributions and relief from Governor Newsom's order to work in the office four days a week. Their contract expired at the end of June. CalMatters reports that workers have accepted pay cuts in three of Newsom's eight years in office. Mediation will not resume until **October 30**.

"The budget is balanced on state employees' backs," said Local 1000 President Anica Walls. The California Department of Human Resources said it "remains focused on achieving a fair and responsible agreement." The WSWS reports that strikers said the union leadership limited the walkout to one day while members work without a contract.

Key facts:
- The strike vote was **96.2 percent** in favor. The union is seeking a **20 percent** raise over three years (WSWS).
- It is the second state-worker walkout under Newsom, after a three-day strike by the California Association of Professional Scientists in 2023
- Newsom vetoed **AB 1729**, which would have required written justification for in-office orders
- Local 1000 gave **$500,000** to Newsom's 2018 campaign and **$1 million** to fight the 2021 recall (CalMatters via WSWS)
- DMV offices and state parks curtailed operations

### Amnesty: terror continues in El Fasher one year after RSF capture

A year after the Rapid Support Forces took El Fasher in North Darfur, Amnesty International reports continuing unlawful killings, torture, arbitrary detention and forced labor. Fewer than **70,000** people remain in a city that held more than one million before the war. Of 19 residents Amnesty interviewed, **17** had been detained for months and got out only by paying ransom or escaping. Detainees were forced to collect bodies and clear rubble. Secretary General Agnès Callamard said "the United Arab Emirates has continued to support the RSF." Amnesty calls on governments to halt arms transfers to the UAE. Officials warn that IPC Phase 5 famine is "highly likely" in North Darfur.

### Gary Tyler, sentenced to death at 17, opens his first solo exhibition

The Grand Rapids Art Museum in Michigan has opened the first solo exhibition of quilts by Gary Tyler, now 68. In 1974, at 16, Tyler was arrested in St. Charles Parish, Louisiana, after a white 13-year-old was shot dead during violence by opponents of school integration. He was defended by a divorce lawyer. Four witnesses later recanted their testimony against him, and the judge told an all-white jury to assume he meant to kill. He became the youngest person on death row, was sent to Angola and placed in a form of solitary confinement, and spent more than four decades in prison. The Louisiana parole board recommended a pardon three times, and governors refused each time. He was released at 58. At Angola, Tyler and other prisoners started a hospice program and raised money for coffins by selling quilts. One quilt raised $30,000 at the Angola Prison Rodeo, and the Smithsonian has acquired another. *Captivity, 1974* (2023) is based on a photograph his brother took a month after the arrest, with Tyler still in the clothes he was arrested in.

The work is a document of Jim Crow justice and of mass incarceration in the state with the highest incarceration rate in the country. It is also artistically serious, transforming a craft learned to bury the dead into portraits that insist on the humanity of prisoners.

### Tom Skerritt dies at 93

Tom Skerritt died on Wednesday at 93 of natural causes, his family said. Born in Detroit, he served in the Air Force before debuting in *War Hunt* (1962). He co-founded the Red Badge Project, which helps veterans with PTSD through writing, and TheFilmSchool in Seattle.
