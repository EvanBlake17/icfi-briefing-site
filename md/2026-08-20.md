### 1. Trump declares "most crushing economic operation ever" against Iran as ships burn in Hormuz

President Trump announced on August 19 what he called "the MOST CRUSHING ECONOMIC OPERATION EVER TAKEN AGAINST ANY COUNTRY," describing it as "Economic Warfare and Isolation on an unprecedented scale." He warned that any nation permitting its "financial institutions, businesses, airports, or government entities" to assist Iran would face "TREMENDOUS Economic Consequences." The declaration follows the collapse of the August 17 US-Iran negotiating deadline and comes atop the continuing US naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, where American forces have fired on vessels attempting passage.

The maritime dimension is now the sharpest edge of the conflict. A sailor aboard the Liberian-flagged bulk carrier *Minoan Dynasty* was killed when an unidentified projectile struck the ship's engine room as it exited the strait — the eighth vessel attack reported by the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency this month, with three ships hit in a single day. Trump nonetheless posted a map labelling the waterway "new U.S. territory" and insisted the strait was "open and operating."

Key facts:

- Iran is already the most-sanctioned country on earth alongside North Korea, with more than 6,000 punitive US measures against its financial, energy, aviation and crypto sectors under "Operation Economic Fury."
- Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi dismissed the campaign as a "so-called 'Economic D-Day'" and "a diversion from domestic American issues," saying it would "only bring further defeat to Washington." He separately claimed the US is "begging to talk."
- Daily vessel crossings of Hormuz are in the single digits, down from more than 130 per day before the US-Israeli war on Iran began. Oil hit a three-week high on the attacks.
- Trump cited South Korea's refusal to join the war on Iran as one reason for scaling back joint US-ROK military exercises (see International developments).

### 2. Russian missile and drone barrage kills 12 in Kyiv, damages children's hospital

An hours-long Russian assault on Kyiv beginning shortly after midnight killed at least 12 people and injured roughly 40, striking 28 separate locations across the capital. Mayor Vitali Klitschko confirmed the death toll while emergency crews were still working at the scene. Key facts:

### 4. US sanctions ICC president and senior trial lawyer

The Trump administration sanctioned International Criminal Court President Tomoko Akane of Japan and Senior Trial Lawyer Abdoulaye Seye of Senegal, freezing their US assets effective September 17. Secretary of State Marco Rubio justified the measure with the declaration that "Americans will never be transported beyond seas to be tried for pretend offenses."

The sanctions extend prior US measures against the Office of the Prosecutor's leadership and eight ICC judges, imposed over the court's investigations into US and Israeli conduct in Afghanistan, Iraq and Gaza. The ICC Presidency of the Assembly of States Parties called it a "flagrant attack" on judicial independence, warning that "the international legal order itself" is being "placed at risk."

Key facts:

- Akane is the first sitting ICC president to be sanctioned by a state party's principal financier of the postwar legal order.
- Japan's foreign ministry called the move "very unfortunate." Dutch Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen: "International courts and tribunals must be able to freely carry out their mandates."
- Japan is a NATO-aligned US treaty ally and the ICC's largest single funder among Asian states; the sanctioning of its national marks an escalation against allied governments as well as the court.

### 5. US national debt crosses $40 trillion as 30-year yields hit highest since 2007

The US national debt has crossed the $40 trillion threshold — up from under $6 trillion at the turn of the millennium, doubled in the past decade, and with roughly $3 trillion added in the last year alone. The government must borrow at least another $2 trillion this year. Annual interest payments now exceed $1 trillion, surpassing the entire military budget and consuming close to one dollar in five of federal revenue, even as the administration drives military spending toward $1.5 trillion.

The 30-year Treasury yield touched roughly 5.32 percent this week, its highest level since 2007, before Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent intervened by doubling government buybacks of long-dated debt, pulling the yield back more than 10 basis points to 5.184 percent. In today's WSWS Perspective, Nick Beams argues the intervention is itself an admission that the American state can no longer rely on ordinary market mechanisms to finance itself, and that the Treasury market has entered a self-reinforcing "debt spiral" in which borrowing increasingly serves only to pay interest on debt already accumulated.

Key facts:

- Marc Goldwein of the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget: "The scariest thing... is how we're starting to see the debt spiral begin."
- Hedge funds doubled their Treasury exposure between 2023 and 2025 through leveraged "basis trades" and now hold 8.5 percent of the market — more than Japan, China and Saudi Arabia combined.
- The *Financial Times*' Robin Wigglesworth is cited describing the Treasury market as "arguably a source of peril"; Ambrose Evans-Pritchard characterised recent Treasury interventions as behaviour that "screams desperation."
- The S&P 500 fell earlier in the week under pressure from surging sovereign yields worldwide, despite having hit an all-time high earlier in August. 2026 has already seen eight weeks in which the gap between the best- and worst-performing S&P sectors hit double digits — a frequency last matched in 2000, 2001 and 2009.

### 6. ABC and Disney sue the FCC, alleging a state retaliation campaign

ABC and parent company Disney filed a First Amendment lawsuit against the Federal Communications Commission in US District Court in Washington, DC, alleging that the agency's investigations and license actions constitute a "retaliatory campaign" by the Trump administration to punish critical coverage. The suit seeks to halt an FCC proceeding in which Chairman Brendan Carr has demanded that ABC's eight owned-and-operated stations submit to license renewal years ahead of schedule.

The regulatory offensive dates to April, when Carr ordered a review of ABC's broadcast licenses — nominally over corporate diversity programs — one day after late-night host Jimmy Kimmel staged a mock White House Correspondents' Dinner mocking Trump. The FCC is separately investigating alleged political bias on the daytime program *The View*.

Key facts:

- ABC/Disney join NPR and PBS, both of which have sued the administration over comparable regulatory retaliation.
- The FCC's leverage rests on the broadcast licensing power — a mechanism with no equivalent for print or digital press, making network television the softest target for state pressure.
- The suit is the first time a major commercial network has directly sued the FCC on First Amendment grounds.

### Trump moves to open 45 million acres of national forest to roads and logging

The Department of Agriculture filed on August 18 to enter the final stages of rescinding the 2001 Roadless Area Conservation Rule, which bars road construction and restricts logging across 45 million acres of the National Forest System — roughly one-third of all national forest land, concentrated in ten Western states. Affected acreage includes 4.4 million acres in California, 2 million in Washington and 1.9 million in Oregon. The repeal is open for public comment.

### Kentucky confirms measles outbreak as US records worst year since 1991

Kentucky health officials confirmed an ongoing measles outbreak with 11 cases since the start of August — 10 in Christian County and one in Trigg County — with spread now documented into Todd, Washington and Owen counties. All cases are in unvaccinated individuals; at least two people have been hospitalised.

Nationally, the CDC reported 2,566 confirmed US measles cases as of August 13, the highest total since the disease was declared eliminated in the United States in 2000, and the worst year since 1991. Now in its second year, the outbreak places US elimination status in jeopardy as county-level vaccination coverage continues to decline — a direct consequence of the dismantling of public health infrastructure and the promotion of anti-vaccine positions at the highest levels of the federal health apparatus.

### COVID wastewater levels up 106 percent even as CDC reports "very low"

Stanford's WastewaterSCAN program recorded SARS-CoV-2 wastewater concentrations 106 percent higher in August than in July 2026, with "high" and rising activity nationwide, worst in the West and South. Roughly 4 percent of COVID tests are returning positive, compared with over 9 percent at this point last year — a milder summer wave than 2025, but a wave nonetheless.

The striking element is the surveillance discrepancy: the CDC's own wastewater read was "very low" as of August 8, in direct contradiction to independent academic monitoring. This gap between federal and independent surveillance is itself a public health story, reflecting the degradation of CDC data collection and reporting capacity.

### Common blood pressure drug class linked to 33 percent higher kidney risk in diabetics

A study of 31,031 adults with type 2 diabetes already receiving RAS inhibitors and SGLT2 inhibitors found that adding a dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker — the class including amlodipine and nifedipine — was associated with a 33 percent higher risk of major kidney complications over roughly 3.5 years of follow-up. The findings were presented at the 63rd ERA (European Renal Association) Congress.

Dihydropyridine CCBs are among the most widely prescribed antihypertensives in the world. The finding suggests they may accelerate kidney damage in diabetic patients even when layered on top of guideline-recommended kidney-protective therapy, and could prompt significant prescribing reconsideration.

### H5N1 reaches South Australian seabirds

Australia confirmed H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4b in a die-off of 49 dead and 35 sick terns off the coast near Adelaide, following the virus's first detection in Western Australian seabirds in June 2026. Australia was the last continent free of this lineage after years of remaining bird-flu-free; its arrival raises the prospect of spread into Australian poultry and native wildlife populations, with the country's isolated fauna particularly exposed. As of August 7, the CDC reported no unusual H5N1 activity in the US and assessed public health risk as low.

Damon's argument turns on the legal architecture rather than the individual atrocity: the death-penalty legislation moving through the Knesset converts what has been de facto extrajudicial killing into codified state procedure administered by military tribunals. He connects this to the conditions of the more than 9,600 Palestinians already in Israeli detention and the at least 84 who have died in custody, arguing that the prison system is functioning as an extension of the campaign in Gaza. His conclusion is that the required response is not appeals to the "Board of Peace" or to Washington — which underwrites the entire operation — but the organised international mobilisation of the working class.

### Ukraine war-hawk defeated in Florida Democratic primary — Patrick Martin

Martin's central point beyond the result itself is Nixon's post-victory conduct: her immediate distancing from DSA positions demonstrates that even a candidate elected on hostility to the war is absorbed into the party apparatus rather than altering it. The Democratic Party's commitment to the US-NATO war is institutional and unaffected by primary outcomes, which is precisely why the antiwar sentiment revealed in the vote requires an independent political vehicle.

### Directors Guild and IATSE more than happy to hand Hollywood over to the oligarchs — Marc Wells

Wells's programmatic conclusion is that the guild bureaucracies' intervention on behalf of the merger is not a tactical error but an expression of their social position: institutions whose officials are salaried at levels detached from the membership and whose function is the administration of concessions. He calls for rank-and-file committees independent of the guild apparatus as the only means of resisting consolidation and the layoffs the "synergies" will produce.

### New Zealand Greens candidate fought in SAS during Afghanistan war — Tom Peters

Josh Jacobsen, ranked 25th on the New Zealand Green Party list for the November 2026 election, served with the NZSAS in Afghanistan in 2010–11 — the same unit implicated in the Operation Burnham raid of 2010, which killed six civilians including a three-year-old child and was later found to have breached the Geneva Conventions. Jacobsen has made no apology for his service. Peters argues that his unchallenged candidacy exposes the Greens as a pro-imperialist party notwithstanding their "anti-war" posturing, and that pseudo-left groups which orient toward the Greens are thereby implicated. The article calls for the construction of an independent working-class antiwar movement on the program of the Socialist Equality Group.

### Meeting attendees denounce Australian government's assault on the disabled — Our reporters

More than 130 people — disabled individuals, care and health workers, teachers — attended an online meeting organised by the Socialist Equality Party, the Committee for Public Education and the Health Workers Rank-and-File Committee to oppose cuts to the National Disability Insurance Scheme. One parent reported that her son's support had been "slashed by 60 percent." Speakers linked the NDIS cuts directly to rising Australian military expenditure and argued for rank-and-file organisation independent of the union apparatus. One attendee stated: "we need a revolution." The meeting is part of the SEP's ongoing campaign connecting social austerity to war spending — the same equation Beams draws at the level of US federal finance.

## Pseudo-left press review

- [Jacobin / DSA](#jacobin-dsa) — Presents unions as the bulwark against "authoritarianism" while remaining silent on the Democratic Party's role in building the deportation apparatus
- [Left Voice](#left-voice) — Elevates "self-organisation" in Bolivia as a model while orienting to union resolutions rather than an independent revolutionary party
- [Liberation News / PSL](#liberation-news-psl) — Claims Trump "failed" in the war against Iran, substituting a pressure-group conception of politics for a class analysis of imperialism
- [Socialist Alternative](#socialist-alternative) — Post-mortems on DSA electoral defeats point back toward "left independent candidates" rather than a break with electoralism
- [Socialist Worker / SWP (UK)](#socialist-worker-swp-uk) — Celebrates jury acquittals and strike suspensions without examining the union officials who suspended them
- [Communist Red / RCP (IMT)](#communist-red-rcp-imt) — Unconditional defence of the Cuban state and race-based framing of a public tragedy

### Jacobin / DSA

**"Minnesota Unions Are a Threat to Trump's Authoritarianism"** (August 19) treats the federal conspiracy indictments of anti-ICE protesters as evidence that the trade unions constitute the principal obstacle to authoritarian rule. The framing inverts the actual relationship: the union apparatus in the United States has for decades suppressed strikes, enforced concessions and endorsed the Democratic Party — the party that constructed the deportation machinery now being turned against these very organisers. Absent from the piece is any accounting of the Biden administration's role, or of the AFL-CIO's support for it.

**"What Multilateralism Looks Like After US Decline"** (August 19) presents Washington's diminishing capacity to enforce its will as an opening for a reconstituted multilateral order built around figures like Lula. This is a program for the political subordination of the working class to sections of the national bourgeoisie in the semi-colonial countries, offered in place of the international unification of workers against the war drive.

**"While Trump Cuts Food Aid, ICE Sits on $100 Billion"** (August 18) is useful in its data — the contrast between SNAP cuts and the ICE budget is stark — but its conclusion is a call for reallocation of appropriations, i.e., a lobbying demand on Congress.

**Assessment:** Jacobin's output is oriented toward reconstituting a left flank of the Democratic Party and rehabilitating the union bureaucracy as an anti-authoritarian force. The class interest expressed is that of a middle-class layer seeking positions within the existing party and union structures.

### Left Voice

**"Self Organization in Bolivia Shows How to Fight the Far Right Internationally"** (August 16) presents the assemblies and roadblocks of the Bolivian uprising as a model for international resistance to Trump and the far right. The article's category of "self-organisation" substitutes spontaneous forms for the decisive question — the construction of a revolutionary party and a conscious struggle for power — which is precisely what was absent in Bolivia in 2003, 2005 and 2019, each time permitting bourgeois restabilisation.

A companion piece on the cancellation of Temporary Protected Status, which places 300,000 additional immigrant workers at risk of deportation, points to NYC Teamsters Local 804's "Resolution to Stop Deportation of Haitian and Syrian Workers" as the model of action. Orienting toward resolutions passed by union locals — however militant in wording — keeps the struggle inside the framework of an apparatus tied to the Democratic Party.

**Assessment:** Morenoite in method — militant phraseology, tactical orientation to existing union structures, and the deferral of the party question to an indefinite future.

### Liberation News / PSL

**"PSL Statement: Why Trump failed in his criminal war against Iran – and why we need to keep up the pressure"** argues that the administration was forced to step back from military action against Iran, and that this demonstrates the efficacy of protest pressure. Published as US warships fire on shipping in Hormuz and Trump announces unprecedented economic warfare, the claim is untenable. The underlying method is a pressure-group conception of politics — the state as an arena to be influenced rather than a class instrument to be overthrown.

**"PSL Statement: Defend our Haitian neighbors! We've stopped ICE before and can do it again!"** frames the deportation drive as reversible through mobilisation without reference to its bipartisan construction.

**Assessment:** The PSL's line remains organised around anti-Trump protest coalitions and a defence of "multipolarity," which functions as apology for capitalist states opposed to Washington rather than as opposition to imperialism as a system.

### Socialist Alternative

**"Democratic Socialist Francesca Hong Concedes In Wisconsin—What Way Forward?"** (August 15) analyses the DSA candidate's loss in the Wisconsin Democratic gubernatorial primary by just over 3,000 votes. **"Support Left Independent Candidates—Build An Alternative To The Two Corporate Parties"** (August 2) promotes Kshama Sawant's independent candidacy.

The two articles together define the tendency's orientation: the problem is identified as the ballot line, not the class content of the program. The conclusion drawn from a defeat inside the Democratic primary is to run outside it — with the same reformist demands and the same conception of socialism as a redistributive program achieved through electoral office.

**Assessment:** Electoralism with an independent label, without a break from the national-reformist framework.

### Socialist Worker / SWP (UK)

**"Win for Filton 25 members as jury refuses to convict"** attributes the acquittals to the "mass movement for Palestine" having "shifted public opinion." **"Arriva bus strikes suspended while workers consider a new offer"** reports the suspension of strike action over workplace heat conditions without any examination of which officials suspended it or on what terms — the standard omission of a tendency that treats the union apparatus as neutral machinery.

**"The far right feed us lies on crime"** correctly identifies the manufacture of crime panics against immigrants, but the political conclusion is a united front with Labour-aligned campaigns.

**Assessment:** Strike reportage without criticism of the bureaucracy, and antiracism organised around alliances with the Labour left.

### Communist Red / RCP (IMT)

**"Defend the Cuban revolution! Defeat US imperialism! Fight for world socialism!"** (August 13) records a unanimous Revolutionary Communist International statement defending Cuba against US intervention. The formulation of unconditional defence of the Cuban state elides the character of the Castroite bureaucracy and its ongoing restoration of capitalist property relations.

**"Jason Arday: Killed by the racist establishment media"** (August 17) attributes the death of the Cambridge professor to a racially motivated media campaign, an interpretation organised around racial rather than class categories — a striking convergence with the identity framework the IMT ostensibly criticises.

**"AI, data centres, and the quiet hum of revolution"** (August 14) reads growing hostility to technology corporations over energy use and job destruction as an incipient revolutionary current, conflating diffuse anti-corporate sentiment with political consciousness.

**Assessment:** Maximalist rhetoric attached to an uncritical defence of Stalinist states and, in domestic coverage, an accommodation to racial politics.

**Note:** No direct attacks on the ICFI, the WSWS or Trotskyism appeared in the past 24 hours' output of these tendencies. Notably, none of them has commented on the 15-year sentence handed to Trotskyist Bogdan Syrotiuk in Ukraine for antiwar writing — a silence that is itself a political position.

The Forest Service's own numbers make the class content of this measure explicit — the permanent destruction of one-third of the national forest system in exchange for timber revenues of between $4.6 and $10.6 million annually.
