### 1. US and Iran lurch toward full-scale war as Hormuz ceasefire collapses

The United States concluded its eighth consecutive night of strikes on Iran early July 19 as the collapse of the summer ceasefire pushed Washington and Tehran toward open war. Two US service members were killed and one remains missing after an Iranian attack on the Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan, raising total US fatalities to 16 since the conflict resumed. Iran's Health Ministry reports 50 people killed and more than 500 injured — including two children — between June 27 and July 18.

The economic shockwave is already global. Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil passes, has collapsed from about 110 ships per day before the war to just 8–13 transits in the past 24 hours. Oil prices jumped more than 4 percent. Writing on the WSWS, Andre Damon documented that US strikes have widened from military to civilian targets — bridges, a railway station, an airport and the control tower of Iran's only deep-water port — marking, in his words, a deliberate escalation of "US imperialist war aims against the civilian population."

Key facts:
- Iran retaliated by striking Kuwaiti desalination plants and oil facilities; the IRGC claims it hit a US support center at Camp Arifjan and destroyed a radar installation.
- Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei called Trump's signature on the earlier ceasefire "utterly worthless and devoid of" credibility, warning of "unforgettable lessons."
- The IMF calls the disruption the "largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market."

The collapse of the ceasefire — signed under US pressure and abandoned within weeks — exposes the fraudulence of Washington's claim to be seeking de-escalation. Each round of strikes and counter-strikes draws the region closer to a general conflagration that could pull in Israel, the Gulf monarchies and the major powers. The war is unfolding in tandem with the escalation of authoritarian rule at home, a connection the ICFI has stressed: imperialist war abroad and the assault on democratic rights domestically are two expressions of the same crisis of the profit system. Only the independent intervention of the international working class — including US and Iranian workers — can halt the drive to a Middle East war with global dimensions.

The expansion of control to 70 percent of the territory points to a policy of permanent occupation and de facto annexation, carried out with the full backing of the Trump administration.

### 5. Sudan's El Obeid under RSF siege, half a million trapped

Roughly 500,000 people are trapped in El Obeid, the capital of North Kordofan, as the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) tighten a siege begun weeks ago, cutting nearly all supply routes except a single eastern corridor. The city has been without electricity for almost a month, and food prices have surged by as much as 300 percent. UN human rights officials warn of an impending "catastrophe," expecting El Obeid to become the site of major ground clashes reminiscent of the El Fasher bloodbath.

Over three weeks, the UN documented 15 drone strikes on El Obeid and surrounding areas, killing at least 45 civilians. The drone campaign points directly to the RSF's Gulf-state backers, principally the United Arab Emirates — the imperialist proxy dimension that receives far less Western scrutiny than the humanitarian toll alone.

Key facts:
- The European Parliament adopted a resolution urging the EU to designate the RSF a terrorist organization.
- The siege is the latest front in a war that has produced the world's largest displacement crisis.

The El Obeid siege follows the pattern of El Fasher, where an RSF assault produced mass atrocities. The war between the RSF and the Sudanese Armed Forces is not a purely internal affair but a proxy conflict fueled by rival regional and imperialist powers competing for control of Sudan's gold, agricultural land and Red Sea coastline — with the UAE's arming of the RSF the most direct expression of this outside intervention.

### Bangladesh measles death toll climbs past 766

Seven more children died from measles in the 24 hours before July 19, pushing the confirmed and suspected death toll to 766. Since March 2026, over 101,000 suspected cases and 6,258 deaths have been reported, with 79 percent of cases among children under five. National vaccination coverage fell from 83.9 percent in 2019 to 81.6 percent in 2023 — a decline health officials link directly to the crisis — while malnutrition and low breastfeeding rates compound the toll. The outbreak is a preventable-disease catastrophe rooted in collapsing public-health infrastructure and social inequality, and it has received little sustained international coverage relative to its scale.

### Public health and outbreaks

- **Cyclosporiasis (US):** On the WSWS, Evan Blake reported that federal health officials identified Taylor Farms as the source behind the worst US cyclosporiasis outbreak on record but left disclosure and recall decisions to the company itself — a case study in regulatory capture prioritizing corporate profit over public health. Thousands have been poisoned.
- **Wildfire smoke:** Shannon Jones (WSWS) documented that production continued at full capacity in US factories and worksites blanketed by hazardous wildfire smoke, with workers collapsing from heat and contaminated air. Fine particulate (PM2.5) exposure at these levels is associated with elevated cardiovascular and respiratory mortality; the trade-union bureaucracies offered no resistance to management's insistence on maintaining output.
- **H5N1 / COVID-19:** Surveillance of avian influenza in dairy and poultry and of COVID-19 wastewater trends continues to warrant monitoring heading into the northern-hemisphere respiratory season; no major new variant designation was reported in the past 24 hours.

## Arts and culture

The most significant cultural development of the past 24 hours was the resignation of PEN America's president over the organization's publication of an article blaming discrimination against Israeli and Jewish writers on anti-genocide cultural boycotts. As Sandy English documented on the WSWS, the resignation is part of a broader capitulation of literary institutions to pro-Israel pressure — the same dynamic that fractured PEN America's 2024 World Voices Festival when dozens of writers withdrew over the organization's stance on Gaza. The episode reveals how ostensibly independent cultural bodies are pressed into service policing dissent, recasting principled opposition to genocide as a form of discrimination. For a body founded to defend free expression, blaming boycott movements for a climate of intimidation inverts the actual relationship between state power and the writers being silenced.

A second front of cultural politics opened around the England–Argentina FIFA World Cup match. In a statement for the WSWS, Andrea Lobo condemned the exploitation of the fixture by political establishments and media in both countries to whip up nationalist chauvinism — reviving Malvinas/Falklands jingoism and imperial nostalgia in England — and called on workers internationally to reject this reactionary framing in favor of class unity. Sport, one of the most widely shared forms of popular culture, is here weaponized as an instrument of national mobilization, a reminder that the culture industry does not stand above the drive toward war but is enlisted within it.

Roundup:
- **Literary institutions:** The PEN America resignation follows a pattern of arts organizations, universities and publishers narrowing the space for anti-war expression under political and donor pressure — a form of soft censorship operating through institutional self-policing rather than open state bans.
- **Culture and war:** Both the PEN America affair and the World Cup nationalism controversy illustrate how the cultural sphere is being reorganized around the imperatives of militarism — pressure to conform on Gaza and the manufacture of national antagonism through mass spectacle.
- **A note on method:** The WSWS framework insists that genuine artistic achievement be recognized regardless of an artist's politics, while nationalist, militarist and identitarian tendencies in cultural production are named for what they are. The task is to defend the autonomy of art and free expression against both state repression and the identity-politics framing that recasts opposition to war as bigotry.

The through-line across the day's cultural news is the mounting pressure on artists, writers and cultural institutions to fall into line behind war and censorship — and the resistance, exemplified by the PEN America resignation, of those who refuse.

No Perspective — or any other content — dated July 19, 2026 had been published to wsws.org as of this briefing; the most recent Perspective is yesterday's "Trump's speech on the elections: A ruling class in crisis lurches toward dictatorship" by Jacob Crosse (summarized in the United States section above).

The following articles from the past 24 hours are summarized for their unique content; where the topic is covered above, only material not already reported is included.

### Trump's speech on the elections: A ruling class in crisis lurches toward dictatorship — Jacob Crosse

Yesterday's Perspective analyzes Trump's "manic and at times barely coherent" speech on the elections as the expression of a ruling class gripped by crisis. Crosse argues that the disorientation on display reflects the fragility of bourgeois democratic forms under conditions of deepening class conflict, and that the administration's response — escalating the Iran war, the ICE dragnet and attacks on democratic rights simultaneously — points toward an accelerating drive to presidential dictatorship. The article's central political conclusion is that the defense of democratic rights cannot be entrusted to the Democratic Party, which is itself complicit in the war and the deportation machine, but requires the independent political mobilization of the working class on a socialist program. It is summarized here rather than in the United States section because no bourgeois-press source in the raw material corresponds to it: the speech was widely reported, but the "ruling class in crisis"/dictatorship framing is specific to the WSWS.

### US broadens bombardment of Iran to civilian infrastructure — Andre Damon

Beyond the escalation reported in Top stories, Damon's analysis emphasizes the systematic targeting of civilian infrastructure — bridges, a railway station, an airport and the control tower of Iran's only deep-water port — as evidence that the war's aim is not merely military but the destruction of Iranian society and economy. He situates the bombardment within the trajectory of US imperialism's decades-long drive to dominate the Middle East, and draws the connection between the war abroad and the assault on democratic rights at home, arguing that the same administration bombing Iranian civilians is building the machinery of dictatorship domestically.

### "We're not animals" — Tom Hall

Hall's report supplies detail on the Salgado family and the Houston immigrant community's grief not captured in the wire coverage, and connects the killing to the broader pattern of ICE deaths and the record deportation pace. He argues the enforcement machine is bipartisan in origin and calls for the independent mobilization of the working class in defense of immigrants, rather than reliance on the Democratic Party or the courts.

### As wildfire smoke engulfs factories and workplaces, workers demand lives before profit — Shannon Jones

Jones's piece is the clearest labor-and-health story of the day. Its unique contribution is the documentation of specific worksites where production continued despite hazardous air, workers collapsing from heat and contaminated air, and the complicity of the trade-union apparatus in enforcing management's output demands. It ties climate-driven disaster directly to the profit system and the failure of the unions to defend workers' health.

### Berlin: multiple protests against Rheinmetall armaments factory — Stefan Steinberg

Steinberg reports on growing protests in Berlin against the Rheinmetall arms factory located in the city center, presenting the demonstrations as a rising confrontation with the German state over militarization and the arms industry's central role in German rearmament. The article links the protests to the broader ICFI campaign against the return of German militarism and the vast sums being diverted from social spending to armaments.

### The fight against the far right in Australia: Lessons from One Nation's history — Oscar Grenfell

Grenfell argues that Pauline Hanson's One Nation is a "toxic byproduct" of the reactionary policies implemented by the Labor Party and the corporatized trade-union bureaucracy, drawing historical lessons for combating the far right. The article's value is its insistence that the growth of far-right forces cannot be fought through alliances with Labor or the unions that created the conditions for it, but only through an independent socialist movement of the working class.

### Hyundai autoworkers in South Korea take strike action — Ben McGrath

McGrath reports on a strike by Hyundai autoworkers, charging that the Korean Metal Workers' Union is isolating the action from the rest of the working class while quietly negotiating a sellout deal behind workers' backs. The report connects the South Korean auto struggle to the international offensive against autoworkers and the WSWS call for rank-and-file committees independent of the union bureaucracy — a link to the same economic instability driving the won crisis.

### Trump administration shields Taylor Farms and Taco Bell — Evan Blake

Covered in the Science section. Blake's unique contribution is the documentation that federal officials delegated the recall and public-disclosure decision to the corporation responsible, and his framing of the episode as a systemic feature of a regulatory apparatus captured by corporate interests — part of the WSWS's sustained coverage of the dismantling of public-health protections.

### Oppose nationalist chauvinism following England-Argentina FIFA World Cup game! — Andrea Lobo

Covered in Arts and culture. Lobo's statement additionally warns against the pseudo-left's adaptation to nationalism, insisting that the only progressive response to the World Cup's chauvinist exploitation is the international unity of the working class against all capitalist governments.

### AEU-Labor government trying to ram through sell-out of Victorian educators: Vote no! — Committee for Public Education

This rank-and-file educators' statement urges Victorian (Australia) teachers to vote "no" in a rushed union ballot on a deal it calls a sellout, and to form independent rank-and-file committees against what it describes as a government–AEU conspiracy. It is a direct organizing intervention, not covered in the bourgeois press, and part of the WSWS-aligned rank-and-file network's work in education.

## Pseudo-left press review

- [Jacobin / DSA](#jacobin--dsa) — leads with the clearest national-reformist material of the day: Democratic Party factionalism and Danish coalition welfare gains
- [Left Voice](#left-voice) — maintains a "neither Washington nor Moscow" line on Gaza and the Malvinas while refusing support to any capitalist party
- [Liberation News / PSL](#liberation-news--psl) — rejects both parties on ICE but leans campist on the Iran war
- [Socialist Alternative](#socialist-alternative) — calls for a "new party," anti-both-parties but electoralist in form
- [Socialist Worker / SWP (UK)](#socialist-worker--swp-uk) — pressures the new Burnham Labour government from within rather than breaking from Labourism
- [Socialist Appeal / RCP "The Communist"](#socialist-appeal--rcp-the-communist) — most consistently revolutionary in tone, opposing all capitalist states

### Jacobin / DSA

- **"Waging War on the Left Within the Democratic Party Won't Work"** treats the Democratic Party as viable terrain for socialist politics, framing Democratic infighting as a strategic error rather than a symptom of the party's bourgeois character, and draws a sympathetic parallel to continued entryism in Britain's Labour Party.
- **"Denmark's Socialist Left Is Delivering Free Dental Care"** praises the Red-Green Alliance for winning welfare concessions by propping up a Social Democratic government from outside cabinet — a textbook national-reformist "parliamentary leverage" strategy presented as a model rather than a subordination to capitalist rule.
- **"Against Geopolitical Football Moralizing"** downplays criticism of Milei's alignment with Trump and Netanyahu and frames Argentine Malvinas nationalism as local sovereignty rather than examining the reactionary politics involved.

Assessment: Jacobin's orientation for the day is the most nakedly national-reformist in this sample — parliamentary maneuvering within and alongside bourgeois-workers' parties is presented as the path forward, and the drive toward war is treated as a matter for "moralizing" to be avoided.

### Left Voice

- **"Trump's Election Tirade Signals Shift Toward Greater Repression Ahead of Midterms"** names the Democrats as pursuing "an imperialist and reactionary agenda" and governing "for Wall Street," refusing to cast either capitalist party as a democratic alternative.
- **"Betrayed by Its Allies, Hamas Accepts Imperialist Control of Gaza"** opposes US and Israeli imperialism while criticizing Hamas's reliance on the regional bourgeois states (Qatar, Turkey, Iran) — a line of solidarity with the Palestinian masses without political support for Hamas or any regional regime.
- **"The Malvinas Debate and the World Cup: A Marxist Response to Bhaskar Sunkara"** argues a British defeat over the Malvinas would be progressive despite Argentina's claim originating under dictatorship, using an "oppressor vs. oppressed nations" framing directed against both imperialism and the dictatorship.

Assessment: Left Voice remains the most consistently Trotskyist-inflected of the US tendencies in this sample, though its Morenoite lineage and orbit around the DSA milieu remain the political questions the ICFI has repeatedly posed to it.

### Liberation News / PSL

- **"ICE terror can be stopped – and restarted – at any moment"** calls ICE "a bipartisan machine… expanded under Obama, Trump, and Biden," rejecting both parties and advancing a class-struggle abolitionist program rather than electoral reform.
- **"A year later — all levels of gov't are still failing Central Texas flood survivors"** indicts federal, state and local government alike, including Governor Abbott, and calls for worker-led disaster relief.
- **"2026 World Cup: The international working class vs. the USA, FIFA, and the billionaire class"** casts the US as imperialist aggressor and portrays Iran sympathetically as a victim of US policy — the PSL's characteristic campist alignment, which substitutes support for a bourgeois-nationalist regime for the independent mobilization of the Iranian working class.

Assessment: The PSL combines a genuinely anti-both-parties domestic line with a campist international orientation that dissolves the class question into a defense of "anti-imperialist" capitalist states.

### Socialist Alternative

- **"Capitalism's Face-Melting Heatwave Shows The Need For Socialist Planning"** calls for nationalizing energy companies and banks under democratic socialist planning.
- **"ICE Murders Again—Build The Fightback From Houston To Maine!"** calls for militant organized resistance rather than electoral or legal channels, treating both parties' enforcement apparatus as the target.
- **"The Democrats Won't Save Us—The Case For A New Party"** explicitly rejects the Democratic Party as unreformable and calls for a new working-class party — though the "new party" proposal remains an electoral-national vehicle rather than a call for the revolutionary rupture and international perspective the situation demands.

Assessment: Socialist Alternative's output stayed anti-both-parties and rhetorically revolutionary, but its horizon remains a national reformist party rather than the building of a revolutionary international.

### Socialist Worker / SWP (UK)

- **"Palestine demo demands Andy Burnham stops Labour support for Israel"** pressures the new Labour prime minister to embargo arms to Israel, framing the reform of a Labour government rather than a break from Labourism as the near-term goal.
- **"Strikes, abstention and arms: Debates over the Unite union leadership election"** argues for a tactical "critical vote" for the incumbent bureaucrat Sharon Graham as offering "better terrain" — endorsing a bureaucratic candidate rather than an independent rank-and-file line.
- **"Starmer broke one pledge after another"** measures the outgoing leadership against unmet reformist promises rather than arguing that Labourism itself is unreformable.

Assessment: The SWP's coverage of the Burnham/Labour transition and the Unite election leans consistently toward pressuring and critically backing figures within reformist structures — a Labour prime minister, a union bureaucrat — rather than breaking from them.

### Socialist Appeal / RCP "The Communist"

- **"Solidarity protests rally behind 'Azad' Kashmir"** argues Kashmiri freedom requires the revolutionary seizure of power by the working class rather than reliance on the Indian or Pakistani ruling elites.
- **"Burnham's 'Number 10 North' and the myth behind 'Manchesterism'"** dismisses the new Labour PM's devolution pledge as leaving capitalist power untouched, giving no support to Labour.
- **"Trump's mad dash back to war"** opposes the US-Iran escalation as a symptom of imperial decline, opposing all the capitalist states involved and calling on the US working class to end American imperialism.

Assessment: The RCP's material was the most consistently anti-both-parties and revolutionary in tone in this sample, though the ICFI's fundamental differences with the IMT over the historical assessment of Stalinism and the record of the Fourth International remain decisive.

No direct attacks on the ICFI or WSWS appeared in the reviewed material. The clearest convergence with bourgeois-press framing came from Jacobin, whose treatment of Democratic Party factionalism mirrors the establishment's presentation of intra-party maneuvering as ordinary politics.
