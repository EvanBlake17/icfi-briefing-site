### 1. US and Israel prepare to resume war on Iran as Houthis strike Saudi oil field

The Gulf war moved closer to a new round of large-scale fighting. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) navy said it struck four ships taking part in oil transfers in the Gulf. It said three more vessels caught fire after hitting mines near the Strait of Hormuz. Yemen's Houthis struck an oil field and an international airport in Saudi Arabia. Washington then ordered non-emergency US government personnel to leave the kingdom, and Iranian media reported unexplained explosions in southern Iran.

On Truth Social, Trump denied that the US is negotiating with Tehran and said the situation would resolve "fairly soon." Iranian officials sent mixed signals. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said "Iran is prepared to give diplomacy another chance," but in a *Financial Times* op-ed he wrote that he had "no trust whatsoever in the sincerity of the US administration's professed desire for peace with Iran." President Masoud Pezeshkian said talks cannot go ahead while the US blockade of Iranian ports remains. Andre Damon wrote on the WSWS that Defense Secretary Hegseth's secret meeting with Netanyahu at Ovda air base, eight days before Israel's October 27 election, shows the two governments coordinating "to resume attacks on Iran" on a timetable set by the US and Israeli elections.

Key facts:
- **Brent** traded at $111.10 and **WTI** at $98.98 early Thursday, each up about 2 percent (Oilprice.com)
- Axios reported on October 7 that the Pentagon had ordered US forces in the region to finish preparing for major combat operations against Iran (cited by WSWS)
- Trump's endorsement of Netanyahu warned that unless Iran changes course, the US "will soon finish the job"
- Kuwait's oil output is back above 2 million barrels a day, but its fuel production remains far below prewar levels (ING via Oilprice.com)
- A war timeline: US–Israeli bombing began February 28; the US blockaded Iranian ports April 13; a June 17 "ceasefire" was broken July 7–8 when US forces hit about 170 Iranian sites

### 2. Witkoff meets Putin as Moscow and Kyiv trade mass drone strikes

US envoy Steve Witkoff met Vladimir Putin in Saint Petersburg on Wednesday for about three hours, his ninth meeting with the Russian president. Jared Kushner joined by video. The day before, Witkoff had met Zelensky in Oslo. Putin announced the release of Stephen Hubbard, a retired US teacher in his seventies who had been sentenced to nearly seven years for allegedly fighting for Ukraine. Witkoff and Kremlin envoy Kirill Dmitriev met Hubbard at Pulkovo airport. Trump called it "the closest we've been" to ending the war. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said Russia is open "in principle" to resuming three-way talks in mid-November, after the US midterms, focused on postwar security. He said there "wasn't really much substance" in the European and Ukrainian proposals Witkoff carried. He also confirmed that a Putin–Trump–Xi summit in Shenzhen is being prepared for next month and called it "the first such meeting in history."

Within hours of the meeting, the two sides launched some of the war's largest strikes on each other's capitals. Fighting also continues over Ukraine's access to SpaceX satellites. Secretary of State Rubio said Starlink access is "governed by a private commercial contract" and is up to Musk. The *Wall Street Journal* reported that Musk ordered a slowdown of Starshield. Reuters, citing two US officials and a Western official, reported that the decision came from the White House, which Zelenskyy confirmed. Musk said speed limits are set "by the US military and ultimately the president." Trump said, "I was not involved."

Key facts:
- Moscow Mayor Sobyanin said **630 drones** were launched at the Moscow region, 163 of them at the capital, and two people were wounded. In Kyiv, 15 people were wounded and metro infrastructure at the Pivdenny Bridge was damaged.
- Russian strikes killed people in Sumy, Kharkiv, Zaporizhzhia (three) and Donetsk (two). Russian-installed officials in Luhansk said a Ukrainian drone killed two.
- On October 14, Zelenskyy adviser Serhii Beskrestnov said SpaceX capped Starshield at 160 km/h, disrupting the drones Ukraine uses to intercept Russian ones. Ukraine says service resumed on October 15.
- Zelenskyy told US senators the Pentagon is holding back Patriot interceptors that European countries have already paid for, pending Hegseth's signature.
- Trump waived sanctions on Russia's war industry and made a diesel deal with Putin. He has reportedly urged Zelenskyy to resign.
- Ukrainian Foreign Minister Sybiha: "appeasement, servility and flattery" will not stop Russia's war

### 3. Paris bans protests as France's union leaders meet Lecornu

French police banned demonstrations from 8 am to 8 pm Thursday in central Paris, covering the Élysée Palace, both houses of parliament, the Louvre and the Champs-Élysées. They also banned a student march, citing the organizer's "inability to prevent violence and vandalism." Nightly curfews remain in force "as long as necessary" in Paris, Bordeaux and Nice, with a partial curfew in Lyon. Authorities have also banned protests in cities from Toulon to Le Havre. The movement began with mass demonstrations on October 17 against the cost of living and fuel prices. France is preparing for another weekend of protests by high school students and the Yellow Vests. On Thursday Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu met party leaders, the interior and justice ministers, intelligence chiefs and the mayors of Paris, Marseille and Lyon. France Unbowed (LFI) wrote to Lecornu demanding "an apology," after *Mediapart* reported that the government pressed the intelligence services to blame LFI for the unrest. The government denies the report.

Lecornu met the union confederations on October 20. Alex Lantier wrote on the WSWS that the invitation framed the agenda around the "freedom to protest" that "some seek to corrupt" rather than wages. The CFTC called the meeting "disappointing," and the confederations still have only a single one-day strike planned, for November 5. Lantier noted that more than 10,000 people have been arrested and that the army has been deployed for the October 24 Yellow Vest protest.

Key facts:
- **Italy:** Meloni's cabinet approved a new crime of "violent participation in a demonstration," harsher penalties for rioters, and financial liability for parents whose children vandalize or loot. It is deploying 500 soldiers after riots in Milan.
- **Greece:** About 10,000 people marched in Athens on October 21, chanting "From Greece to France, we want public, free education." Average monthly pay of €1,500 is back at its 2009 level.
- Paris trials over the October 17 violence have begun
- Thailand, Malaysia and China have issued travel advisories for France
- An NGO found that pro-Russian accounts amplified French anger before the protests, a finding that lends itself to blaming the unrest on foreign interference

### 4. Ebola reaches Nigeria and Kenya as Congo's toll passes 4,600 confirmed deaths

Nigeria confirmed its first Ebola case in 12 years. The patient is a woman who flew to Abuja from Uganda on October 3 and tested positive on October 19, and she is reported to be in multi-organ failure. Officials have traced 44 contacts, including health workers, other patients and fellow travelers. Genetic testing matched the virus to the Bundibugyo strain driving the Democratic Republic of Congo epidemic. Nigeria is the fifth country to detect this outbreak's virus. Kenya has confirmed nine cases since October 6, including at least three health workers. Africa CDC head Jean Kaseya said the outbreak is "no longer one country's fight." There is no licensed vaccine or specific treatment for Bundibugyo. Benjamin Mateus wrote on the WSWS that the spread reflects deliberate underinvestment and a ruling-class policy that treats "the lives of workers and the poor... as expendable."

Key facts:
- Congo's October 18 bulletin, day 156 of the outbreak: **9,552 confirmed cases and 4,602 confirmed deaths**, a 48.2 percent case fatality rate, across 66 health zones in eight provinces. Nord-Ubangi, on the border with the Central African Republic, is the latest province affected.
- The bulletin reported 97 new cases in a day, up from 40, and flagged a resurgence of transmission
- Nigeria's case was confirmed 12 years to the day after WHO declared the country Ebola-free in 2014
- Uganda says the patient only transited there after a business trip to eastern Congo

### 5. US embassy accuses Canada of midterm interference after Alberta rejects secession

The US embassy in Ottawa released a fact sheet accusing Canadian politicians of trying to influence the November 3 midterms through their opposition to Trump's tariffs. It cites Industry Minister Mélanie Joly's meetings with Democratic officials and Ontario Premier Doug Ford's anti-tariff advertising campaign. ### 6. New Mexico judge fines Meta $25 billion over Facebook privacy violations

New Mexico state judge Francis Mathew ordered Meta to pay $25 billion, the largest penalty ever imposed on the company. A jury had found more than 43 million violations of the state's consumer protection laws. "Facebook promised users that they (the users) controlled their data. This was false," the judge wrote. Attorney General Raúl Torrez said the money will go to the state's education system. Meta's chief legal officer, CJ Mahoney, said the case seeks to "impose a tax on speech," and the company will appeal.

Key facts:
- The award is **five times** the $5 billion fine the FTC imposed in 2019
- It is a state consumer-protection verdict, not a federal one, and it shows how little federal enforcement has done against the platform monopolies

**Europe**

### Ireland's Dáil votes to scrap UN approval for troop deployments

The Dáil voted 79–71 to remove the requirement for UN approval before Irish troops are deployed overseas. This scraps the third element of the "triple lock"; approval by government and parliament is still required. The bill also raises from 12 to 50 the number of personnel who can be sent without parliamentary approval. Taoiseach Micheál Martin said "the UN Security Council is no longer functional; it's paralysed." Sinn Féin leader Mary Lou McDonald said the change "opens the door to Ireland being dragged into EU military structures," and called for a referendum. The vote fits the drive across Europe to rearm and integrate formally neutral states into the war against Russia.

### AI-discovered PHP2Shell flaws force governments to take websites offline

Two vulnerabilities in the PHP programming language, together called PHP2Shell, let an attacker run commands remotely on servers running older PHP code without a password. PHP powers more than 70 percent of websites. The flaws were found with AI models: security firm Calif reported the first on August 11 together with OpenAI, and Alphabet-owned Wiz reported the second on August 24. Wiz said the flaws "rank among the most severe vulnerabilities discovered in PHP, and arguably in software history." It added that proving they could be exploited required a chain of steps "at the edge of what human researchers could practically achieve." The underlying bugs had existed for decades.

- The **NSA** issued a rare public warning and advised taking sites offline if they cannot be patched immediately
- Germany, France, Belgium, the Netherlands, Finland and the Czech Republic took government sites offline to patch them, and Japanese PM Sanae Takaichi posted a public warning
- Australia faces a spike in data breaches (ABC)
- The volunteer-run PHP Foundation warned that AI will make such flaws easier to find and called for a coordinated industry response. Critical infrastructure depends on unpaid maintainers while AI capabilities are concentrated in a few corporations.

Henry Antonio Marmol Martinez, 52, a maintenance mechanic from the Dominican Republic and a father of six, was crushed to death on October 9 while repairing machinery on the night shift at McWane Ductile in Phillipsburg.
