### 1. Houthi missile strike on Riyadh airport kills 12 and Trump says the US may join the Saudi war in Yemen

On Saturday, October 10, a missile hit the complex housing Terminals 3 and 4 (domestic flights) at Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport. Saudi officials say at least 12 people were killed and 309 wounded, several of them critically. It was the third attack on the airport in a week. An earlier strike on Thursday killed three Saudis, including Saudia captain Hamoud Ali Alkalthami. Saudi Arabia's coalition command blames the Houthis. The Houthis have not claimed Saturday's strike, though they did claim Thursday's, and they accused Riyadh of bombing Sanaa International Airport the same day. A "projectile" also fell near Dammam's King Fahd International Airport on Saturday evening.

The attack comes a week into a Saudi-led offensive meant to retake all Houthi-held territory, including Sanaa. The coalition says it hit 136 military targets on October 9 alone. Asked whether Washington would join, Trump said: "We may. We're gonna look at it… we move very quickly." He plans to call Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman. The Saudi-led coalition promised a "decisive" response. Even so, the Saudi energy ministry was still promoting a major international energy conference due to open in Riyadh on Sunday.

Key facts:
- **Dead by nationality** (General Authority of Civil Aviation): four Saudis, two Bangladeshis, and one each from the US, Egypt, Jordan, Syria, Palestine and Sudan. The victims are mostly the migrant workforce on which the Gulf economy depends.
- **Aviation impact:** Operations were suspended until midnight, and FlightAware counted roughly 290–325 cancellations. Lufthansa, Emirates, Etihad, Air India, FlyDubai and PIA suspended Saudi flights. EASA warned of a heightened risk of civil aircraft being misidentified, and the Houthis warned airlines "for the last time" to stay out of Saudi airspace.
- **Strategic stakes:** Houthi gains along the Red Sea coast give them surveillance of the Bab al-Mandab, the main alternative to Hormuz for Gulf exports. A Saudi diplomat says government forces retook the strait and Mayyun Island; the Houthis deny it.
- **Reactions:** Rubio "strongly condemns" the attacks. UN envoy Hans Grundberg called them a "grave escalation." Pakistan, which recently activated its mutual defence pact with Riyadh, called the strike "a heinous act of terrorism." Oman alone urged restraint.

### 2. US jet disables blockade runner in Gulf of Oman as Iran claims control of Hormuz

On Saturday a US fighter jet hit the stern of the Panama-flagged cargo ship M/V *Ocean Molica* (also called *Arika Sun*) with a precision munition after the ship left an Iranian port. CENTCOM says the crew "ignored repeated warnings and attempted to run the ongoing naval blockade," and that the strike disabled propulsion "without harming the crew." The war is now in its eighth month (it began in late February). The two sides are in a counter-blockade standoff: Iran demands that ships get permission to use its preferred routes through Hormuz, and the US has blockaded Iranian ports since July 14. The IRGC says it struck the LPG carrier *NV Sunshine* for using an "illegal route" and that its forces "have taken control of the administration of the strait." UKMTO separately reported an outbound tanker hit by an unknown projectile in Hormuz, which set it on fire, and on October 8 a ship off Qatar's north coast was hit by several projectiles, causing casualties.

Diplomacy is going through Moscow and Beijing. The Kremlin says Putin passed Iran's peace terms to Trump in a call on Friday. President Pezeshkian says Iran has revised its proposals but complained: "Every time we hold talks with the U.S., they attack again." Trump told a rally in upstate New York that Iran must "give us everything, or they don't exist any longer." On Truth Social he said the US "will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the Midterm Elections." His war policy is now openly tied to the electoral calendar.

Key facts:
- **CENTCOM blockade tally since July 14:** 4 commercial vessels disabled, about 135 turned around, and 10 IRGC-linked tankers destroyed.
- **Cost of the war:** A new congressional report counts at least 81 US military aircraft and drones damaged or destroyed. The USS *Abraham Lincoln* returned to San Diego after an unprecedented 265 days at sea, with at least eight suicide attempts aboard amid food shortages and contaminated water. The Navy moved its regional supply hub to Diego Garcia after Iran destroyed its Bahrain logistics base in February.
- **Sen. Chris Murphy** asked why "taxpayers [are] paying billions to hit commercial ships" and called the situation "a disaster." The Pentagon earlier refused him access to Al Udeid Air Base.
- **Stranded tankers:** Sri Lanka says it has no plans to help 19 Iranian tankers stranded off its coast.

### 4. India detains thousands in New Delhi lockdown over purge of 130 million voters

On Saturday the Modi government put the capital under effective lockdown to stop a rally at Jantar Mantar by the Gen-Z "Cockroach" movement. The movement is demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and a rollback of the "Special Intensive Revision" of voter rolls, which removed about 130 million names. Critics say the revision makes citizens prove their right to vote and falls hardest on Muslims, the poor and minorities. The Indian Express reported that Kumar overruled two fellow election commissioners who questioned the integrity of the voter database. Opposition parties say the revision shaped several recent state elections that the BJP won.

Police took movement founder Abhijeet Dipke and two other leaders off an Air India flight from Mumbai. A magistrate released them Sunday on condition of "no breach of peace for one year." Detainees also included YouTuber Dhruv Rathee, CPI(ML) general secretary Dipankar Bhattacharya, AISA president Neha Bora, Supreme Court lawyer Prashant Bhushan and, according to TV channels, former chief ministers Mamata Banerjee, Arvind Kejriwal and Pinarayi Vijayan.

- **Detentions:** About 2,500 in Delhi (The Hindu, citing police), or about 2,000 (Reuters, citing the Home Ministry), plus about 600 arrests in Mumbai (ANI). Five journalists, one of them from Reuters, were briefly held.
- **Lockdown measures:** Mobile internet was cut in the central business district, dozens of metro stations and markets were closed, and anti-riot vehicles with tear-gas launchers guarded key routes.

### 7. Justice Department opens antitrust probe of TV networks that halted Trump coverage

On Saturday DOJ spokesperson Emily Convington confirmed an investigation into whether ABC, CBS, CNN, NBC and Fox News broke federal antitrust law by suspending White House pool coverage in September. "Group boycotts among commercial competitors can violate the Sherman Act," she said. The five-network pool stopped coverage in solidarity after the White House declared on September 19 that covering the president is a "privilege – not a right" and barred CNN, MS NOW and Politico. On September 24, US District Judge Timothy Kelly ruled the ban likely unconstitutional and ordered access restored.

Key facts:
- **Disputes continue:** Fights over access to specific events and pool assignments have gone on since the credentials were restored.
- **CPJ's Jose Zamora** warned against using antitrust law to "intimidate outlets defending press access."
- **Media consolidation:** The probe comes days after the Trump-allied Ellison family's Skydance took control of both CBS News and CNN (see Arts and culture).

Using antitrust law to punish a collective press protest is a new tool in the administration's campaign to bring the media under direct state control.

### US measles cases pass 4,080 as Pennsylvania records fifth death

As of October 8, the CDC counts 4,080 confirmed measles cases in 47 jurisdictions, plus 18 among international visitors. There have been 42 new outbreaks, and 96% of cases (3,897) are outbreak-associated. The 2025 full-year total was 2,289 cases, so 2026 is already about 78% higher with nearly three months left. Kindergarten MMR coverage has fallen to 92.4%, from 95.2% in 2019–20, well below the roughly 95% needed for herd protection.

Pennsylvania's outbreak, now the largest in the country and the state's largest in more than three decades, reached 1,136 cases on October 9, up from 890 on September 27. There have been 219 hospitalizations (19.3%). According to the *Philadelphia Inquirer*, five people have died. The latest was a 20-year-old Amish woman on chemotherapy for leukemia, who could not be vaccinated. The CDC lists only two measles deaths nationally. On September 4 it began requiring death certificates to list measles as the *underlying* cause, which is rare because measles usually kills through complications. New York Governor Kathy Hochul declared a disaster emergency after 108 cases (as of October 3), the most since 2019. The order lets pharmacists, paramedics and midwives vaccinate.

- **Paul Offit (CHOP):** "Measles is now endemic in the United States."
- **Federal policy:** In August Trump reimposed by executive order the cut to the childhood vaccine schedule, from 17 diseases to 11, after a court had stayed it. On October 7 RFK Jr. announced a federal "vaccine injury" initiative, including an NIH clinic that may examine aluminum-containing vaccines (NYT, as cited by the WSWS).

### Jacobin / DSA

**["The War in Ukraine Is Spiraling Out of Control"](https://jacobin.com/2026/10/russia-ukraine-war-eu-nato-escalation)** (Branko Marcetic, Oct 10). **Assessment:** Jacobin's output this week is aimed at the state, the union apparatus and "progressive" governments. Nothing in it is directed at the independent mobilization of workers against the war.
